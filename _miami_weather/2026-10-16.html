.SHORT TERM...
(Today through Saturday)
Issued at 130 AM EDT Fri Oct 16 2026

A synoptic scale trough amplifying across the the Great Lakes, 
Central US and extending into the deep south will continue to 
advect eastwards today and into this weekend, which is expected to
drag a cold front southwards over the weekend as well. As this 
trough advects eastward, the mid-level high pressure ridge will 
shift offshore into the western Atlantic and low-level to mid-
level flow will shift southerly/southeasterly today. This will 
usher in deeper tropical moisture (PWATs of 2+ inches for most of 
the area) and provide ample moisture for the production of diurnal
showers and thunderstorms. The prevailing southerly to 
southeasterly low level flow pattern will support the most 
widespread showers and thunderstorms occurring over inland 
portions of South Florida today. Current QPF projections are for 
no more than 0.5-1.0" of rain today across inland and SW Florida 
today along with 90th percentile QPF of 1-2". Therefore, outside 
of any localized urban flooding due to poor drainage, excessive 
rainfall is not a high concern for today.

As the cold front rushes further southwards on Sunday, forcing for 
ascent will be increased across the entire region and deep moisture 
will be pooled over the area as well. Thus, widespread showers and 
thunderstorms are anticipated to form on Saturday. Depending on rain 
rates and storm training, flooding due to excessive rainfall is much 
more possible for Saturday. By this time, low level will shift 
southwesterly and steer the strongest convection towards the east 
coast metro areas. 90th percentile QPF for Saturday is much higher 
at 3-6", mainly across SE Florida. The Weather Prediction Center 
(WPC) currently has a Marginal Risk issued for excessive rainfall 
that could lead to flash flooding, which corresponds to at least a 
5% chance for rainfall to exceed flash flood guidance. This has 
potential to be upgraded if guidance hints at further impacts. A lot 
of the rain that will be observed on Saturday could become more 
stratiform and create a lesser flood risk with lighter rain rates if 
the atmosphere gets worked over quickly, but that would likely be a 
short notice change given that the trough and attendant cold front 
will provide plenty of continuous forcing and vorticity maxima that 
will take advantage of the moist and unstable environment. 

High temperatures today will be in the upper 80s to low 90s once 
again, and then are expected to fall to the low to mid 80s for most 
areas on Saturday due to the cold front approaching and a more rainy 
day expected.

.LONG TERM...
(Saturday night through Thursday)
Issued at 130 AM EDT Fri Oct 16 2026

Once the frontal boundary discussed in the short term clears through 
the area on Sunday and into early next week, rain chances will lower 
back below 40-50% for most of the region as drier air arrives. 
Strong surface high pressure will re-develop in the wake of the 
trough starting to advect offshore, creating a stout pressure 
gradient that could last several days into next week. Winds are 
first expected to increase on Sunday and last into the early to 
middle portion of next week, with several days of wind gusts in the 
20-30 mph range possible. 

Additionally, with winds expected to remain northeasterly during 
this time frame and stronger winds over the local waters, frictional 
convergence along the coast is likely to be the main driver in rain 
shower or storm development for next week. Uncertainty in remains in 
what potential rainfall could amount to in this type of setup, but 
it is worth noting that we have seen these setups over-perform 
before. Therefore, we will continue to monitor trends closely. High 
temperatures in the long term will fall to the low to mid 80s each 
day in the wake of the front late this weekend. Overnight lows will 
range from the mid to upper 60s near the lake region to the low to 
mid 70s elsewhere.
