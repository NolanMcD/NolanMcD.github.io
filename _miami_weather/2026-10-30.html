.SHORT TERM...
(Today through Saturday)
Issued at 321 AM EDT Fri Oct 30 2026

The synoptic pattern should remain generally unchanged today, with 
mid-level ridging in place over the Gulf and an area of high 
pressure overspreading the southeastern US. This setup will continue 
to promote light easterly-notheasterly winds across the area. Low-
level moisture should remain plentiful (with PWATs in the 1.5-1.8 
inch range), but the 00Z MFL sounding, along with ACARS data from
KMIA, KFLL and KDJT, and forecast soundings, all show a 
pronounced dry layer between 700-500mb that looks to remain in 
place today. This, combined with subsidence beneath the ridge, 
should help continue to limit convective activity across South 
Florida throughout the day. A few isolated showers will remain 
possible, mainly as a result of diurnal heating in the afternoon,
with PoPs in the 20-40% range. Chances for thunderstorms also 
remain very low, but cannot be ruled out entirely. Highs today 
will remain in the mid to upper 80s. 

The pattern begins to transition on Saturday as an upper-level 
disturbance develops over the Gulf and gradually shifts eastward. At 
the same time, the axis of thr surface high over the SE CONUS 
will shift southeastward, helping to strengthen the pressure 
gradient across the western Atlantic waters and the FL peninsula. 
This will lead to increasing northeasterly to easterly winds 
across the area through the day, especially along the immediate 
East Coast. Concurrently, moisture advection aloft will help erode
the dry air pocket and moisten the atmospheric column, while also
helping enhance instability across the area. This setup, along
with increasing convergence due to the enhanced pressure gradient, will
help drive up the chances for more widespread rainfall activity 
on Saturday. In fact, some of the high-res guidance shows the 
possibility of repeated rounds of rainfall moving in from the
Atlantic Ocean throughout the day. This could result in locally 
heavy rainfall and some localized flooding in spots. HREF rainfall
accumulations show some 24-hour-accumulation bullseyes in the 1-3
inch range along the East coast metro, with a 1 in 10 chance of
some locations seeing 2-4 inches. This, combined with lingering 
minor coastal flooding concerns, could exacerbate generalized 
flooding concerns across vulnerable areas. Highs on Saturday will 
remain in the mid 80s, with the warmest temperatures over interior
and southwest FL.

.LONG TERM...
(Saturday night through Thursday)
Issued at 321 AM EDT Fri Oct 30 2026

The aforementioned upper-level disturbance should move across the 
Florida peninsula on Sunday, bringing the wettest period of the 
forecast. Increasing large-scale ascent, cooler mid-level 
temperatures and a deep moisture profile should support 
widespread shower and thunderstorm development through much of the
day, with PoPs in the 80-90% range. Heavy rainfall and potential 
flooding will be the main concerns, with WPC already placing the 
East Coast metro areas under a Marginal Risk (Level 1 of 4) of 
Flash flooding on Sunday and Monday. In fact, guidance shows the 
highest convective coverage could be most likely along the East 
Coast. However, this will ultimately be very dependent on the 
timing, track and strength of the feature as it moves across the 
area. 

DESI 500mb heights ensemble cluster analysis still shows a primary 
cluster with a weaker system that takes a more northerly track, 
while the other three clusters favor some flavor of a deeper system 
taking a more southerly track. The more northerly track would take 
the higher rainfall totals north across northern-central FL, while
the more southerly tracks would keep the heavy rainfall 
concentrated across central and south FL. Among the global 
ensembles, the GEFS moves the system eastward faster, while the 
ENS shows the disturbance, and thus its impacts, lingering into 
Monday. As such, the ENS solution is also the wetter of the 
possible options. As a result of these slight guidance 
discrepancies, there is still somewhat low-to-medium confidence 
regarding the forecast for Sunday and Monday, but we erred on the 
side of the more southerly solutions and kept high PoPs gradually 
tapering off from west to east on Monday night. 

Confidence in the forecast declines further as we head into the 
middle of next week. Mid-level ridging will look to build briefly 
across the area, favoring a brief dry out across the region
Tuesday and Wednesday. However, later in the week the global 
models try to develop the next upper level trough and surface low
over the Rockies. This complex would gradually move eastward and 
potentially bring a cold front across the FL peninsula late in the
week. Considerable spread remains in the timing and strength of 
this solution, and we will continue to monitor the evolution of 
the forecast as we approach next week. 

Temperatures will cool down a bit Sunday-Monday with the increased 
storminess and cloud cover, with highs in the mid 80s. Lows will 
slide into the mid 60s across the interior, and the low 70s along
the coasts.
