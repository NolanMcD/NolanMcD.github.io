.SYNOPSIS...
Issued at 322 AM EDT Sat Oct 31 2026

Deep layer ridging covers the Southeast and much of the center of 
the country, with a shortwave over eastern TX and the Lower MS 
Valley. Ensembles generally bring the shortwave through the 
Southeast overnight tonight through Monday morning, and only 
gradually nudge the low-level ridge axis northeastward. Then, 
ridging briefly builds back over the Southeast before a stronger 
frontal system reaches and crosses through the Southeast mid to late 
week.

&&

.SHORT TERM...
(Today through Sunday Night)
Issued at 322 AM EDT Sat Oct 31 2026

The continued strong easterly flow at the base of the low-level 
ridge will keep the coastal trough in place and allow for scattered 
showers to move inland today and tonight. While the probability of 
rainfall across south Florida is high, the scattered nature of the 
showers makes it difficult to pinpoint where and when a location may 
be impacted by a passing shower. The one thing hi-res guidance does 
agree on is that the highest probability of heavier rainfall is in 
the late night/early morning hours (within training bands) with 
mostly light to moderate showers during the daytime. Additionally, 
with the exception of any training, showers should be rather brief 
since the steady easterly flow is expected to keep showers moving 
quickly.

The deep-layer forcing associated with the approaching shortwave is 
forecast to arrive late Sunday afternoon into the overnight hours. 
The primary expectation is more widespread rainfall with the assist 
from the large-scale ascent and high PWATs. With the low-level 
easterly flow expected to continue (and the associated trough 
remaining in place), the pattern of west-east oriented bands of 
heavier precip will likely continue but be enhanced by the support 
aloft. For these reasons the WPC has outlooked much of the Florida 
east coast with a Slight Risk for excessive rainfall on Sunday. 
Essentially this is a level 2/4 risk for isolated to scattered flash 
flooding potential. On average across South Florida, a reasonable 
worst case QPF would be on the order of 1.5-2", but wherever an W/E 
oriented band sets up, the reasonable worst case jumps closer to 3-
5". With those amounts likely to fall over the course of 1-3 hours, 
there would be a slight flash flooding risk. Since this risk runs 
through the overnight hours Sunday, into early Monday morning, WPC 
keeps the FL east coast under a Slight Risk in Monday's outlook as 
well.

&&

.LONG TERM...
(Monday through Friday) 
Issued at 322 AM EDT Sat Oct 31 2026

For the first part of next week we return to a pattern consisting of 
ridging aloft and moderate easterly flow around the base of a low-
level ridge. That means a return to scattered passing showers each 
day and night. The pattern will change a bit come mid-week when flow 
begins to veer ahead of a developing/approaching frontal system. 
There's still quite a bit of uncertainty around the pre-frontal 
environment and how mesoscale features will affect storm 
distribution each day, but guidance is in fair agreement of a 
frontal passage some time on Friday. How strong and how far south 
the surface low will be, and what that means for the potential for 
an isolated severe storm or two, remains in question with quite a 
bit of model spread. Certainly something to keep an eye on once we 
get past this weekends heavy rain threat.
