.SYNOPSIS...
Issued at 218 AM EST Thu Nov 19 2026

Over the past couple of days, a northern stream shortwave moved out 
of the Lower MS valley and across the northern Gulf while a southern 
stream shortwave moved from southern TX through the central Gulf. 
These features merged into a mid/upper trough across the eastern 
Gulf. The merging of these features resulted in what is now a cutoff 
mid-level low, with an upper-level trough axis that bends through 
the Mid-Atlantic and Southeast, and then down the spine of Florida. 
At the surface, we've remained at the base of the eastern CONUS 
ridge, with broad Gulf troughing in response to the features aloft. 
The strong easterly flow across the Atlantic SST front along the 
Gulf Stream favors banding convection that typically has nocturnal 
peaks. This surface forcing has been aided by the aforementioned 
features aloft to result in robust training convection that brought 
considerable flooding to locations across south Florida. 

Water vapor imagery this morning depicts a sliver of dry air moving 
from the Florida Straits into south Florida. While it wasn't quite 
in place at the time of the 00z sounding this evening, it was at 
KEY, with a large area of dry air present above 700mb. A 04z hand 
analysis depicts the persistent east coast trough in the thermal and 
pressure fields.

&&

.SHORT TERM...
(Today through Friday)
Issued at 218 AM EST Thu Nov 19 2026

While the generalized picture of the synoptic evolution is well 
agreed upon by the global ensembles, there are some differences in 
the smaller details which could have an impact on precip coverage 
today. Generally speaking, the mid-level low will absorb into the 
northern stream as a large shortwave moves through the center of the 
country. At the same time, the upper trough will continue its drift 
into the western Atlantic as another southern stream wave moves out 
of south Texas into the western Gulf. The subtleties are just how 
amplified the surface pattern becomes through the day which has to 
do with two separate features (1) western Gulf wave (2) the east 
coast cutoff. A slightly more amplified west Gulf wave may illicit a 
more amplified trough in the Gulf which could take away a bit of the 
direct onshore flow locally. A larger or further south east coast 
wave could support more robust surface response near the Bahamas 
which could also remove some of the direct onshore flow. The wetter 
solutions tend to preserve the more direct onshore flow and 
persistent east coast surface trough. CAM guidance hasn't been a 
huge help as they've tended to have quite a bit of misplaced 
convection compared to the current conditions. I can find a few HRRR 
runs that have a decent grasp on the current picture, and those tend 
to be a little drier today on the widespread average (though still 
have bands of rain passing through the area). Global guidance with a 
larger mid-level low seem to match a little closer to an objective 
SPC analysis of 500mb heights from 00z radiosondes. This plus the 
dry air on satellite imagery and noted in the soundings steer me in 
the direction of a gradual pattern shift today. 

Less upper-level support, a north/south PWAT gradient, but continued 
low-level easterly flow seem to favor a pattern of bands of precip 
affecting mainly Palm Beach and Broward counties with slightly lower 
rain rates than the past couple of days. Across these locations 
expect that showers could quickly produce 1 to 2 inches of rainfall, 
with the localized higher amounts being on the order of 3 to 5 
inches. The signal for isolated amounts of 8", 10", or above appear 
to move north of South Florida today. In many locations, however, 
the damage has already been done and any additional rainfall could 
aggravate ongoing flooding, and even 1 to 2 inches could result in 
quite impactful consequences. Thus, the flood watch will remain in 
place today and our bar for Flash Flood warnings lowered a bit in 
the hardest hit areas.

How things play out on Friday will depend on the evolution of the 
surface low in the western Atlantic. The general idea is that the 
upper wave today will assist in the early formation of the low, and 
by Friday the eastern Gulf shortwave will reach the western Atlantic 
and influence further development of the low. The GEFS and ECMWF ENS 
support this with varying position and strength solutions while the 
GEPS favors very little development and continued onshore flow 
(we'll count this as an outlier for now). Depending on how strong 
and how far west the low tracks, Palm Beach county may remain under 
the threat for heavy rainfall for another day. While it'll be most 
common for an inch or two of rain in passing storms, there is a low 
end chance that an isolated location could pick up 2-4" over a short 
period of time. For now, any extension of the flood watch will be 
passed on to our day shift for further consideration based on 
rainfall today and model trends over the next 12 hours. While it's 
unlikely that it'll be completely dry elsewhere across the south 
Florida, only light inconsequential showers are anticipated at this 
time.

.LONG TERM...
(Friday night through Wednesday)
Issued at 218 AM EST Thu Nov 19 2026

As we start the weekend, the aforementioned surface low is expected 
to move up the Eastern Seaboard, removing its influence from the 
local area. The southern stream is forecast to shift north across 
the southern third of the CONUS resulting, locally, in mostly zonal 
flow aloft at the base of a broad full CONUS trough. Global 
ensembles are in rather good agreement in this evolution through 
early next week. They begin to diverge a bit around mid-week when 
the pattern amplifies a bit and a shortwave moves across the 
country bringing our next best chance of rain. We should be able 
to squeeze out 2 or 3 relatively dry days before then though. 
Seasonable temperatures are expected late this weekend through 
early next week.
