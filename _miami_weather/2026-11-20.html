For the North Atlantic...Caribbean Sea and the Gulf of America:

Offshore of the Southeast U.S. Coast:
Several broad areas of low pressure are located along a front that 
extends from central Florida eastward into the Atlantic for several 
hundred miles, and are producing disorganized showers and 
thunderstorms.  One of the low pressure areas, near the central 
Florida coast, is likely to remain inland without significant 
development.  A second low farther east could acquire subtropical or 
tropical characteristics while it moves generally northward to 
north-northeastward, passing near or east of the mid-Atlantic coast 
through the weekend.

Regardless of subtropical or tropical development, this large 
weather system is expected to produce gusty winds, dangerous surf, 
rip currents and coastal flooding along portions of the southeast 
and mid-Atlantic U.S. coasts into early next week.  In addition, 
heavy rainfall and the threat of flash flooding will continue 
across portions of the northeast Florida peninsula today, spreading 
northward over coastal portions of the Carolinas and the 
mid-Atlantic over the weekend.  Interests along the eastern coast of 
the United States should continue to monitor the progress of this 
system.  Additional information on this system, including Gale 
Warnings, can be found in High Seas Forecasts issued by the National 
Weather Service, as well as products issued by your local National 
Weather Service forecast office.

Rain chances for South Florida continue to decrease as a surface low 
deepens off the coast of East Central Florida. This has facilitated 
the advection of a drier air mass across the region and finally led 
a break to some of the incessant rain and cloud cover impacting the 
region earlier in the week. This surface low will continue to 
propagate northward through the day today and its impacts should 
continue to decrease. As the system progresses northward, westerly 
flow develops across the area and continues through today and 
tomorrow. Outside of a few isolated showers, mainly dry conditions 
should prevail across the area today. Highest rain chances will be 
across the Palm Beach County area nearest the area of low pressure. 

High temperatures will reach the low to mid 80s with pleasant

.LONG TERM...
(Saturday night through Thursday)
Issued at 306 AM EST Fri Nov 20 2026

Continuing into the weekend, the aforementioned surface low is 
expected to continue moving up the Eastern Seaboard, removing its 
influence from the local area and maintaining mostly dry conditions 
through the end of the weekend into early next week. The southern 
stream is forecast to shift north across the southern third of the 
CONUS resulting, locally, in mostly zonal flow aloft at the base of 
a broad full CONUS trough. Global ensembles are in rather good 
agreement in this evolution through early next week. They begin to 
diverge a bit around mid-week when the pattern amplifies a bit and a 
shortwave moves across the country bringing our next best chance of 
rain. We should have a stretch of a few generally dry days before
then, though. Seasonable temperatures are expected late this 
weekend through early next week.
