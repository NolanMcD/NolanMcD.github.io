.SHORT TERM...

Issued at 313 AM EST Mon Nov 16 2026

OVERVIEW & IMPACTS...

A complex atmospheric pattern will set the stage for an 1) Increased 
threat of urban flooding along the east coast of South Florida, 2) 
Dangerous marine conditions, and 3) Life-threatening rip currents 
and surf at east coast beaches for much of the workweek. Forecast 
confidence remains high (60-80%) on the timing and evolution of 
marine and beach impacts as gusty northeasterly winds strengthen 
today and persist over the next several days. Boaters and beach-
goers alike should be mindful of the upcoming prolonged period of 
dangerous conditions and plan accordingly. The main change in the 
forecast over the past 24 hours has been a more favorable alignment 
of atmospheric features (discussed in great detail below if one 
wishes to partake in the nitty-gritty meteorology) that has 
supported a greater threat of urban flooding across our east coast 
metro during the middle portion of this week. The Weather Prediction 
Center has responded accordingly, increasing the risk of excessive 
rainfall to a Slight Risk (Level 2 of 4) for Miami, Broward, and 
Palm Beach metro areas on Tuesday, Wednesday, and Thursday. Forecast 
confidence on exact timing on when the heaviest rainfall could occur 
remains low (10-30%) with a slight uptick in forecast confidence for 
the overall threat of heavy rainfall to the medium (20-40%) range. 
Saturated soils from recent heavy rainfall events could provide a 
lower barrier to urban flooding. The main question will be just how 
much rainfall occurs and in what time frame as that will determine 
the severity of any urban flooding that occurs. 

DETAILED SYNOPSIS...

Today will very much so be a transition day between the post frontal 
airmass that has dominated the local weather regime across South 
Florida over the last several days and a series of synoptic features 
(aloft and at the surface) that is forecast to result in an active 
pattern during the middle of this week. Boundary layer moisture will 
begin to pool across the region as a stationary boundary forms over 
the Gulf and builds into the Florida Peninsula. Upstream features 
for our upcoming set-up are now not only just in the model guidance 
but also present on RTMA and GOES Imagery. 07z Mesoanalysis depicts 
a sharp mid-level shortwave digging southward across the northern 
Gulf coast as well as a deepening surface low across the western 
Gulf this morning, aided by upper level divergence. At the same 
time, Expansive surface ridging is forecast to remain in place 
across much of the eastern half of the contiguous United States 
today. The movement of the surface low and stationary front eastward 
across the Gulf will result in the enhancement of a pressure 
gradient across South Florida, resulting in an enhancement of 
surface winds during the afternoon and early evening hours. Most 
locales are forecast to remain dry today with the exception of low 
end rain chances across coastal Palm Beach County as low-topped 
shower activity could briskly move onshore in the enhancing 
northeasterly flow. 

Model guidance has trended in the direction of a much slower 
propagation of the robust mid-level shortwave across the region 
during the middle portion of this week. Guidance has also trended 
with a closer passage of a frontal boundary and various surface lows 
(both east and west of South Florida) during the mid-week period. 
These are important forecast detail as a slower moving mid to upper 
level feature would act to provide the necessary upper level 
divergence and mid level lapse rates to sustain shower and 
thunderstorm activity for a longer duration (both during the day and 
overnight). The proximity to a frontal boundary or surface low would 
act to provide additional low level ascent and convergence. Synoptic 
boundaries often enhance mesoscale phenomena (Coastal Convergence) 
setups with historical precedent. At the surface, the aforementioned 
frontal boundary which is forecast to remain in the vicinity of our 
area on Tuesday will result in a continuation of the pressure 
gradient and resultant gusty winds across the region. The highest 
wind speeds are projected over the local waters and along the 
immediate coastline of southeastern Florida before surface friction 
acts to reduce wind speeds at the surface further inland. This 
enhancement of onshore winds coupled with the pooling of moisture 
along the boundary across South Florida could set the stage for 
increasing spatial and temporal coverage of showers (and heavy 
rainfall) along the east coast of South Florida beginning on 
Tuesday. 

The implications of friction will also play a role in any threat of 
heavy rainfall during this period that will primarily focus across 
Miami-Dade, Broward, and Palm Beach metro areas. As air slows along 
the coast due to increased friction, ascent is maximized as air 
parcels converge and are forced to rise. Combining this mesoscale 
forcing with a potential gradient of instability along the immediate 
coastline, sufficient boundary layer moisture, and the synoptic 
kinematics (discussed in the totality of this synopsis) could set 
the stage for repeated rounds of showers and thunderstorms across 
our east coast metro locales beginning on Tuesday and continuing 
into the extended period. Forecast model soundings have been 
persistent in showing a classic skinny CAPE profile, good low to 
middle level saturation (>80%), and opposing 1km to 6km wind 
directions. Mesoscale models (CAMs) should give us a better 
indication of precipitation patterns over the next 24-48 hours as 
upstream features approach South Florida. Even so, the 
predictability of the placement of higher end rainfall maxima's 
remains low.

begins to give way during the second half of the week.

tropical jet streak over the western Atlantic waters and 
northwestern Bahamas will set the stage for an area of favorable 
upper level divergence. Model guidance (ECWMF & GEFS) has come into

a stationary frontal boundary in close proximity to South Florida

solutions now depict a gradual deepening of the surface low pressure 
system with the low gradually lifting northwards beginning on

of a surface low to our southeast could eventually usher in a 
reinforcing surge of continental dry air via cyclonic flow around 
the feature as it lifts further away from the region late in the 
work week into the upcoming weekend. This may act to lift the 
greatest threat of heavy rainfall to the north of the region by 
Friday and return the region to low end rain chances during the 
upcoming weekend. A gradual warming trend across the region with 
maximum temperatures is forecast to continue during the second half 
of the week as temperatures are forecast to reach the low to mid 80s 
each afternoon through the upcoming weekend.

A complex atmospheric setup could provide the necessary ingredients 
for an increased urban flood threat across portions of the Miami-
Dade, Broward, and Palm Beach metro during the middle portion of 
this week. With a trend of increasing forecasted rainfall totals 
over the past 24 hours, the Weather Prediction Center (WPC) now 
highlights a large portion of the east coast metro areas in a Slight 
Risk (Level 2 of 4) for excessive rainfall beginning on Tuesday and 
continuing daily through Thursday. This means that within the Slight 
Risk area, there is a 15-39% probability of excessive rainfall 
leading to flash flooding within 25 miles of a given point. At the 
same time, soils remain very saturated across the eastern half of 
South Florida with NASA SPoRT-LIS percentiles still in the 80-99th
percentile range. Repeated rounds of heavy rainfall due to the 
synoptic and mesoscale phenomena (discussed above) could have a 
lower threshold/barrier than usual to cause urban flooding across
these areas. As model guidance shifts from run to run, 
considerable variability exists within the projected range of rainfall
totals. This is especially important with the upcoming setup as 
coastal convergence can often overperform (or alternatively, 
underperform) the forecast. 

Rather than anchor to a specific range of rainfall values that will 
be discussed below, the main takeaway from this section is that 
several inches of rainfall are a noteworthy possibility across 
portions of the east coast of South Florida this week. As of the 
latest forecast guidance, most likely accumulations (widespread) 
of 2-4" are forecast across the Miami-Dade, Broward, and Palm 
Beach metro through Friday morning. A reasonable worst case 
scenario (90th percentile, 1 in 10 chance of exceedance) suggests 
that the potential exists for localized pockets of 6-8"+ inches 
of rainfall through Friday morning. Contextualizing the values 
above with impacts, 2-4" of rainfall (most likely scenario) in a 
short period of time could result in localized urban flooding. If 
the higher end scenario amounts pan out then there is a greater 
concern for impactful urban flash flooding.

Ensemble guidance continues to depict uncertainty regarding the 
greatest signal (Central Florida vs South Florida) for the highest
rainfall totals with a wide assortment of potential solutions 
amongst ECWMF and GEFS members. In simple terms, additional 
fluctuations (up or down) in forecasted rainfall totals remains 
possible as uncertainty remains high.
