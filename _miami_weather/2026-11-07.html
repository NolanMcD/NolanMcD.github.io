.SHORT TERM...
(Today through Sunday)
Issued at 159 AM EST Sat Nov 7 2026

A rather potent mid level low/trough complex will push through a 
good portion of the Eastern Seaboard today into tonight before 
gradually pushing off into the western Atlantic on Sunday. While the 
trough axis will remain well to the north of the region, it will 
suppress the mid level ridging to the southeast which will result in 
mostly zonal flow over South Florida this afternoon and through the 
rest of the weekend. At the surface, a very weak frontal boundary 
currently over Central Florida will continue to slowly push 
southeastward through the region this afternoon before stalling 
over the southern portion of the area or over the Keys heading 
into the second half of the weekend. 

Out ahead of the front, some lower level moisture will gradually 
pool over the region as PWAT values gradually increase and will 
generally range between 1.3 and 1.6 inches this afternoon through 
Sunday. Most of this moisture will be in the lower levels of the 
atmospheric column as the latest forecast model soundings show a 
pretty good swath of drier air remaining in place across the mid 
levels during this time frame. The weakening frontal boundary moving 
into the region combined with an increase in lower level moisture 
could be enough to spark off some scattered shower activity this 
afternoon out ahead of the front. With a good amount of drier air in 
place across the mid levels, thunderstorm chances will remain very 
low as the front pushes into the region and continues to weaken. 
However, an isolated thunderstorm cannot be entirely ruled out over 
the southern areas and the Atlantic waters where instability may be 
slightly higher. 

As the front loses its forward momentum and gradually stalls out 
on Sunday, there will still be enough lower level moisture to 
keep just a slight chance of showers in place mainly over the east
coast as well as the Atlantic waters. However, most of the region
will remain dry through a good portion of the day. High 
temperatures for today will generally remain in the lower to mid 
80s along and north of Alligator Alley as well as the east coast 
metro areas. High temperatures could rise into the upper 80s 
across interior portions south of Alligator Alley. High 
temperatures on Sunday will generally rise into the lower to mid 
80s across most of the region, however, some interior locations 
south of Alligator Alley in Southwest Florida could see upper 80s 
once again on Sunday afternoon. 

&&

.LONG TERM...
(Sunday night through Friday)
Issued at 159 AM EST Sat Nov 7 2026

For the early portion of the week, mid level zonal flow gradually 
gives way to some weak mid level ridging through Monday and into 
Tuesday. At the surface, what is left of the very weak frontal 
boundary washes out as high pressure builds in from the north. This 
will allow for a light to moderate east northeasterly wind flow to 
remain in place during this time frame. With this area of high 
pressure in place, combined with the weak mid level ridging aloft, 
there will be enough subsidence taking place to support mostly dry 
conditions across the region on Monday and Tuesday. However, with 
the east northeasterly wind flow continuing, some lower level 
moisture may still linger over the region which could keep a very 
low end chance of showers in place mainly over the Atlantic waters
and east coast. Any shower that does develop will be low topped 
and rather short lived. High temperatures on Monday and Tuesday 
will generally range from the lower 80s across the Lake Okeechobee
region and east coast metro areas to the mid to upper 80s over 
Southwest Florida. 

Heading into the middle of the week, the weather pattern will start 
to change a bit as a mid level shortwave pushes across the Gulf 
Coast states and into Northern Florida on Wednesday into Wednesday 
night. At the surface, high pressure will gradually start to push 
further east into the western Atlantic. The latest global and 
ensemble guidance remain in relatively good agreement keeping 
this shortwave off to the north of the region, however, it will 
start to draw in some deeper moisture over South Florida. This 
could increase the chances for showers during the middle of the 
week especially during the afternoon hours during peak diurnal 
heating. High temperatures on Wednesday will generally range from 
the lower 80s across the Lake Okeechobee region and the east coast
metro areas to the mid to upper 80s across Southwest Florida. 

The uncertainty in the forecast towards the end of the week rises as 
the latest global and ensemble guidance show a deeper trough 
pushing over the Eastern Seaboard during this time frame. At the 
surface, guidance is showing signs of a developing area of low 
pressure over the Mid-Atlantic region during the Thursday and 
Friday time frame. The cold front associated with this system 
could move into and pass through the region towards the end of 
the week which would increase the chances of showers and 
thunderstorms during this time frame. However, guidance still does
remain in disagreement in regards to exact timing of the front as
well as its intensity as it approaches the region towards the end
of the week. With the front nearby, the latest forecast increases
the chances of showers and thunderstorms for the end of the week.
Behind the front, there will be the potential for a much cooler
air mass to push into the region for the following weekend. More
to follow on this as the details become clearer as the week 
progresses.
