.SHORT TERM...
(Today through Wednesday)
Issued at 335 AM EDT Tue Oct 13 2026

07z surface analysis depicts the establishment of light peninsular 
drainage flow across South Florida early this morning as a dry 
continental airmass continues to gradually filter in across the 
region. In fact, HYSPLIT backward trajectories trace the airmass in 
place across South Florida today back to the southwestern United 
States over a week and a half ago. A true welcome reprieve from the 
moisture and humidity that has persisted across our region over the 
past several days. Dewpoints are running about 5-10 degrees lower 
than at this time last night and are expected to drop a few more 
degrees before daybreak. Away from the coast and underneath clear 
skies, these lower dewpoint values will allow for efficient 
radiational cooling. A few isolated locales could even dip into the 
upper 50s to low 60s at daybreak across inland southwestern Florida 
as winds decouple underneath a nocturnal near-surface inversion. For 
the vast majority of us however, wake-up temps will remain in the 
low to mid 70s. 07z mesoanalysis this morning also reveals a very 
sharp precipitable water gradient across South Florida as drier air 
continues to push southward across the region. In fact, observed 
values upstream are in line with prior deterministic and ensemble 
guidance that forecasted precipitable water values across South 
Florida below the 10% percentile for this time of year. While rain 
chances will remain minimal (<10%) today, overnight mesoscale model 
guidance (HRRR,RRFS,etc) continues to hint at the potential of 
isolated shallow convection (sprinkles) pushing onshore along the 
east coast late this afternoon into tonight. Even in (all things 
considered) a very dry weather regime, never count out breezy 
northeasterly flow above the warm Gulfstream waters producing an 
isolated coastal sprinkle or two. No significant temperature change 
is expected today (forecast highs have held steady in the upper 80s 
to low 90s) but there will be a noticeable change in the air as 
dewpoints remain in the upper 60s to low 70s all day. With the 
humidity out of the air for the time being, peak apparent 
temperature values this afternoon will only reach the low to mid 90s 
(compared to persistent values in the upper 90s to low 100s over the 
last several days). I know it may be difficult to contain your 
excitement after learning this latest factoid, but we must take our 
weather wins when we can.

The advertised reprieve from moisture will be a short lived one as 
the frontolytic boundary and envelope of associated moisture lift 
back across the region during the day on Wednesday as an expansive 
area of surface high pressure becomes established across the 
southeastern United States. Mid-level ridging will also build across 
the southeastern United States on Wednesday, keeping the region 
devoid of any frontal systems. Wednesday still appears to be, very 
much so a transition day between the current dry airmass in place 
across South Florida and continued moisture advection later in the 
week. Breezy northeasterly flow and increasing moisture will support 
the potential of quick moving shower activity, still vertically 
capped due to residual dry air aloft. The latest forecast continues 
to depict 20-40% rain chances across the southeastern half of the 
region on Wednesday afternoon. Forecast high temperatures range from 
the upper 80s along the east coast to values in the low 90s across 
southwestern Florida.

.LONG TERM...
(Wednesday night through Monday)
Issued at 335 AM EDT Tue Oct 13 2026

Northeasterly surface flow will gradually veer to an easterly 
direction by Thursday as the axis of surface ridging across the 
southeastern United States slides eastward into the western Atlantic 
waters. This will focus the best diurnal rain chances across the 
western half of the peninsula during the second half of the work 
week, although overnight and morning activity will still be a 
possibility along the east coast of South Florida given the breezy 
easterly flow and continued moisture advection. Mid-level ridging 
briefly established across the southeastern United States during the 
early to mid portion of the work week will quickly flatten by 
Thursday as the next lobe of troughing slides eastward across the 
northern United States. As the positively tilted trough amplifies 
and digs across the eastern United States, a strengthening surface 
low will strengthen and push east-northeastward in tandem. Model 
guidance remains in agreement that a surface frontal boundary 
attached to the aforementioned surface low will push southwards 
across the eastern United States in response and arrive in the 
vicinity of the Florida Peninsula during the upcoming weekend. There 
remains a great amount of variability/ uncertainty in model 
solutions of exact frontal boundary placement) from run to run and 
model member to model member (ensemble vs deterministic). Model 
guidance has trended away from a full passage of the boundary across 
the region and now depicts an initial stall of the boundary somewhere 
across the Florida Peninsula. 

The placement of the aforementioned frontal boundary in relation to 
South Florida, surface high pressure axis to the north, and any 
mesoscale coastal convergence will have a direct influence on 
potential rainfall totals, wind gusts, and the severity of any 
marine/coastal impacts across the region during the upcoming weekend 
into next week. Forecasted precipitable water and low level RH 
values (via forecast soundings) during this time period appear to 
approach the daily max for this time of year, indicative of an 
airmass that is saturated in the low level boundary layer. 500mb 
temperatures are also forecast to be on the warmer side which may 
support an expansive warm cloud layer. Outside of the heavy rain, 
guidance has also been consistent in showing the enhancement of a 
pressure gradient across the western Atlantic and South Florida. 
This would act to increase wind speeds across the area, resulting in 
gusty conditions and potential marine and coastal impacts. The 
forecast remains highly uncertain and fluid, stay tuned for a 
clearer idea of what may happen as we progress through the 
remainder of the work week. Forecast high temperatures will remain 
status quo through the end of the work week with values in the upper 
80s to low 90s possible each afternoon. Apparent temperatures will 
also rebound with increased moisture, reaching the upper 90s to low 
100s on Thursday and Friday. An increase in wind and rain chances 
may keep ambient and apparent temperatures lower beginning during 
the upcoming weekend.
