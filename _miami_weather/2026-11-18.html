.SHORT TERM...
(Today through Thursday)
Issued at 1232 AM EST Wed Nov 18 2026

Heavy rainfall remains the main concern through the short term 
period as a complex and somewhat uncertain weather pattern continues 
to play out. At the surface, high pressure remains entrenched across 
the eastern seaboard north of Florida, while a weak area of low 
pressure continues to meander across the Gulf. Higher up in the 
atmosphere, a shortwave continues to slowly progress eastward across 
the Gulf coast states which could aid to maintain the surface low, 
or even lead to a gradual strengthening over the next couple of 
days. A stationary boundary associated with this system remains 
draped across the Florida peninsula today and likely tomorrow as 
well which will act to maintain a quite impressive plume of moisture 
across the Southern Florida peninsula. PWATs from the 00Z MFL 
sounding were 1.74 inches which sits just at about the 90th 
percentile for this time of year, although modeled PWATs rise into 
the 1.8-2.0" range over the coming 24 hours, which would be 
extremely impressive for November. In the past 24 hours, portions of 
coastal SE Florida have received between 3-5" with coastal Broward 
and NE Miami-Dade receiving the highest totals. While we are 
currently in a lull during the late overnight hours tonight, the 
stage remains set for additional heavy rainfall today and Thursday, 
with coastal convergence being the primary concern which could lead 
to training cells and flash flooding across urban areas. 

Global models still remain a bit confused on the evolution of this 
surface low, and latest GFS/EURO runs show an additional surface low 
deepening  across the western Atlantic late on Thursday. The exact 
progression of this feature will determine where the stationary 
boundary meanders over the coming days, which will determine exactly 
where the highest rainfall totals will fall. Despite this, I can 
speak broadly that the eastern coastal areas of South Florida will 
continue to be favored today and tomorrow for periods of heavy 
rainfall. Rainfall totals over the next 48 hours will broadly be in 
the 2-4" range across most areas of SE Florida, although a 
reasonable worst case could bring an additional 5-8" especially for 
areas that receive repeated rounds of rainfall. A Flood Watch 
remains in effect through Thursday evening.

.LONG TERM...
(Thursday night through Tuesday)
Issued at 1232 AM EST Wed Nov 18 2026

On Friday, global models depict multiple areas of low pressure 
across the Gulf and western Atlantic. Our original area of low 
pressure remains in the Gulf while an additional lobe of 
vorticity is able to break off into the western Atlantic and 
becomes the dominant feature. Across the eastern CONUS, high 
pressure at the surface begins to breakdown as a cold front sweeps
across the central United States. Before the arrival of drier air for
the weekend, perhaps one additional day of heavy rainfall
potential on Friday, especially earlier in the day as PWATs remain
maximized and will still approach 2". Exact forecast for Friday
will be heavily dependent on the evolution of the surface lows,
however, so I would place confidence on this portion of the
forecast on the lower end for now. The cold front sweeping across
the central CONUS and retreat of high pressure will help pull the
low over the western Atlantic towards the north and finally usher
in drier air across the area through the weekend timeframe. As 
this system deepens and races off to the north, PWATs drop into 
the 1.2-1.3" range and Saturday and continue to fall to perhaps 
below 1" on Sunday and Monday. This should help limit rain chances
through the weekend, although there will still be a chance 
(10-20%) for scattered shower activity each day through the 
weekend and early next week. 

Moisture begins to return heading towards the middle portion of the 
week as an area of low pressure slides across the Gulf states. This 
will likely be our next shot for more significant rainfall although 
details will become clearer over the coming days.
