.SHORT TERM...
(Today through Saturday)
Issued at 318 AM EDT Fri Oct 23 2026

Coastal showers along the Atlantic waters will continue to be a 
nuisance through the early morning hours as coastal convergence 
continues to set up along the more unstable Gulf Stream. Mid 
level water vapor imagery and the recent MFL upper air sounding 
indicate that there is quite a bit of dry air above 700 mb, so 
most convection will remain very weak and low topped. The better 
moisture content looks to be further east along a weak inverted 
surface trough in the Bahamas. But low level lapse rates of 7 
C/Km and limited instability of around 1000 J/kg should continue 
to support isolated to scattered showers moving onshore. PoPs 
along the Atlantic waters and adjacent coastal zones will be 
between 50% and 60% through the afternoon. More inland, chances 
for showers will be isolated, at around 30% for the eastern metro 
areas and less than 20% for interior portions of South Florida. 
With showers pinned along the coast, a few areas may see up to an 
inch of rain. However, the drier air and subsidence aloft should 
keep most activity pretty weak. With precipitable water values 
near climatology, chances of seeing greater than an inch are only 
about 30% for South Florida. Therefore, the risk of urban flooding
will be very low (at least related to precipitation, keep reading
for more info on the King Tides).

Aloft, most of the eastern CONUS finds itself in deep longwave 
troughing, with a couple of embedded shortwave vorticity maxima 
moving southeast into the Atlantic. Meanwhile at the surface, strong 
high pressure centered over New England will continue to tighten 
the pressure gradient along the eastern seaboard. This will result
in strengthening winds through the weekend from the northeast. 
Winds will generally be 10 to 15 mph, with gusts up to 25 mph. 
While these winds won't be quite impactful yet, they will serve to
enhance onshore flow along the Atlantic coast, which when paired 
with increased swell and wave activity may help increase the risk 
of urban tidal flooding along metro Dade, Broward, and Palm Beach 
during the peak King Tides.

On Saturday, the base of the mid-level trough swings across the 
peninsula, bringing along with it a swath of vorticity through the 
region. However, the better dynamics for thunderstorms will remain 
offshore over the Atlantic where there is better divergence aloft. 
Another player on Saturday will be the evolution of a shortwave 
trough digging south from the Carolinas and into the Florida coast. 
Some ensembles are starting to come into agreement in showing a cut 
off low developing off of the Space Coast on Saturday. There is a 
lot of uncertainty at this moment regarding the timing of this 
feature and how far south it may travel into our region. For now, 
PoPs for Saturday afternoon remain low across most of South Florida 
(less than 20%) outside of the immediate Atlantic coast where 
coastal convergence storms keep chances higher. HREF ensembles LPMMs 
keep all the heaviest precipitation offshore and to the north 
through Saturday evening, with solutions only showing about 0.5 to 1 
inch of rain at most.

.LONG TERM...
(Saturday night through Thursday)
Issued at 318 AM EDT Fri Oct 23 2026

There is increasing confidence in greater chances of precipitation 
on Saturday evening into Sunday morning. Increased moisture will 
surge into the region as the mid level Atlantic trough or cut off 
low continues to dig south into the western Atlantic. Some solutions 
display this system stalling as far south as Cuba, with a stationary 
frontal boundary at the surface draped across South Florida. 
However, precipitable water values don't seem all too impressive, 
with values climbing up to around 1.6 to 1.7 inches, which is 
between the mean and 75th percentile for this time of year. So while 
chances of precipitation will be high, between 85% and 95% for 
the Atlantic waters and coastal Atlantic areas, QPF continues to 
trend down among guidance. Ensemble QPF clusters vary both 
spatially and temporally due to the uncertainty regarding the 
upper low feature. Some solutions keep the highest QPF offshore, 
while others bring it onshore but north of South Florida. One 
scenario brings a swath of heavier precipitation along the eastern
metro areas, between 2 to 3 inches through Monday. Model 
soundings indicate that conditions will be favorable for coastal 
convergence storms to be pinned along the coast, with brisk 
northeasterly flow at the surface nearly opposite to the strong 
westerly to southwesterly flow in the 700 mb and 500 mb heights. 
So, if several storms were to be pinned along the coast for 
several hours, localized urban flooding for low lying and poor 
drainage areas may be possible. However, chances of widespread 
amounts of greater than 2 inches are only at around 45%, with 
highest rain amounts likely staying over the waters.

Strong northeast winds will persist through Monday and some 
locations near the coast will be flirting with Wind Advisory 
conditions. Broken tree branches and loose objects may be flung 
about in winds between 25 to 35 mph. One thing to keep in mind 
for this weekend is the combination of the King Tide with heavy 
rain and increased onshore flow. All these factors together may 
raise tide and water levels into the moderate flood category for 
coastal urban areas along Dade, Broward, and Palm Beach. Roadways 
and low lying areas may be flooded for several hours, especially 
if the high tide coincides with heavy rainfall. Remember, turn 
around, don't drown. Never drive, walk, or wade, over flooded 
areas. 

Surface high pressure over the northeast will keep the tight 
pressure gradient along the eastern seaboard through the middle of 
the week. This will allow for breezy winds and coastal showers to 
continue through at least Wednesday. Chances of precipitation  drop 
to around 40% for eastern parts of South Florida through the end of 
the week as mid-level ridging begins to build over the Gulf and
slide over Florida.

.HYDROLOGY...
Issued at 408 PM EDT Thu Oct 22 2026

Tied with, if not edging out, the expected impacts from heavy rain 
is the threat for moderate or higher coastal flooding as we draw 
closer to the peak of this King Tide cycle. Not only will we reach 
the peak of the cycle Sunday into Monday, but it is also expected to 
be the peak King Tide of the season as well. Unfortunately this peak 
also comes at the time of greatest onshore winds and heavy rain 
potential. Both of these factors are expected to increase the 
impacts of the saltwater flooding, either from impeding drainage of 
heavy rain or adding a surge component to the peak tides. It is 
within the ensemble envelope that Major category flooding will occur 
along the immediate coast, and even if the gauges don't quite reach 
Major level, it's possible the impacts would be the same if 
freshwater flooding is adding to the saltwater flooding. All 
sensitive locations down the east coast should be preparing for the 
highest tidal impacts of the season.
