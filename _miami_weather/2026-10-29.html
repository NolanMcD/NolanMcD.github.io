.SHORT TERM...
(Today through Friday)
Issued at 326 AM EDT Thu Oct 29 2026

A slight transition is in the works for South Florida as we head 
into the end of the week and the weekend. The pressure gradient 
responsible for the breezy conditions the past couple of days will 
continue to relax as the area of low pressure previously over the 
Bahamas continues to drift eastward over the Atlantic Ocean. As this 
happens, an area of surface pressure over the southeastern US will 
extend south along the peninsula, driving continued light 
northeasterly flow across the area. Moisture will also continue 
to advect across South Florida along the periphery of this surface
ridge, with forecast soundings showing well-moistened lower 
levels likely through the period. However, chances for 
precipitation will remain generally on the lower end each 
afternoon (PoPs in the 30-40% range at best), thanks to ridging 
and drier air sitting aloft. A few pop-up showers and a rogue 
thunderstorm or two could still develop as diurnal heating destabilizes
the atmospheric column, but convective growth should be limited 
by subsidence associated with the ridge. 

High temperatures will rise a few degrees as high pressure builds 
and more sunshine is possible, so temperatures will reach the mid to 
upper 80s. 

&&

.LONG TERM...
(Friday night through Wednesday)
Issued at 326 AM EDT Thu Oct 29 2026

A wetter pattern looks likely this weekend as mid-level ridging over 
the Gulf is forecast to break down, giving way to a more zonal flow 
pattern aloft across the Florida peninsula. At the same time, an 
upper level shortwave feature will begin to develop over the western 
Gulf. Moisture will pool across the area on Saturday ahead of this 
feature, and with ridging aloft and near the surface both weakening, 
weaker northeasterly winds will allow sea breeze dynamics to once
again play a more prominent role in convective development during
the afternoon. This will mean a gradual increase in rain chances 
through the day, with the majority of convection focused across 
interior and southwest FL during the afternoon hours where the 
breezes meet. Afternoon temperatures could peak in the upper 80s. 

Conditions become more unsettled Sunday into Monday as the 
aforementioned upper-level disturbance approaches the peninsula. 
This feature will help support large-scale ascent across the area, 
and should cool the mid-levels enough to support more instability 
along the atmospheric column. This is supported by the ECMWF Extreme 
Forecast Index (EFI) from yesterday's 12Z run, which shows the 
ensemble leaning towards unusually high CAPE across the area.As 
such, expect scattered to numerous showers and thunderstorms each 
afternoon. Frequent lightning, gusty winds and locally heavy 
rainfall will be the main concerns with any of the stronger storms
that develop. 

Conditions look to dry out as we head into the middle of next week, 
with chances for rainfall limited to some coastal showers each day 
(PoPs 15-20%). Temperatures will cool down a bit Sunday-Monday with 
the increased storminess and cloud cover, with highs in the mid 80s. 
Lows will slide into the mid 60s across the interior, and the low
70s along the coasts.
