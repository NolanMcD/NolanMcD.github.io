.SHORT TERM...
(Today through Monday)
Issued at 140 AM EDT Sun Oct 11 2026

Pumpkin spice beverages simply won't suffice today as a flashback to 
summer-like conditions continues across South Florida. Something 
more tropical would be more appropriate as it would be on brand for 
the weather outside even as we enter mid October. Temperatures will 
remain above average (upper 80s) for this time of year, remaining in 
the low 90s for much of the afternoon hours outside of any 
convective cooling influences. While high temperature record values 
for today's date appear to be safe, the combination of dewpoints in 
the mid to upper 70s, slackening surface winds out of a southerly 
direction, and a lack of widespread convection will result in heat 
indices (apparent temperatures) reaching the 100-105F range this 
afternoon. The prototype Probabilistic HeatRisk depicts a high (70-
90%) chance of Major HeatRisk across inland suburbs of South Florida 
today with a 100% probability of Moderate HeatRisk at coastal 
locations. Members of the the public who are sensitive to heat 
should limit prolonged exposure and take necessary precautions 
accordingly. As of 06z this morning, our overnight temperatures at 
coastal locations also remain well above average and close to the 
daily max minimum temperature for today's date. In large part this 
is due to a slight onshore component (SSErly) of surface winds, that 
still primarily remain out of a southerly direction across the 
region. Near max overnight temperatures and apparent temperatures in 
the low 100s today will bring little relief from the humidity and 
above average temperatures that will remain with us for the 
remainder of the weekend. 

The vorticity of what was once Isaias has been completely absorbed 
by a expansive lobe of mid-level troughing over the eastern United 
States. Pressure falls along a triple point (occluded front, warm 
front, and cold front) to the south of the Appalachian Mountains 
signal the development of a forecasted new area of low pressure that 
will accelerate to the northeast, eventually bringing a frontal 
boundary into South Florida on Monday. In the meantime however, a 
plume of deep moisture out ahead of that weak frontal boundary 
remains in place across much of the Florida Peninsula with 
precipitable water values well above the 90th percentile for today's 
date. Aloft, the axis of mid to upper level ridging will briefly 
build into South Florida today with 500mb temperatures remaining on 
the warm side, between the 75th-90th percentile. Riveting technical 
factoids aside, the main item to takeaway from the mid-level ridge 
enhancement is a pocket of drier air aloft that will push westward 
into the eastern half of the region today. This already shows up 
quite nicely this morning on GOES East Mid-Level Water Vapor 
imagery. The mixing down of the drier air aloft in tandem with 
meager low and mid-level lapse rates may suppress most shower and 
thunderstorm activity across the eastern half of the region today. A 
precipitable water and dewpoint gradient is forecast to materialize 
across the region today with drier values across the southeastern 
half of the region and perceptible water values near the daily max 
across southwestern Florida which will be close in proximity to the 
frontal boundary. The latest forecast continues to feature the 
highest rain chances today (60-70%) across inland locales and the 
Lake Okeechobee region. Convective parameters once again appear 
lackluster with the main forecast concern remaining isolated swaths 
of 1-2" of rainfall in short periods of time. 

As the mid-level trough pushes offshore of the northeastern United 
States, the stout mid-level ridge in place across the western 
Atlantic waters will flatten and lose influence over our region by 
Monday morning. The enhancement of a surface low offshore of the 
northeastern United States will provide enough forcing for the 
aforementioned frontal boundary across Central Florida and the Gulf 
to slowly slide southward across South Florida during the day on 
Monday. With all of the best dynamics now well removed from the 
region, convective activity on Monday is forecast to be much more 
sparse in nature. That being said, maximized ascent and residual 
moisture along the boundary could still set the stage for some 
isolated to scattered shower and thunderstorm activity. High 
temperatures on Monday will rise into the upper 80s along the east 
coast to the lower 90s across interior portions of Southwest 
Florida.

.LONG TERM...
(Monday night through Saturday)
Issued at 140 AM EDT Sun Oct 11 2026

As the boundary slides southward across the region Monday night 
into early Tuesday, a dry continental airmass will make a long-
awaited return to South Florida. While this will not be a "cold" 
or even "cool" front by any metric (consider keeping the fall gear
in the wardrobe for now), the change in the air will certainly be
noticeable as we drop from forecasted dewpoints in mid to upper 
70s on Monday to dewpoints in the upper 60s to low 70s on Tuesday.
Even more remarkable will be the drop in precipitable water 
values, deterministic and ensemble guidance suggest a potential of
seeing precipitable water values across South Florida below the 
10% percentile for this time of year. No significant temperature 
change is expected behind this front during the daytime hours 
(forecast highs in the upper 80s to low 90s Tuesday and Wednesday)
but a slight drop in overnight temperatures is forecast with the 
potential of low temperatures in the mid to upper 60s across 
inland southwestern Florida on Tuesday morning. 

The advertised reprieve from moisture will be a short lived one as 
the frontolytic boundary and envelope of associated moisture lift 
back across the region late Wednesday into Thursday as an expansive 
area of surface high pressure becomes established across the 
southeastern United States. Mid-level ridging briefly established 
across the southeastern United States during the early to mid 
portion of the work week will quickly flatten by Thursday as the 
next lobe of troughing slides eastward across the northern United 
States. As the positively tilted trough amplifies and digs across the 
eastern United States, a strengthening surface low will strengthen 
and push eastward in tandem. While this is at the tail end of the 
extended period, ensemble and deterministic model guidance depict the 
arrival of a frontal boundary in the vicinity of South Florida late 
in the work week into the upcoming weekend. Deep tropical moisture 
in the warm sector out ahead of the boundary could result in higher 
rain chances towards the end of the work week with drier and cooler 
weather behind it. For now however, it's still way too early to 
speculate on exact forecast details.
