.SHORT TERM...
(Today through Saturday)
Issued at 140 AM EST Fri Nov 13 2026

The same overall atmospheric configuration looks to remain in place 
across the Contiguous United States early this morning with a 
sprawling ridge of high pressure dominating the western half of the 
country and a potent trough situated over the eastern half. Over the 
southeastern US, the base of this deepening upper trough (with 500-
mb height anomalies near its base over the Southern Appalachians 
falling within only the 2nd percentile for the date as per the REFS) 
has united with the strong flow aloft that is associated with the 
subtropical jet that we continue to have overhead here in South 
Florida. As a result, an excessively strong jet streak has developed 
within this area of confluence between the two air currents, 
encompassing northern and central Florida and extending several 
hundred miles into the Southwest Atlantic waters, with winds aloft 
at the 200mb level oscillating between 120 and 130 kts which, 
according to the REFS, is between the 98 and 99th percentiles for 
this time of year.

Although South Florida is displaced to the south of this impressive 
jet streak, the same subtropical jet is still pumping in winds aloft 
at between 80 and 95 kts, which is between the 92nd and 96th 
percentiles as per climatology. Embedded moisture along this 
subtropical jet kept our skies here in South Florida mostly cloudy 
on Thursday, and recent IR (infrared) satellite imagery continues to 
suggest the same this morning with an abundance of mid to high-level 
clouds still streaming across the peninsula from southwest to 
northeast. Both ACARS and the 00Z MFL soundings support these 
observations with minuscule dew point depressions in the upper third 
of the atmosphere and slightly drier air in place across the low to 
mid-levels.

Taking a look at RTMA-derived surface winds, a weak surface trough 
can be pinpointed over South Florida's local Atlantic waters with a 
frail and slackening cold front located a little further south over 
the Florida Straits. There are showers in association with these two 
features, mainly confined to their respective axises of surface 
convergence. Over mainland South Florida, all remains mostly dry as 
there is little in the way of forcing across the region. The main 
story recently has been the strong cold front that is expected to 
bring us our first real cooldown of the Fall season, and you may be 
wondering, where exactly is it as we speak? Looking off the north 
towards the borders of Florida, Georgia and Alabama; a sharp 
demarcation in dew points ultimately reveals the position of the 
front, as does the presence, via surface plots, of post-frontal 
northwesterly surface winds over the Florida panhandle and southern 
GA/AL and southwesterly flow over north-central Florida along and 
just ahead of the front. This front will continue to work its way 
down the rest of the Florida peninsula through the rest of the 
overnight hours, roughly arriving at the Lake Okeechobee region 
shortly after daybreak Friday morning, which rightfully explains the 
lack of cooling across South Florida as the air mass we will be 
waking up to will be still be pre-frontal. We will, thus, be walking 
our the door Friday morning to lows that will be fairly similar to 
yesterday morning's, ranging between the mid to upper 60s north of 
Alligator Alley and in the low 70s across the southern half of the 
peninsula.

High-res model guidance does suggest that the front could try and 
squeeze out a few showers as it approaches our region Friday morning 
and into the first half of the afternoon as it continues to push 
southward, but PoPs do not want to exceed 10% in many South Florida 
locations as it does so. The 00Z MFL sounding showed a measly 
surface-based CAPE value of just 2 J/Kg, and with model guidance not 
suggesting it to climb past 500 J/Kg through the rest of the 
overnight hours and into the morning and early afternoon hours, the 
risk of scattered showers and let alone thunderstorms will be very 
low. As per forecast soundings (and the lingering saturation they 
continue to show in the mid to upper levels), the plentiful cloud 
cover we have observed this morning is forecast to stay in place 
through Friday afternoon and should continue to suppress any 
significant amounts of surface destabilization, thus keeping things 
rather quiet across South Florida and only resulting in some light 
stratiform rain here and there as the boundary pushes through our 
region.

Continental high pressure that, according to surface observations, 
extends from Manitoba/Ontario in Canada southward through the 
Midwest and Lower Missouri River Valley will allow winds to become 
northwesterly later this morning across South Florida amidst our 
passing cold front. They should be fairly light across mainland 
South Florida through the majority of the day, before starting to 
increase over the frictionless local Atlantic and Gulf waters as a 
local pressure gradient begins to develop between the aforementioned 
high to the north and the departing front. Since the northern half 
of South Florida will be the first to experience the intrusion of 
this much cooler air (in addition to the high cloud cover we are 
expecting) between the morning and early afternoon hours coinciding 
with the peak diurnal heating period, high temperatures will only be 
climbing into the mid to upper 70s north of Alligator Alley. Will be 
slightly warmer over the southern half of South Florida ahead of the 
advancing front, but lingering cloud cover will allow our highs to 
drop by a few degrees to what we experienced yesterday and achieve 
values that are closer to average for this time of year (low 80s).

High-res mesoscale models i.e. the RAP and HRRR show our atmospheric 
profile beginning to undergo a notable north-to-south drying trend 
during the second half of the afternoon across South Florida as dry 
air on the the backside of the large upper trough over the eastern 
US makes its push into our region and helps to displace the moist 
subtropical jet to our south. This will gradually reduce our cloud 
cover into Friday evening, setting the stage for radiational cooling 
and our first real taste of Fall as the cooler, less humid air mass 
succeeding the front spills into the remainder of South Florida. We 
are forecast to wake up on Saturday morning to temperatures that 
will be between 5 and 10 degrees below average for this time of 
year: in the low to mid 50s around Lake Okeechobee and over the 
interior, with our coastal metros generally dropping into the upper 
50s. NBM has a 30-35% chance of temperatures dropping to below 50F 
west of the Lake, while the LREF has just a slimmer of a higher 
chance of 35-40% over the northwesternmost portion of our CWA in 
Glades County. As a result of this cool air advection, high 
temperatures on Saturday will only be climbing into the mid to upper 
60s around Lake O and the interior, and into the low 70s across our 
east coast and Naples metros. With a very dry, post-frontal 
atmospheric column, expect mostly sunny skies with a crisp breeze 
out of the north in response to high pressure over the Midwest/Lower 
Missouri Valley today shifting slightly eastward towards the Mid-
Mississippi Valley. Winds will be stronger over the local waters and 
along coastal regions than they will be today due to an enhancing 
pressure gradient, so it will be best to avoid any boating 
activities and to instead go out and perhaps buy yourself a 
delicious peppermint mocha (too soon?).

SUNDAY & MONDAY...

Brisk upper level flow will remain the atmospheric status quo across 
South Florida during the second half of the weekend as the sub-
tropical jet stream remains entrenched across our region. Model 
guidance has come into better agreement within the past 24 hours 
regarding the passage of a shortwave axis across the region during 
the day on Sunday. However even with the injection of upper level 
dynamics (Maxima of upper level divergence and low level convergence 
across South Florida as the region resides in the right entrance of 
a developing jet-streak), the continental airmass (post-FROPA) still 
in place will limit any threat of convective development over land, 
with an outside chance (<10%) of a few isolated showers developing 
over the instability laden waters of the Gulfstream. Forecast model 
soundings depict a dearth of moisture in much of the atmospheric 
column (above 850-900mb) across our region on Sunday but also reveal 
a subtle but important shift in prevailing wind direction at the 
surface. With such a transient mid to upper level pattern 
increasingly in play across the southern United States, the axis of 
surface ridging will slide eastwards across the upper Mississippi 
River valley on Saturday night into Sunday morning. Why is this 
detail important to us here in South Florida? The siting of surface 
ridging across the eastern United States is instrumental in 
determining just how long a "cold" snap is able to last across our 
region given the moderating influences of the surrounding Atlantic 
waters. The longer surface flow remains out of a northwesterly 
direction (Straight from the continent), the longer the duration of 
a "cold" snap across our area. In this case, the advertised 
temperature drop for South Florida will be rather short-lived, so be 
sure to partake (Pumpkin spice anyone?) in this iteration of Florida 
fall. 

As the surface ridge slides eastward, anticyclonic flow on the 
periphery of the expansive ridge across South Florida is also 
forecast to veer in direction. Nocturnal stratification at daybreak 
is expected to result in light northerly flow across most of the 
region before surface winds re-couple and veer north-easterly across 
the region post-sunrise. This could result in an interesting 
distribution of slightly warmer minimum temperature values along the 
east coast of South Florida by daybreak on Sunday, while areas 
across inland southwestern Florida could see values in the upper 40s 
as surface winds slacken and decouple. The veering of surface winds 
to a breezy northeast direction during the day on Sunday is forecast 
to jump-start a gradual warming trend as the airmass in place across 
South Florida gradually moderates. Forecast high temperatures will 
range from the upper 70s across coastal locales to temperatures in 
the mid 70s near Lake Okeechobee. 

Ensemble and deterministic guidance continue to depict the next mid-
level shortwave sliding southeastward from the Southern Plains into 
the Lower Mississippi Valley and along the Northern Gulf Coast 
during the day on Monday. The orientation of the sub-tropical jet 
stream and a maxima of upper-level divergence out ahead of the short-
wave continues to be resolved by model guidance in developing an 
area of surface low pressure across the western Gulf. While this 
feature is forecast to remain well to the west of South Florida on 
Monday, a strengthening pressure gradient (Between the surface low 
in the Gulf and surface high across the eastern CONUS) is being 
depicted by model guidance to result in gusty conditions and gradual 
moisture return across the region on Monday. While the airmass will 
still be in the process of moderating, gusty NELY to ENErly flow 
will support the threat of low-topped shower activity moving 
onshore along the east coast of South Florida during the afternoon
hours. High temperatures will also continue on an upward 
trajectory with forecast high temps in the upper 70s to low 80s.

TUESDAY & BEYOND...

As the mid-level shortwave accelerates southeastward across the 
northern Gulf during the day on Tuesday, the accompanying area of 
surface low pressure is modeled to do so in tandem, moving briskly 
along across the central Gulf. Comparing yesterday's model suite to 
the latest deterministic and ensemble guidance solutions, the 00z 
model suite has continued to trend with a more southern trajectory 
of the mid-level shortwave which would also have direct implications 
on the track of the associated surface low and frontal boundaries 
displaced further to the south. Similar to last night, 500mb cluster 
analysis suggests that ensemble guidance (ECMWF, GEFS, and CMCE) 
remains in fair agreement with the placement & timing of the 
shortwave as variance remains low. The shift southward of the 
surface low and consequently, best foci of ascent is also 
represented in QPF cluster analysis with the greatest clustering of 
ensemble members now depicting the possibility of lower rainfall 
totals occurring across South Florida than what is depicted on the 
ensemble mean (GEFS, ECMWF, and CMCE). 

While the latest forecast guidance has trended to a more southern 
track of the surface low just to the south of South Florida, two 
other potential trajectories include: 1) a passage directly over 
South Florida or 2) just to the north of South Florida. The 
atmosphere is a fluid and forecast alterations are certainly still 
possible as we remain within the 4-5 day time frame. Lift, 
instability, and moisture are the three main ingredients for 
thunderstorm development and will directly be influenced by the 
exact placement and evolution of the surface low. The fourth 
ingredient that is needed for more robust thunderstorm development, 
shear, will be dictated by where the sub-tropical jet-stream maxima 
is located in relation to surface features.

As deterministic guidance can vary widely from run to run and depict 
a singular outcome , ensemble guidance can present a wider and more 
probabilistic array of potential solutions. The best analogy to help 
make this important distinction (deterministic vs ensemble) is to 
picture a race car derby. Consider a singular derby car that is 
propelled with force down the derby ramp. One singular input 
produced one singular outcome, which is a really simplistic 
equivalent to deterministic model guidance. To contrast this, now 
imagine a line of derby cars, each built with different materials 
and given varying levels of force down the derby ramp. Each derby 
car had slightly different input parameters which will lead to 
different outcomes, a wider array of potential solutions. Bringing 
the point home, current ECENS and GEFS ensemble members show a very 
wide spread of potential outcomes with this system. Some ensemble 
members depict the threat of thunderstorm activity, heavy rainfall, 
and higher precipitation rainfall totals across South Florida. The 
orientation of projected rainfall totals on some of these ensemble 
members hint at the threat of frictional convergence as gusty 
northeasterly winds are slowed along the east coast of Florida, 
which could hint at a threat of locally higher amounts at isolated 
locations should these solutions become a reality. Alternatively, a 
large subset of ensemble members depict the opposite with the 
greatest impacts displaced away from the region over the 
northwestern Bahamas and western Atlantic waters. Each ensemble 
member has slightly different inputs on the placement of the frontal 
boundaries and surface low pressure system, which is resulting in a 
wide array of outcomes. This wide array of outcomes also helps us 
infer that there is still a considerable amount of uncertainty in 
the evolution of this feature next week and how we here in South 
Florida could be impacted. 

The monitoring of upstream features (shortwave, surface low 
location, etc) in real time will be instrumental in getting a better 
handling of which solutions may transpire for South Florida. The 
approaching window of Convection Allowing Models (CAMs) may also 
give insight into how things may play out. Specifics aside, ensemble 
guidance remain in line with the latest run of the National Blend of 
Models (NBM) that depict a prolonged period of gusty winds across 
South Florida through much of the upcoming work week. This may act 
to keep temperatures near normal for this time of year, without 
accounting for any potential influence of convection across the 
region. With the surface low potentially remaining in the vicinity 
of South Florida through the middle to late portion of the work 
week, the pressure gradient could very well remain rather stout in 
nature. This may act to keep surface winds gusty across SoFlo with 
high temperatures near average for this time of year.
