.SHORT TERM...
(Today through Friday)
Issued at 1257 AM EST Thu Nov 26 2026

Quieter and a more comfortable weather period is expected to replace 
the wet and muggy weather experienced in the last day or two, just
in time for baking those turkeys. Latest satellite imagery 
identifies the center of low pressure beginning to shift offshore 
and lift northwards, along with a weak cold front arriving from 
the west- northwest that will start to advect drier air into the 
region. This boundary is identified by a strict dew point gradient
near the center of the Peninsula. As this drier air fully advects
across South Florida, dew points will fall into the low 60s for 
most areas later today and simultaneously the upper level pattern 
becomes nearly zonal. With this setup and weaker land to sea 
surface temperature gradients, there will not be enough moisture 
or lift to initiate much shower activity today. While some 
isolated showers could still be seen, they would be highly meager 
and non- impactful other than some brief light rain. 

This pattern is expected to remain largely in tact through Friday 
with northwesterly flow continuing to result in drier air advection 
and a stable atmosphere. High temperatures will primarily reach the 
upper 70s today and mid 70s on Friday.

With this nicer weather, make sure to get those turkeys in the
oven early and have the side dishes prepped so that everyone can
enjoy the outdoors and some football this afternoon and
evening. Happy Thanksgiving!

&&

.LONG TERM...
(Friday night through Wednesday)
Issued at 1257 AM EST Thu Nov 26 2026

A mostly zonal flow pattern with perhaps slight high pressure 
influences at the surface and aloft will last for one more day 
through Saturday despite longwave troughing causing positive 
vorticity advection across the region. Dry air will still dominate 
the local atmosphere with enough stabilization to prevent shower 
development. Dew points are expected to fall into the 50s along with 
cooler daytime highs in the low 70s, leaving little to no momentum 
for any potential convection on Saturday. Beyond Saturday though, 
the pattern will begin to shift as the next low is expected to swing 
across the Gulf as part of a larger longwave trough. Flow will shift 
much more southwesterly as this low advects across the Gulf, which 
will enhance moisture advection back into South Florida. Latest 
guidance suggests PWATs rising above average to the 1.8-2.0" range 
with vorticity advection occurring around the mid-level trough. 

While this setup may not produce steady rainfall Sunday evening 
through the early week period next week, there will be potential for 
bouts of heavier rain and thunderstorms. Uncertainty still exists 
for any potential impacts but we will monitor the situation as the 
dynamics and QPF become more clear in the next few days. The next
frontal boundary is then currently expected to clear out the 
deeper moisture sometime towards the middle of next week, although
uncertainty still is high with that as well.

Temperatures will be in the low to mid 70s on Saturday and then rise 
back towards the upper 70s and low 80s for Sunday and through early 
next week. Overnight lows range from the low to mid 50s for the Lake 
O region to the upper 50s and low 60s near the coasts on Sunday 
morning. Lows then rise into the upper 60s and low 70s for Monday 
morning through mid-week.
