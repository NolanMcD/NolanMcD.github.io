.SHORT TERM...
(Today through Sunday)
Issued at 434 AM EDT Sat Oct 17 2026

A deep upper-level trough progressing eastward across the Eastern US 
will drive a surface frontal boundary toward South Florida today, 
establishing an increasingly unsettled weather pattern across the 
region. The combination of abundant moisture, enhanced ascent, and 
southwesterly steering flow will support the development of numerous 
to showers and thunderstorms across the East Coast. Furthermore, 
vorticity impulses rounding the base of the trough will provide 
additional ascent.  

Widespread rainfall totals of 1-3 inches are forecast along 
the East Coast, with 0.5-1 inch expected elsewhere across South 
Florida. HREF LPMM guidance indicates the potential of locally 
higher amounts of up to 6-8 inches along the East Coast, supported 
by several individual ensemble members. Given the potential for slow-
moving or repeated rounds of convection, pockets of localized 
flooding will be possible, particularly across urban and poor-
drainage areas. The Weather Prediction Center has included most 
of the east coast metro areas in a Slight Risk for Excessive 
Rainfall, highlighting the potential for rainfall rates and 
accumulations to exceed local flash flood guidance, which ranges 
anywhere from 4-6 inches in a 3 to 6 hour period. 

The frontal boundary is expected to stall across the Florida Straits 
through at least Sunday. While PWATs are forecast to decrease on 
Sunday, lingering moisture and the proximity of the boundary will 
maintain elevated rain chances across the Atlantic waters and 
southernmost portions of the peninsula. A tightening pressure 
gradient behind the front will support increasing northeasterly flow 
across the region, which may lead to some frictional convergence 
showers beginning Sunday despite deep moisture beginning to 
decrease. 

High temperatures today will range from the lower to mid 80s near 
Lake Okeechobee to the upper 80s across southern portions of the 
peninsula. Overnight lows will fall into the mid to upper 60s across 
SW Florida and the lower to mid 70s across the East Coast. Cooler 
temperatures are expected Sunday, with highs generally in the lower 
to mid 80s across South Florida.

.LONG TERM...
(Sunday night through Friday)
Issued at 434 AM EDT Sat Oct 17 2026

The frontal boundary remains projected to pass through the area by 
Sunday night while also degrading in the process. This boundary will 
eventually stall over the Florida Straits while diffusing completely 
early this coming week. With moisture levels dropping closer to 
average to below average in some areas, rain chances will lower 
significantly to below 20% for most of interior and SW Florida as 
drier air arrives in the wake of the front. Strong surface high 
pressure will re-develop as the low off the coast of the Carolinas 
begins to advect offshore and shift more towards the Mid-Atlantic 
on, which will create a stout pressure gradient that could last into 
the mid-week time frame. Breezy winds are expected out of the NE 
with wind gusts in the 20-30 mph range possible through Tuesday 
night and potentially a portion of Wednesday. By Wednesday 
afternoon, the pressure gradient should be weakened enough for much 
less breezy conditions. Even with the breezy and gusty winds early 
this week, they should stay below Wind Advisory levels.

The other important aspect of the long term forecast period relates 
to any rain potential through the end of the week. While drier air 
filters in with the passing of the frontal boundary, strong 
northeasterly winds over the local waters will be present in 
combination with slower wind speeds over land, which can result in 
isolated to scattered frictional convergence showers forming along 
the coast. This process is likely to be the main driver in any rain 
shower or storm development through the end of the week, but is not 
expected to produce impactful rainfall or thunderstorms. Instead, 
most of the activity should be of the meager variety. We will 
continue to monitor trends in case a more impactful develops for 
heavy rainfall. High temperatures will fall to the low to mid 80s 
each day in the wake of the front. Overnight lows will range from 
the mid to upper 60s near the lake region to the low to mid 70s 
elsewhere on Monday night, then fall into the 60s across the entire 
region Tuesday night through the end of the week.
