.SHORT TERM...
(Today through Wednesday)
Issued at 200 AM EST Tue Nov 10 2026

Weak mid level ridging will gradually move eastward today in 
response to a fast moving shortwave which will move towards the 
lower Mississippi Valley tonight before moving across the Northern
Gulf coast and into Northern Florida as Wednesday progresses. At 
the surface, high pressure will gradually push into the western 
Atlantic today and it will continue to pull further away from the 
region tonight into Wednesday. While plenty of drier air will 
remain in tact across the mid to upper levels of the atmospheric 
column, lower level moisture will continue to filter in over the 
region as a light east to southeasterly surface wind flow 
continues today. This will help to keep PWATs generally between 
1.4 and 1.6 inches area wide today with most of this moisture 
residing in the lower portion of the column. While most of the 
region will remain dry today, this lower level moisture will be 
just enough to have the potential to spark off some isolated 
shower activity mainly due sea breeze interactions later in the 
afternoon. Any shower that does develop today will be low topped, 
however, a brief heavy downpour cannot be entirely ruled out with
any stronger shower that is able to take advantage of additional 
forcing from mesoscale boundary collisions. High temperatures 
today will generally range from the lower to mid 80s along the 
east coast and Lake Okeechobee region to the upper 80s across 
interior portions of Southwest Florida. 

The aforementioned shortwave that will be pushing across the 
Northern Gulf coast and into Northern Florida on Wednesday will 
stay well off to the north, however, it will help to shift the mid
level wind flow to more of a southwesterly direction. At the same
time, it will help to introduce some cooler air aloft (500mb 
temps of -8 to -10C) over the region which will help to create 
some steeper lapse rates especially up towards the Lake Okeechobee
region which will be closer to the shortwave. As the surface high
pulls further away, winds at the surface will remain rather 
light, however, the synoptic flow will gradually veer to more of a
southerly direction as the day progresses. As deeper layer 
moisture advects into the region, this could create the 
opportunity for higher chances of shower and thunderstorm 
development during the afternoon and evening hours. With a 
southwesterly steering flow in place, afternoon convection will 
favor the interior/Lake Okeechobee region as well as portions of 
the east coast metro areas. The main forcing mechanism for initial
convective development will still be the sea breeze boundary 
collisions. While strong thunderstorm development chances will 
still remain on the lower end, with the colder air aloft and the 
additional instability in place some isolated strong thunderstorm 
activity cannot be ruled out especially where mesoscale boundary 
interactions take place. The stronger storms would be capable of 
producing gusty winds, frequent cloud to ground lightning, and 
heavy downpours. The best chances of stronger thunderstorm 
activity on Wednesday would tend to favor the Lake Okeechobee 
region as that is where the colder temperatures will be aloft 
closer to the actual shortwave. High temperatures on Wednesday 
will generally rise into the lower to mid 80s, however, upper 80s 
are possible over Southwest Florida mainly south of Alligator 
Alley.

(Wednesday night through Monday)
Issued at 200 AM EST Tue Nov 10 2026

The weather pattern starts to change heading into Thursday and

Midwest Wednesday night and then into the Mid-Atlantic and 
Northeast for Thursday and Friday. Mid level troughing will extend 
well down to the south into the Gulf and over the Southeastern 
part of the county as well as the Florida Peninsula during this

before gradually pushing off the New England Coastline Thursday 
night into Friday. The cold front associated with this 
intensifying system will extend southwestward through the 
Southeastern States on Thursday and then across the Florida 
Peninsula Thursday night into Friday.

Out ahead of this frontal boundary, the pressure gradient will 
increase across South Florida on Thursday as winds veer to a 
southwesterly direction. This could create breezy and gusty 
conditions across the area during this time frame. The latest 
guidance trends seems to be leaning towards a faster frontal 
passage across the region which would occur later Thursday night 
into early Friday morning. With guidance trending towards the
faster and more progressive solution, shower activity will be
more scattered in nature out ahead of the front from Thursday 
into early Friday morning. This also would suggest chances for 
thunderstorm development will remain rather low as the best 
dynamics and instability associated with the system will be 
located well off to the northeast of the region. High temperatures
on Thursday will generally range from around 80 across the Lake 
Okeechobee region to the lower to mid 80s further to the south. 

Behind the front, winds will gradually shift and increase out of the 
northwest across the region for the rest of Friday. This will 
allow for strong cold and dry air advection to take place 
especially heading into Friday afternoon as strong high pressure 
begins to build in from the north. This area of high pressure will
keep dry conditions in place through the upcoming weekend and it 
will usher in the coolest airmass of the fall season so far. Cold 
air advection will continue to take place through the first part 
of the weekend before winds start to shift and become more 
northeasterly on Sunday. Before the northeasterly wind shift,  
low temperatures could drop into the 50s across most areas on 
Friday night into Saturday morning, and then again on Saturday 
night into Sunday morning. High temperatures on Saturday may only 
reach the upper 60s along and north of Alligator Alley due to the 
strong cold air advection while areas south of Alligator Alley see
high temperatures in the lower 70s during this time frame. High 
temperatures on Sunday will start to moderate as the winds become 
northeasterly and cold air advection comes to an end.
