.SHORT TERM...
(Today through Monday)
Issued at 140 AM EDT Sun Oct 18 2026

Today is expected to be somewhat of a transitional day as we see the 
weakening frontal boundary move through the area and eventually 
stalling over the Florida Straits. Currently, coastal convergence 
along the Broward and Palm Beach county coasts is supporting ongoing 
development of rain showers that are producing periods of moderate
to heavy rain. This has been enhancing the flood risk in these 
late night hours, but this threat should end once winds both over
the water and land areas become uniform out of the NE/NNE today. 
However, the risk for localized flash flooding will continue until
then as we have seen 3-5" of rain fall in a 1-2 hour period of 
time and more redevelopment continuing to occur. 

The front is expected to push through the area by this afternoon 
with winds veering to the NE both over land and over the local 
waters. With blustery winds and gusts to around 30 mph expected 
consistently today, along with stronger winds over the waters, speed 
convergence could result in some weaker variety shower activity 
across Southeast Florida. Weak mid-level ridging spilling over the 
area from the Gulf should help cap convection to an extent, but we 
also will still have broader upper level troughing in the vicinity 
that can aid in additional convection. Therefore, while PoPs still 
remain high over Southeast Florida (70-80%) we are not expecting 
shower and storm activity today to result in an overall risk for 
flash flooding. Instead, the risk for flash flooding will be highly 
localized. General QPF over Southeast Florida is forecast at 1-2" 
with 90th percentile values (a reasonable worst case) at 2-3". 
Neither of these situations will cause high flooding concerns.

Heading into early this week, drier air will continue to filter into 
the region under the NE flow as PWATs fall to 1.3-1.5" or less 
across the region, which will create a more stable environment that 
leads to mostly dry conditions. A few isolated showers are still 
possible by way of speed/friction convergence, but any convection 
overall will be meager on Monday. 

High temperatures today and tomorrow are generally expected in the 
low to mid 80s. Overnight lows Sunday night are expected in the 
upper 60s to low 70s for Southwest Florida and the low to mid 70s 
across Southeast Florida.

&&

.LONG TERM...
(Monday night through Saturday)
Issued at 140 AM EDT Sun Oct 18 2026

Upper-level troughing will remain anchored across the eastern CONUS 
and western Atlantic through much of the week, while surface high 
pressure builds across the Southeast US. Meanwhile, a strong low-
pressure system is expected to meander off the Eastern Seaboard. The 
resulting tight pressure gradient will support breezy and gusty 
north-northeasterly winds across South Florida through mid-week. 
Most global guidance suites suggest this pressure gradient will 
begin to relax towards the latter half of the week as the offshore 
low-pressure system gradually lifts away, though persistent north-
easterly flow will remain established across South Florida.

In terms of moisture, PWAT values are forecast to dip into the 1.1-
1.3 inch range through mid-week as dry air continues to filter into 
the region. Moisture values will gradually recover toward more 
seasonal levels of 1.4-1.5 inches by late week. With low-level 
convergence maximized along the Atlantic coast, isolated to 
scattered showers and a few embedded thunderstorms could develop 
each day, favoring the East Coast coastal zones, while the rest of 
South Florida should expect mostly dry conditions through mid-week. 

The primary hazards through most of the week will remain marine and 
coastal concerns along the Atlantic beaches, including a high risk 
of rip currents as well as rough surf that could prompt beach 
erosion. Heading into Friday, model guidance hints at a mid/upper-
level disturbance digging southward toward the region, which could 
support an uptick in convective activity and coverage across the 
eastern half of South Florida heading into the weekend. 

Temperatures will trend slightly below climatological averages 
toward the end of the week. Daytime highs will generally start in 
the mid to upper 80s, cooling slightly into the low to mid 80s by 
late week. Overnight lows will range from the lower 70s along the 
East Coast to the mid to upper 60s across the interior and SW 
Florida.
