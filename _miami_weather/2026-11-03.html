.SHORT TERM...
(Today through Wednesday)
Issued at 401 AM EST Tue Nov 3 2026

Model consensus remains consistent in showing a mid-lvl disturbance 
moving across the area to begin gradually dissipating through mid 
week. This is mainly driven by an interaction of the disturbance 
with an approaching northern stream frontal boundary. But model 
soundings still keep abundant moisture in place, with PWATs 
well above normals, still lingering around 2" today and Wednesday. 

Ensembles amd high-res solutions depict a feature resembling a 
cutoff low aloft over the E Gulf providing enough dynamic lifting 
across the state today. Meanwhile, forecast CAPE around 1500 J/Kg, 
along with having better chances at periods of more efficient 
daytime heating during the morning hours. Therefore, expect 
another round of heavy downpours this afternoon and evening with 
widespread Max POPs in the 60-70% range. Particularly vulnerable 
to localized flooding impacts will again be east coast metro 
areas, which were significantly impacted yesterday. 

In terms of severe weather potential, sounding and model data show 
some lingering low-lvl CIN, along with a narrow layer of mid-lvl 
drier air, which may provide some capping to vertical updrafts. This 
may help in preventing explosive development, however, it may favor 
localized, highly efficient convective downpours. Flooding threat 
will be particularly high with consecutive showers and slow-
moving storms, with isolated max accumulations around 5" possible.

Also, coastal hazards continue as high astronomical tides combine 
with persistent onshore winds to keep threat of coastal flooding in 
place today.

.LONG TERM...
(Wednesday night through Monday)
Issued at 401 AM EST Tue Nov 3 2026

Long range models depict a synoptic scenario with a series of 
shortwave troughs aloft passing through the area late this week and 
into the weekend, while some drier air filters into the mid/low 
lvls. Meanwhile, model consensus is increasing regarding the 
potential for a dry frontal passage for the later part of the work 
week. 

There is also a potential shortwave cutting off from the northern 
stream and slowly moving across the E CONUS, which will ultimately 
decide when/if we get the FROPA all the way into SoFlo later this
week. Regardless of the final outcome, models still keep this 
FROPA fairly dry with little indications of significant 
rain/thunderstorm activity associated with it. Winds are also 
expected to diminish as pressure gradients relax in the wake of 
the frontal passage. 

Temperatures will generally remain in the mid to upper 80s,
warmest over southern areas of SoFlo.
