.SHORT TERM...
(Today through Tuesday)
Issued at 407 AM EDT Mon Oct 26 2026

The recent 00z MFL sounding indicates that the lower levels of the 
atmosphere have moistened up quite a bit over the past 24 hours. Low 
level relative humidity is up to 90% and precipitable water 
content has climbed up to 1.81 inches, within the 75 the 
percentile for this time of year. As a result of this increased 
moisture, scattered showers have been peppering the Atlantic 
coastline all evening, and this shower activity should continue 
through the morning and afternoon hours on Monday. Much of this 
activity is courtesy of a mid-level low churning over northern 
Cuba and the Bahamas, providing wide spread forcing and ascent 
across the region. However, mid level water vapor imagery and the 
recent upper air sounding are showing a deep layer of very dry air
above 500 mb. As a result, the dry and stable environment aloft 
has kept most convection very weak and low topped. The threat of 
any heavy rainfall is looking less and less likely under these 
conditions. While several showers will train over the same area 
throughout the day, strong north to northeast flow across all 
layers of the atmosphere will keep rainfall moving at a pretty 
quick pace. Therefore, there is pretty high confidence that 
rainfall amounts will generally be under an inch. Perhaps a few 
localized areas near the coast can see 1 to 2 inches with a bit of
help from the more unstable Gulf Stream waters. A similar story 
will play out for Tuesday, with scattered to numerous showers 
moving in from the Atlantic waters. Chances of precipitation will 
once again be around 75% for the eastern metro areas, with 
activity then dissipating as it moves west.

While an area of surface troughing stretches across the northern 
Caribbean, a sprawling ridge of high pressure extending from the 
Gulf and into the Appalachians will keep a tight pressure gradient 
across the state of Florida. This will keep breezy to gusty winds 
across much of the region for the next couple of days. With 
sustained winds of 20 to 28 mph and gusts up to 40 mph, you will 
feel like a dog sticking it's head out the window of a moving car. A 
Wind Advisory has been issued for the metro areas of Miami-Dade, 
Broward and Palm Beach, through Tuesday evening. Small tree branches 
may be broken and loose objects may be blown about under these 
conditions. 

There is medium to high confidence that tidal gauges will reach 
major flood stage over the next few high tide cycles across the 
coastal Atlantic. This tidal flooding may also be exacerbated by 
coastal showers and strong northeast winds, which could lead to 
significant water rises and inundation of property and roadways 
along eastern metro areas. A Coastal Flood Warning is currently in 
effect for all Atlantic coastal areas through the net couple of 
days. Remember to never drive, walk, or wade across flooded
roadways.

.LONG TERM...
(Tuesday night through Sunday)
Issued at 130 AM EDT Mon Oct 26 2026

A stationary mid-level low trapped underneath this ridge block 
pattern near the Bahamas is expected to finally eject further out 
into the Atlantic by the Wednesday-Thursday time frame, along with 
an upper level ridge expected to rebuild back over the region from 
the Southwestern Gulf and Caribbean. Additionally, several shortwave 
troughs are expected to advect across the mountain west and towards 
the central and eastern U.S., which will help to flatten the high 
pressure currently in place and allow winds to become less breezy by 
Thursday and Friday. Therefore, while scattered showers and 
thunderstorms are still expected each day through the end of the 
week, the weather as a whole should be much less unsettled than it 
currently is right now. Low level wind flow would be expected to 
shift back to a NE/ENE direction and favor inland South Florida for 
the highest rain chances. With the upper level ridge helping 
suppress deep convection from Thursday and into the weekend, QPF 
each day is expected for less than one inch, which results in no 
concerns currently for excessive rainfall through the period. High-
end scenarios and clusters hint at potential for perhaps up to 2-4 
inches of rain in a two day period, which further does not support 
evidence for flooding risk at this current time.

As a result of the information above, the concern to monitor into 
the end of the week is the ongoing King Tide cycle contributing to 
exacerbation of flooding with any rainfall occurring. Given that 
some level of showers and embedded storms are likely each day, the 
King Tide cycles can exacerbate flooding in the vulnerable coastal 
locations. While the peak tides will be during the beginning to 
middle of the week, moderate coastal flooding may persist through 
the end of the week as we come out of this full moon phase.

High temperatures will begin to rebound by a few degrees for the 
long term period, with highs rising back to the mid 80s for 
SE Florida and  upper 80s for SW Florida.
