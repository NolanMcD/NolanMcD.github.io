.SHORT TERM...
(Today through Sunday)
Issued at 114 AM EST Sat Nov 14 2026

We have already begun to feel the arrival of a crisp, continental 
air mass across South Florida early this morning as a cold front, 
currently located just south of the Florida Keys and west of Andros 
Island, has ushered in much drier conditions in its wake with dew 
points having dropped into the 50s across practically all of South 
Florida and into the 60s across the Keys. Looking at the large-scale 
synoptic pattern, a deep and profound trough is churning over the 
eastern U.S. with 500-mb height anomalies near its base falling 
within the 0.5 and minimum climatological percentiles for the date 
according to the NAEFS (combination of 31 GEFS and 21 GEPS 
members). The REFS has these anomalies a tad bit higher within the 
whopping 1st percentile for climatology, but clearly an anomalously 
strong trough that is near or at record intensity for Mid-November 
standards. Over the central U.S. and extending northward into 
Central Canada, an equally vigorous upper-level ridge of high 
pressure with 500-mb geopotential heights lying within the maximum 
climatological percentile for the date according to NAEFS and near 
the 99th percentile as per the REFS has helped to aid the climatical 
augmentation of the downstream trough.

Just off the coast of New England, both the NT (nighttime) 
microphysics ABI (Advanced Baseline Imager) satellite band as well 
as the latest ASCAT 25km descending pass reveal a counterclockwise-
swirling area of surface low pressure within the existing low-level 
cumulus field that has developed beneath bountiful upper-level 
divergence within the left exit region of a screaming jet streak 
that features wind speeds aloft of 140-170 kts, which is above the 
99th percentile on both the REFS and NAEFS CFSR (Climate Forecast 
System Reanalysis) 1979-2009 climatology. With this low pressure 
system is an attendant cold front that can be observed extending 
down along the Eastern Seaboard of the U.S. into north-central 
Florida and stretching into the northern Gulf. This feature is 
difficult to identify via RTMA surface winds, but can be more easily 
pinpointed through a locally tight isodrosothermic gradient that is 
derived by mesoscale surface parameters in and around north-central 
Florida. As previously mentioned, although we have already noticed 
an intrusion of drier, cooler air here in South Florida from the 
passage of our initial strong cold front that is now situated to our 
south, this secondary boundary will gradually work its way down the 
rest of the Florida peninsula and ultimately help bring a 
reinforcing shot of arid, refreshing air from the continent. This is 
supported by high resolution model guidance that illustrates a 
secondary push of lower, 40-degree dew points arriving at the Lake 
Okeechobee region shortly before or around daybreak today and later 
overspreading the rest of South Florida into the afternoon hours.

We will be waking up to temperatures generally in the widespread 50s 
across South Florida today. Probabilities of achieving lows below 50 
degrees to the west of Lake Okeechobee have dropped on both the 
National Blend of Models (NBM) and the LREF from around 30-35% and 
35-40% yesterday to 25-30% and 20-25% today, respectively. High 
temperatures will be up to 10 degrees below average for this time of 
year, only climbing into the mid to upper 60s across Lake Okeechobee 
and interior portions of South Florida and into the low 70s across 
both of our west and east coast metros. Infrared (IR) satellite 
observations this morning insist that although we have little to no 
low-level clouds present across South Florida, we have continued to 
have a recurrence of mid to upper-level clouds as they stream into 
the peninsula from southwest to northeast along our persistently 
strong subtropical jet stream flow aloft. Forecast RAP soundings via 
the SPC mesoanalysis page that go out through the next 6 hours show 
an acute amount of dry air in the vertical column with some upper-
level saturation still associated with the nearby presence of the 
moist subtropical jet. Subsequently proceeding with the operational 
RAP and other mesoscale models such as the HRRR and surprisingly the 
rejuvenated version of the NAM (now recognized as the "WOOF-NAM"), 
it is determined that this immensely dry layer within the first 5 
kilometers of the atmosphere or so will stay in place throughout the 
day Saturday with at least some form of lingering mid to upper-level 
saturation at or around 6 kilometers. With northwesterly winds this 
morning expected to veer generally out of the north at around 5-15 
mph later today (and thus removing any maritime influence of cloud 
advection), we are not expecting many clouds of the "puffy" variety 
(cumulus), but rather of the wispy, feathery kind in the form of 
perhaps alto or cirrus clouds. Nonetheless, plenty of sunshine will 
be expected across South Florida today with Fall in full effect. 
PWATs will be as low as 0.4-0.7" (which are below the 10th 
percentile) regionwide, so to say we are dry is an understatement. 
All in all, we shouldn't even be thinking about rain in the 
slightest. 

A second injection of upper-level dry air on Sunday will look to 
thrust the moist conveyor belt that is the subtropical jet far 
enough to the south to where we will lose this saturation in the mid 
to upper-levels and inversely increase our low-level moisture 
content ever-so-slightly with our surface flow quickly veering off 
the ocean again and becoming east-northeasterly. This will be in 
response to a ridge axis of continental high pressure shifting 
eastward from the Mid to Upper-Missisissippi River Valley today 
closer to the vicinity of the Midwest. We will also see an 
intensification of this surface ridge, with MSLPs around much of the 
Midwest and Great Lakes region rising to values greater than 10 hPa 
above normal as per CFSR 1981-2010 climatology. This places it 
within the 95th and 90-97th percentiles according to the REFS and 
NAEFS, respectively.

This wind shift will not occur, however, until Sunday afternoon, 
meaning that with the continuation of light northerly flow into 
Sunday morning, we should be expecting low temperatures to remain 
chilly for South Florida standards. With this overall flow pattern 
inherently favoring peninsular drainage across South Florida as the 
pre-existing cooler air further north is able to filter down into 
our region more effectively, we are forecasting lows to be a few 
degrees colder around Lake Okeechobee and over Southwest Florida. 
There could be some lingering high cloud cover as per high-res 
mesoscale model guidance that could slightly prevent maximum 
radiational cooling from occurring, but nonetheless we are 
foreseeing Sunday wake-up temperatures to drop into the upper 40s 
west of Lake O and low to mid 50s over the interior and Southwest 
Florida. Our east coast metros will be experiencing lows similar to 
this morning's, in the upper 50s/low 60s. With our aforementioned 
surface wind flow shifting to an east-northeasterly direction for 
Sunday afternoon, our high temperatures, as a result, will begin to 
moderate and climb back to just slightly below average values into 
the mid to upper 70s mainly area-wide to near 80 degrees over the 
southern half of our region. Rightfully excluding the minimal amount 
of low-level moisture that we could achieve on Sunday with this 
onshore wind component, it should be another gorgeous day across 
South Florida with just a few shallow-layer cumulus clouds (yes, the 
puffy kind!) streaming in from the Atlantic throughout the day. 
Goose eggs in the way of PoPs should again be the expectation across 
our region with PWATs slowly climbing back into the 0.7 to 1" range 
but still remaining within the 20th and 30th percentiles for the 
date.

OVERVIEW...

Blustery weather is on tap for South Florida during much of the 
upcoming work week as a collection of atmospheric features set the 
stage for a prolonged period of gusty northeasterly flow. Outside of 
quick moving shower activity and gusty conditions across the land 
areas of South Florida proper, a prolonged duration of dangerous 
marine (Atlantic, Gulf, and Biscayne Bay waters) and beach 
conditions (Palm Beach, Broward, and Miami-Dade counties) could 
materialize across our local waters and coastal portions of South 
Florida for much of the work week.

EARLY WORK WEEK...

The full suite of ensemble and deterministic model guidance remain 
steadfast in depicting a robust mid-level shortwave accelerating 
southeastward from the Southern Plains into the Lower Mississippi 
Valley and eventually across the northern Gulf Coast during the day 
on Monday. The orientation of the sub-tropical jet stream and a 
maxima of upper-level divergence out ahead of the short-wave axis 
continues to be resolved by model guidance in initiating the 
development of an area of surface low pressure across the western 
Gulf waters during this time frame. While this feature is forecast 
to remain well to the west of South Florida on Monday, a 
strengthening pressure gradient (Between the surface low in the 
western Gulf and surface high across the eastern contiguous United 
States) is still being depicted by model guidance to result in gusty 
conditions and gradual moisture advection into the region on Monday. 
While the airmass over South Florida will still be in the process of 
moderating, gusty NErly to ENErly flow will support the threat of 
low-topped shower activity moving onshore along the east coast of 
South Florida during the afternoon hours. Forecasted high 
temperatures will also continue on an gradual upward trajectory with 
forecast high temps in the upper 70s to low 80s.

As the mid-level shortwave slides southeastward across the 
northeastern Gulf waters on Tuesday, the accompanying area of 
surface low pressure is modeled to do so in tandem, moving briskly 
across the central Gulf waters. The overall trend has definitely 
been in a favorable direction (for us here in South Florida) over 
the past 48 hours as the majority of model solutions have remained 
firm in maintaining a more southern trajectory of the mid-level 
shortwave. This has had direct implications in keeping the track of 
the associated surface low and frontal boundaries also further to 
the south of our region. Ensemble guidance (ECMWF, GEFS, and CMCE) 
has also come into better agreement as far as timing and placement 
of the shortwave as variance between 500mb EOF clusters has 
continued to decrease. This southward shift in the track of the 
surface low has resulted in lower forecasted precipitation amounts 
across South Florida as deterministic, ensemble mean, and ensemble 
clusters all now depict the highest precipitation amounts through 
Tuesday night; mainly confined to the eastern Gulf waters and across 
the northwestern Bahamas as opposed to the land areas of South 
Florida. 

MID TO LATE WORK WEEK...

By Wednesday, the axis of the aforementioned mid-level shortwave is 
forecast to elongate and reside across the western Atlantic waters. 
A poleward jet-streak is depicted by model guidance to remain over 
the western Atlantic waters & northwestern Bahamas, with the right 
entrance region (upper level divergence & low level convergence) 
residing in the same general vicinity. What had been relatively good 
agreement in model guidance up until this point, finally splits in 
conceptual thought with different solutions. The GEFS & 
deterministic GFS show the resultant development and deepening of a 
surface low pressure system underneath the jet-streak across the 
northwestern and central Bahamas, with the feature accelerating to 
the northeast during the remainder of the work week. Alternatively, 
the ECWMF & ECENS depict a continuation of surface ridging (no sign 
of surface low development) across the western Atlantic waters with 
periodic surface wind surges out of the northeast. Uncertainty 
remains high during this period as these different model solutions 
vary greatly. The development of a surface low (GFS/GEFS solution) 
to our southeast could usher in a reinforcing surge of continental 
dry air via cyclonic northerly surface flow as it lifts further away 
from the region which may act to preclude any potential of higher 
forecasted rain chances. This will continued to be evaluated as we 
approach the mid to late week period. The modification of the 
airmass in place across South Florida is forecast to continue which 
will result in a continuation of a gradual warming trend across the 
region with maximum temperatures forecast to reach the low to mid 
80s each afternoon during the mid to late week period.
