.SHORT TERM...
(Today through Friday)
Issued at 200 AM EST Thu Nov 12 2026

GOES mid-level water vapor imagery shows a clear illustration early 
this morning of the undulating jet stream pattern we currently have 
in place across the Continental United States. Profound ridging is 
found over the western half of the country while the eastern half is 
mainly dominated by troughing. Over the Mid-Mississippi River Valley 
and Midwest, an upper-level trough is seen digging southeastward 
with its associated surface low pressure system and cold front 
sweeping eastward across the upper Ohio River Valley and towards the 
vicinity of the central Appalachians. This is a crucial atmospheric 
feature whose evolution we will continue to closely monitor for the 
cold air advection it will bring to South Florida in the not-too-
distant future. Closer to home, a minute shortwave trough on water 
vapor imagery is seen exiting the coast of the Southeast U.S., 
supported by SPC mesoanalysis which is showing a broad swath of 50 
to locally 60-knot wind speeds at the 500-mb height level around 
this general region which encompasses most of north and central 
Florida and the offshore Atlantic waters.

As for our local conditions here in South Florida, an abnormally 
strong subtropical jet stream has bolted downstream from the Eastern 
Pacific across Mainland Mexico and the Gulf of America and into the 
Florida peninsula. This can be backtracked by model trends, and its 
arrival aloft here in South Florida can be confirmed by regional 
ACARS soundings which are displaying robust upper-level westerlies 
at around 60-80 knots which, according to our newest rendition of 
the HREF (the REFS), is roughly around the 80th percentile for this 
time of year. With both this subtropical jet and aforementioned 
shortwave disturbance exiting the Southeast U.S. has come the 
advection of moisture into South Florida, which is being accurately 
reflected in both water vapor imagery (with a narrow band of 
moisture enveloping the entire Gulf and the Florida peninsula) and 
ACARS (to a certain extent showing some low to mid-level saturation, 
depending on the exact terminal of choice).

Forecast soundings via mesoscale models (i.e. HRRR and RAP) show 
some more deep-layer saturation occurring through the rest of the 
overnight hours and into the daytime hours Thursday as the main 
corridor of highest moisture associated with the subtropical jet 
shifts slightly southward and focuses directly over South Florida. 
This, also supported by NBM and ensemble guidance, should evidently 
result in mostly cloudy skies across our region today. Moving to the 
surface, a weak cold front can be identified on SPC mesoanalysis off 
the Florida Treasure Coast in association with a thin line of marine 
showers and a sharp confluence of winds along the boundary. This 
weak boundary will be slowing pushing southward through South 
Florida throughout the day Thursday, which (in addition to an 
already moist atmosphere with PWATs of 1.7-1.9" representing the 85-
95th percentiles) could help provide some extra lift to support the 
formation of scattered showers and isolated thunderstorms. With the 
approach of this feeble feature, our surface flow will pick up 
slightly out of the west almost exactly parallel to the orientation 
of the front. In addition, with our background flow fairly 
unidirectional and predominately out of the west-southwest with the 
base of the aforementioned upper-level trough still expected to be 
churning well to our north over the vicinity of the Central 
Appalachians, we should be seeing convection be steered to the east-
northeast throughout the day across South Florida. There will be 
questions as to how much surface heating we could receive through 
the diurnal cycle given the high cloud cover, which will also be 
subsequently affecting our high temperatures that are forecast to be 
slightly cooler than they were yesterday (also aided by the slow and 
subtle passage of this weak front). A clear temperature gradient is 
displayed on the NBM, with places closer to the Lake that will be 
positioned behind the front forecast to peak in the upper 70s while 
the rest of South Florida (preceding and further displaced from the 
surface front) generally reaches the low to mid 80s. Low to mid-
level lapse rates will not be the most ideal in supporting strong to 
severe thunderstorms nor for rapidly kick-starting multiple 
potent updrafts, but given the presence of a cold pocket aloft 
with 500-mb temperatures of around -9C forecast to be between the 
25th and 50th percentiles for the date, the abnormally strong flow
in the upper levels in tandem with the subtropical jet (80-90 
knots of mean shear at the 250-mb level corresponds to the 90-95th
percentiles for the date as per the REFS), and elongated 
hodographs in forecast soundings, cannot completely rule out maybe
some gusty winds translating to the surface from an isolated 
stronger downdraft. Locally heavy rainfall that can potentially 
lead to isolated instances of minor urban flooding will be 
something to monitor, though overall confidence is low. LPMM shows
the possibility for a few localized areas of 1-2" spread out 
across the peninsula through Friday morning. In broader view, PoPs
will range between 20 and 40% across much of South Florida today 
with a slimmer chance of thunder (10-20%).

Thursday night into Friday is when ensemble guidance has a 
significant deepening in our upper-level trough over the Appalachians 
(500-mb geopotential heights around the base of the trough dropping 
to below the 1st percentile for the date as per various ensemble 
clusters including the NAEFS and REFS) which will be abetted 
upstream by a substantial strengthening and amplification of the 
upper-level ridge that was seen positioned over the western U.S. 
this morning as it shifts northeastward towards Canada. Geopotential 
heights at 500mb across Central Canada and the Hudson Bay will be at 
or near maximum climatological percentiles as per the same NAEFS and 
REFS guidance, perfectly complimenting the also near-record heights 
at the base of the downstream trough. At the surface, the same cold 
front mentioned at the beginning of this discussion that was 
traversing the Ohio Valley this morning will continue to quickly 
slide south and eastward through the Southeast U.S. and down the 
Florida peninsula in tandem with a strong surface low that will 
bring nor'easter-like impacts to the northern Mid-Atlantic and New 
England. Unlike the weak cold front that will be crossing South 
Florida today and quickly washing itself out over the southernmost 
portions of our peninsula, this succeeding front is expected to be 
much stronger and will help inject the first chilly air mass of the 
Fall season into South Florida.

We now have a firm timing due to better overall agreement by the 
models on the arrival and passage of this front, as it is forecast 
to descend through South Florida during the pre-dawn and early 
morning hours on Friday. High-res guidance is not overly optimistic 
on the amount of precip that we experience with this FROPA, mainly 
since it will be occurring at night and there will be little to no 
instability for the weak showers that form ahead and along of the 
front to work with. The way it looks now is that these pre-frontal 
showers that we encounter during the Friday early morning hours will 
be no more than just a nuisance-type of rain. PoPs do not appear to 
be any higher than 10% during this nocturnal period and shortly past 
daybreak, decreasing thereafter to near 0% through the rest of the 
day Friday with the front already having moved through our region 
and cooler temperatures having filtered in. Though forecast 
soundings and model projections indicate that our atmospheric 
profile will remain fairly saturated for the first half of the day 
with South Florida still located within the rich conveyor belt of 
moisture that is the subtropical jet (which should again lead to 
more clouds than sun to start off our day), the lack of sufficient 
surface destabilization (CAPE of perhaps only up to 300 J/Kg) due to 
the intrusion of a drier and cooler air mass will likely prevent any 
additional showers from popping up during the peak daytime heating 
hours. We will see more breaks in the cloud cover during the 
afternoon as drier mid-level air starts to slowly filter into South 
Florida on the backside of the potent upper trough, which will also 
help shunt the subtropical jet, and thus our primary moisture 
source, to our south. Winds should start to turn northwesterly 
around daybreak Friday in the wake of the front, gradually becoming 
breezier as the day progresses and especially into the evening hours 
with a modest temperature gradient building over our region.  

The cool air advection behind the front on Friday will lead to our 
high temperatures only climbing into the low to mid 70s for many 
north of Alligator Alley and upper 70s to near 80 degrees south of 
there. Lows both today and tomorrow morning will range between the 
mid 60s (closer to Lake Okeechobee and over the northern half of 
South Florida) and low 70s (further south across the peninsula while 
we await the arrival of that much cooler air by Saturday morning).

THIS WEEKEND...

Anomalously stout mid-level troughing across the eastern contiguous 
United States is forecast to advect northeastward during the early 
morning hours of Saturday. A combined southern & polar stream jet-
streak will also lift northwards across the western Atlantic waters 
in tandem, providing continued kinematic support (maxima of upper 
level divergence & low level convergence) for the continued 
deepening of a surface low pressure system offshore of the 
northeastern United States. The injection of synoptic forcing will 
facilitate the continued progression of the still attached, surface 
frontal boundary further south and away from South Florida, 
gradually ushering in a continental and rather anhydrous airmass 
into the region during this time frame. On the westward periphery of 
the departing mid-level trough, a sprawling dome of surface high 
pressure is forecast to slide southwards across the central United 
States, facilitating the development of a pressure gradient across 
South Florida and our surrounding local waters. The combination of 
the negative phase of the North Atlantic Oscillation (NAO) and 
Arctic Oscillation (AO) could be clearly evident as an airmass that 
initially originated across northern Canada and the Arctic Circle is 
forecast to settle in across the southeastern United States and 
Florida. 

While the coldest and most anomalous 850mb temperatures are forecast 
to remain to the north of our region (forecasted 850mb values are 
below the 5th percentile across the northern extent of the Florida 
Peninsula and the remainder of the southeastern United States), 
Clearing skies and breezy peninsular drainage flow on Saturday 
morning is forecasted to result in wake-up temperatures on Saturday 
in the low 50s near Lake Okeechobee and in the mid to upper 50s 
elsewhere. How does this compare to normal minimum temperatures for 
this time of year? Taking into account spatial variability across 
our local long-term climate sites here in South Florida, the 
currently forecast minimum temperature values would be roughly 5 to 
10 degrees below average. Breezy northerly flow and efficient cold 
air advection (CAA) may then act to stifle diurnal heating on 
Saturday afternoon which could keep high temperatures below average. 
Our normal maximum temperatures for this time of year across South 
Florida are just above a balmy 80F, varying slightly depending on 
exact location. The latest high temperature forecast continues to 
depict values in the upper 60s to low 70s, roughly 10 degrees below 
average for day-time high temperatures. Copious amounts of dry air 
in the vertical column will likely preclude any vertical extent of 
cloud cover with rain chances remaining limited in nature.

With such a transient mid to upper level pattern increasingly in 
play across the southern United States, the axis of surface ridging 
will slide eastwards across the upper Mississippi River valley on 
Saturday night into Sunday morning. The siting of surface ridging is 
instrumental in determining just how long a "cold" snap is able to 
last across our region given the moderating influences of the 
surrounding Atlantic waters. This subtle slide of surface high 
pressure eastward will veer surface winds across the region out of a 
more northerly component prior to daybreak on Sunday morning as the 
pressure gradient gradually wanes in intensity. This could result in 
an interesting distribution of slightly warmer minimum temperature 
values along the east coast of South Florida by daybreak on Sunday, 
while areas across inland southwestern Florida could see values in 
the upper 40s as surface winds slacken and decouple. The veering of 
surface winds to a breezy northeast direction during the day on 
Sunday is forecast to jump-start a gradual warming trend as the 
airmass in place across South Florida gradually moderates. Forecast 
high temperatures will range from the upper 70s across coastal 
locales to temperatures in the mid 70s near Lake Okeechobee. 

NEXT WEEK...

Ensemble and deterministic guidance continue to depict a rather 
robust mid-level shortwave sliding southeastward from the Southern 
Plains into the Lower Mississippi Valley and along the Northern Gulf 
Coast, Monday into Tuesday. A sub-tropical jet-streak to the east of 
the shortwave axis could set the stage for the development of a 
surface low pressure system in the Gulf as the same frontal boundary 
previously discussed, gradually retrogrades northwards as a warm 
front. While this pattern may resemble a "classic El Nino" depiction 
at first glance, there are several important details to unpack. As 
deterministic guidance can vary widely from run to run and depict a 
singular outcome , ensemble guidance can present a wider and more 
probabilistic array of potential solutions. The best analogy to help 
make this important distinction (deterministic vs ensemble) is to 
picture a race car derby. Consider a singular derby car that is 
propelled with force down the derby ramp. One singular input 
produced one singular outcome, which is a really simplistic 
equivalent to deterministic model guidance. To contrast this, now 
imagine a line of derby cars, each built with different materials 
and given varying levels of force down the derby ramp. Each derby 
car had slightly different input parameters which will lead to 
different outcomes, a wider array of potential solutions. Bringing 
the point home, current ECENS and GEFS ensemble members show a very 
wide spread of potential outcomes with this system. Some ensemble 
members depict the threat of thunderstorm activity, heavy rainfall, 
and higher precipitation rainfall totals across South Florida. 
Alternatively, several ensemble members depict the opposite with the 
greatest impacts displaced away from the region. Each ensemble 
member has slightly different inputs on the placement of the frontal 
boundaries and surface low pressure system, which is resulting in a 
wide array of outcomes. This wide array of outcomes also helps us 
infer that there is a considerable amount of uncertainty in the 
evolution of this feature next week and how we here in South Florida 
could be impacted. 

Cluster analysis reveals that ensemble guidance (ECENS, GEFS, and 
CMCE) is in fair agreement with the placement and timing of the 
shortwave, however the exact timing and strength & placement of the 
surface low pressure system & attached frontal boundaries is the 
main mechanism which could either enhance or hinder local impacts 
across the region. Three potential trajectories (Surface low remains 
to the south, Surface low passes directly overhead, Surface low 
passes to the north) for the system exist with consequent forecast 
implications for South Florida. Lift, instability, and moisture are 
the three main ingredients for thunderstorm development and will 
directly be influenced by the exact placement and evolution of the 
surface low. The fourth ingredient that is needed for more robust 
thunderstorm development, shear, will be dictated by where the sub-
tropical jet-stream's jet-streak is located in relation to surface 
features.

Specifics aside, ensemble guidance is in line with the 
latest run of the National Blend of Models (NBM) that depicts the 
potential of a prolonged period of gusty winds across South Florida 
during the early to mid period of next week. This may act to keep 
temperatures near normal for this time of year, without accounting 
for any potential influence of convection across the region.
