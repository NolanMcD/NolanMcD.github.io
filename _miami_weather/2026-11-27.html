.SHORT TERM...
(Today through Saturday)
Issued at 136 AM EST Fri Nov 27 2026

A tranquil weather period will continue to end the week and begin
the weekend. Weak surface high pressure over the Gulf and 
extending over the Florida Peninsula plus a dry air mass will keep
both winds and rain chances on the lighter and drier side 
respectively through Saturday. Latest ACARS soundings show PWATs 
near 0.5" which is below the 10th percentile for this time of 
year. Therefore, despite some vorticity impulses advecting across 
the area beginning today and into Saturday, there will be little 
to no moisture or other support for convection. Overall, expecting
quiet weather to last through Saturday, although we will start to
see an increasing amount of clouds over the weekend. High 
temperatures will trend slightly cooler today with highs in the 
low to mid 70s and overnight lows Friday night in the mid to upper
50s for most areas. 

&&

.LONG TERM...
(Saturday night through Thursday)
Issued at 136 AM EST Fri Nov 27 2026

For the second half of the weekend and into early next week, the 
pattern will begin to shift as the next low is expected to swing 
across the Gulf as part of a larger longwave trough. Flow will shift 
much more southwesterly as this low advects across the Gulf, which 
will enhance moisture advection back into South Florida. Latest 
guidance suggests PWATs rising above average to the 1.8-2.0" range 
with vorticity advection occurring around the mid-level trough into 
the region. However, vertical growth could be tempered by an upper 
level anticyclone centered over the Caribbean that will be 
circulating over South Florida at the same time as the vorticity and 
moisture advection occurs around the base of the mid-level trough.  

As a result of the counteracting features mentioned above, this 
setup may not produce steady rainfall until Tuesday when the 
anticyclone gets forced out of the area by the incoming mid-level 
trough. Thus, at the moment the expectation is for Sunday and Monday 
to experience some scattered shower activity with perhaps an outside 
chance for a thunderstorm, but this would be a highly conditional 
risk given the expected vertical growth capping caused by the upper 
level high. Currently, Tuesday has the best chance for a more 
widespread rainfall day along with periods of moderate to heavy 
rain. Uncertainty still exists for specific impacts, but we will 
continue to monitor. The next frontal boundary is then currently 
expected to clear out the deeper moisture sometime towards the 
middle to late portion of next week, although the strength of this 
front may not result in moisture being fully cleared out. 
Nevertheless, rain chances are expected to drop considerable by by 
the middle to end of next week.

Temperatures will rise back into the upper 70s to low 80s each day 
through the long term period, although some areas may reach the mid 
80s for the middle of next week. Overnight lows also increase back 
up, reaching the upper 60s to low 70s.
