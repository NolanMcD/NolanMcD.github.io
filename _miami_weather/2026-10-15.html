.SHORT TERM...
(Today through Friday)
Issued at 122 AM EDT Thu Oct 15 2026

For the end of this week, surface high pressure will be set up over 
the western Atlantic to end the week along with a mid-level ridge 
propagating from the southeastern US to the offshore western 
Atlantic waters. This will occur in tandem with a synoptic scale 
trough amplifying across the central US. As a result of the mid-
level high shifting further east, low-level and mid-level flow will 
shift southerly/southeasterly and usher in deeper tropical moisture 
(PWATs around 1.8-2 inches or slightly more) and provide ample 
moisture for the production of diurnal showers and thunderstorms. 
With the surface high pressure over the western Atlantic setting up 
an easterly wind regime across the area, the most widespread showers 
and storms should focus across SW Florida as the Gulf breeze gets 
pinned, while some isolated to scattered showers and storms form 
over the east coast metro areas mainly between late morning and 
midday. Some storms may contain stronger wind gusts and periods of 
heavy rainfall, but overall parameters are lacking in terms of 
strong convection potential as well as low flooding risk. However, 
an isolated occurrence of poor drainage urban flooding cannot be 
entirely ruled out. 

Flow will shift a little more southeasterly on Friday as the trough 
over the central US begins to advect towards the northeast US and 
pushes the ridge over the Southeast offshore. This southerly flow 
pattern will support ongoing deep moisture advection, and with low 
level out of a SE/ESE direction, the focus for showers and storms on 
Friday will be across both interior and SW Florida, and perhaps 
stretching into Palm Beach county with PoPs in these areas in the 70-
85% range. Flow will be a bit weaker on Friday compared to Thursday, 
so there will be a higher risk for flooding impacts on Friday. High 
temperatures for the end of this week will be in the upper 80s to 
low 90s. Elevated moisture will keep dew points and corresponding 
heat index values elevated in the 100-105F range as well, posing 
moderate to major HeatRisk across the region as we typically don't 
see this level of heat this late in the season.

.LONG TERM...
(Friday night through Wednesday)
Issued at 122 AM EDT Thu Oct 15 2026

Moving into the weekend, the speed of a propagating frontal boundary 
off of the trough that is swinging across the central and eastern US 
will be a big player in the amount of rainfall that we may see over 
the weekend. Ensembles plus much of the deterministic guidance are 
generally coming together at this point with the front pushing into 
central and perhaps the northernmost portions of the South Florida 
forecast area Saturday evening and overnight into Sunday. The 
steadiest rain would be expected directly ahead of the front, 
leading to the most widespread rainfall likely to occur on Saturday 
before the front pushes out the deeper moisture from portions of the 
region. Potential rainfall amounts are still heavily uncertain, but 
the latest 90th percentile 24-hour QPF is between 2-4" for Saturday 
and lower for Sunday, suggesting that even in a worst case scenario 
we may only need to be concerned for localized flooding. 
Nevertheless, we still need another day or two to get a more 
accurate perception of how much rain is possible in different time 
spans this weekend.

Following the front stalling out late this weekend and into early 
next week, there is better agreement across the ensemble guidance 
that a steep pressure gradient will set up over the entire state of 
Florida, bringing with it a jump in easterly winds. The exact timing 
of the onset of winds here will be tied to the speed and direction 
of the high pressure system building behind the front which is not 
completely resolved yet, but less widespread rain is expected as we 
move into next week and extending into the middle of the week.
