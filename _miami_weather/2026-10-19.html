.SHORT TERM...
(Rest of today through Tuesday)
Issued at 107 PM EDT Mon Oct 19 2026

It is exceptionally dry out there today with respect to atmospheric 
moisture content across South Florida. Current mesoanalysis shows 
PWATs ranging between 1.1 and 1.3", which are within the 20th and 
30th percentiles for the date according to HREF guidance. Both the 
12Z MFL and regional ACARS soundings show an immense dry layer in 
the mid to upper levels in tandem with a distinguishable moist layer 
in the lower levels roughly between the first 1-3 km of the 
atmosphere. Looking at the bigger picture as to why this abundance 
of dry air is in place, a sharp longwave upper trough has continued 
to dig further south into South Florida, allowing dry air advection 
to occur on the upstream side of it. Latest surface analysis map 
from the Weather Prediction Center (WPC) shows an anomalously strong 
ridge of high pressure reading 1035 mb (which remains near the upper 
echelon of climatology for this time of year) still centered well to 
our north around the vicinity of the Allegheny Mountains in 
Pennsylvania. To our south, we continue to have a stalled frontal 
boundary over the Florida Straits and near the proximity of northern 
Cuba, which is where we precisely find the highest moisture content 
(generalized PWATs of 1.5-2.0") with widely scattered showers and 
isolated thunderstorms inhabiting this maritime region.

Despite our atmospheric column being so dry, we are still expected 
to have isolated showers (coverage of 10-20% as per our PoPs) 
develop across South Florida today as they move briskly to the 
southwest along our prevailing northeasterly surface flow (which 
continues to be caused by the tight pressure gradient between the 
record-strong high to our north and the stalled frontal boundary to 
our south). Given the overall layout of the atmospheric profile 
today, these showers should be fairly short-lived and shallow-
layered as they swipe across our peninsula. This light to perhaps 
occasionally moderate precipitation should be able to penetrate far 
enough inland this afternoon to possibly bring some flimsy rainfall 
to the interior around the Lake Okeechobee region and portions of 
the western half of South Florida. Once we lose the component of 
diurnal heating tonight, isolated showers should stay relatively 
confined to the east coast as they push onshore from the Atlantic. 
Shouldn't be more than just some nuisance rain to mist at times with 
no flood threat to be concerned about. Similar to yesterday, it will 
stay breezy across South Florida with winds gusting out of the 
northeast to around 25-35 mph.

Should be a copy and paste forecast for tomorrow, with a plethora of 
dry air staying in place across South Florida but enough low-level 
moisture to support the development of isolated showers, mainly 
across the east coast with PoPs below 20%. Model guidance shows the 
ridge axis over the Allegheny Mountains shifting a bit to the north 
toward the Canadian border tomorrow, which should allow our winds to 
come down just by a sliver, but to no avail with respect to our 
local impacts. With MSLPs (mean sea-level pressure) expected to 
remain well above average for this time of year around the vicinity 
of the Great Lakes and a regional pressure gradient still existing 
across our region, it is forecast to be another breezy day with NBM 
guidance suggesting northeasterly wind gusts of 20-30 mph across 
much of South Florida.

High temperatures will stay nice and comfortable in the low to mid 
80s across South Florida today (which is right around where we 
should be this time of year) and Tuesday due to the breezier-than-
normal conditions and a drier and less humid air mass having 
filtered down the peninsula behind our stalled front to the south. 
It will take another day or two for those across Southeast Florida 
to feel the modest but pleasant drop in low temperatures; but those 
around the Lake, over the interior, and along the Gulf coast will 
already be waking up to temperatures in the mid to upper 60s Tuesday 
morning while the east coast metros will settle for the mid 70s. 
Hang in there, the slight cooldown is coming!
