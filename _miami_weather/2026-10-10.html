.SHORT TERM...
(Today through Sunday)
Issued at 347 AM EDT Sat Oct 10 2026

07z RTMA in tandem with GOES mid-level water vapor imagery reveal 
that South Florida is located in the center of quite the active 
atmospheric pattern in our geographical vicinity today. After making 
landfall in the western Florida Panhandle earlier tonight, the 
capture, elongation, and eventual alignment/absorption of the 
residual vorticity of Isaias and the lobe of mid-level troughing 
that is currently across the southern United States is occurring in 
short order. To the east, mid-level ridging situated in the western 
Atlantic continues to build into the region with 500mb observed 
temperature values on the 00z MFL sounding residing between the 75th-
90th percentile for this time of year. Without getting into more 
specific details about the additional features out there at the 
moment (Hurricane Simon's outflow and the upper level low to our 
south over the Caribbean), let's take a closer look at the more 
important mechanisms behind our forecast for the remainder of the 
weekend. The infusion of baroclinic dynamics and vorticity 
elongation has resulted in Isaias becoming post-tropical in nature, 
with an attendant warm front lifting further away from the Florida 
Peninsula. However, even though we are now far removed from the 
departing boundary, South Florida remains firmly in the warm sector 
of the departing system which will feature breezy to at times gusty 
southerly surface flow and ample moisture advection across South 
Florida today. This continuation of breezy conditions is also in 
large part due to an expansive surface ridge that remains in place 
across the western Atlantic waters, facilitating a pressure gradient 
across the region that will gradually relax tonight into early 
Sunday. With Post Tropical Cyclone Isaias continuing to drift 
northwards away from the region, an attendant frontal boundary is 
forecast to gradually slide eastward across the Gulf today into 
Sunday. 

Continued southerly flow to the east of the frontal boundary will 
continue to usher in deep tropical moisture (precipitable water 
values of 2.0 to 2.2 inches), which will support coastal showers 
along both the Atlantic and Gulf coasts this morning. A more robust 
diurnal round of showers and storms is forecast to develop during 
the afternoon and early evening hours. Diurnal heating may veer 
southerly winds slightly onshore during the afternoon hours as a 
meso low develops onshore in a classic sea-breeze pattern. A high 
moisture, high instability environment will set the stage for 
convection to develop along sea-breeze boundaries before background 
southerly surface flow results in a foci of activity residing across 
the northern half of the region (Palm Beach County and Lake 
Okeechobee). Warm 500mb temperatures, meager low and mid-level lapse 
rates, and mid-level ridging aloft will keep the threat of strong to 
severe convection minimal. However, with such a deep warm cloud 
layer and saturated atmospheric profile, a signal exists in 
HREF/REFS guidance for an isolated swath of higher precipitation (2-
3") values this afternoon. Predictability remains low but mesoscale 
paintball probabilities show the greatest odds of isolated higher 
rainfall totals occurring to the east of Lake Okeechobee over 
portions of Palm Beach County. If 2-3" inches of rainfall occurs 
over an urban area in a short period of time, localized flooding 
could be possible. Outside of the cooling influences of showers and 
storms, it's going to be a warm, breezy, and muggy day across South 
Florida with peak forecasted temperatures in the low 90s and heat 
indices (apparent temperatures) forecast to reach the 100-105F 
range. 

A bit of a more challenging forecast on Sunday as a pocket of drier 
air pushes into South Florida from the east as surface ridging 
continues to enhance. However at the same time, the frontal boundary 
(and deep moisture pooled along it) previously in place across the 
Gulf will also slide southeastward across the peninsula. Uncertainty 
remains high for the exact placement of the frontal boundary as well 
as the timing/arrival of the pocket of drier air moving in across 
the eastern half of the region. Fluctuations in the forecast remain 
possible as the exact timing and placement of these features is not 
set in stone. Regardless, the latest forecast features a gradient of 
lower rain chances across the southeastern half of South Florida on 
Sunday with a maxima in precipitation chances across southwestern 
Florida and west of Lake Okeechobee. Convective parameters once 
again appear lackluster with the main concern remaining isolated 
swaths of 1-3" of rainfall in short periods of time. Temperatures 
will trend lower closer to normal (mid to upper 80s) across the 
northern half of the region, likely due to cloud cover and 
convective initiation. Temperatures across the southern half of the 
region will remain in the low 90s if the drier solution currently 
depicted remains in the cards.
