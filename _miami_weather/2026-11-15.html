.SHORT TERM...
(Today through Monday)
Issued at 241 AM EST Sun Nov 15 2026

South Florida remains entrenched in a cool and dry post-frontal air 
mass as we saw low temperatures yesterday morning drop into the low 
50s around Lake Okeechobee and over the interior and into the upper 
50s/low 60s across our Naples and east coast metros. According to 
WPC's latest surface analysis, a 1027-1028 mb surface ridge of 
continental high pressure continues to be centered a little over 
1,000 miles to our northwest over the Mid to Upper Mississippi River 
Valley, which is currently inducing a light northerly wind down the 
Florida peninsula early this morning. This is typically a setup for 
us here in South Florida that favors a process called "peninsular 
drainage flow", where the prevailing wind direction is nearly 
parallel to our peninsula and ultimately negates any type of warm 
maritime influence that could otherwise prevent our temperatures 
from cooling further. Surface obs over Glades County currently show 
temperature readings in the low 50s with a light and variable wind, 
which could ultimately set the stage for a few locations to 
potentially drop into the upper 40s this morning given not only the 
aforementioned observations, but also the  nearest ACARS sounding 
(KRSW - Fort Myers Southwest Florida Int'l Airport) starting to 
showing the development of a surface inversion and point-soundings 
from mesoscale models also suggesting the same. Despite us 
continuing to have mid to upper-level clouds stream into the region 
this morning from southwest to northeast as seen on CLD (Cloud-Top 
Phase) satellite with the lingering presence of a subtropical jet 
with embedded moisture, it does not appear as if this will have an 
adverse effect on the low-temperature forecast. We should be waking 
up at around daybreak to temperatures in the upper 40s west of Lake 
Okeechobee (maybe even extending slightly southward towards Hendry 
and northern Collier Counties), in the low to mid 50s across 
interior and Southwest Florida, and in the upper 50s/low 60s across 
the east coast metros. Not necessarily falling-iguana criteria, but 
still relatively chilly for South Florida standards!

Regional ACARS and the 00Z MFL soundings both continue to show a 
profound dry slot from the surface up through around 550-600 mb or 
so with higher saturation in the mid to upper-levels of the 
atmosphere due to the aforementioned persisting presence of a moist 
subtropical jet stream, but forecast soundings show this moist layer 
gradually eroding away into this afternoon as drier air begins to 
work its way into South Florida. At the same time, the area of high 
pressure located over the Mississippi Valley will slowly start to 
expand eastward towards the Great Lakes and Ohio River Valley, 
allowing our winds here in South Florida to become northeasterly. 
Limited cloud cover on infrared satellite projections over the 
Midwest; strong radiational cooling with plenty of cold, Arctic air 
still in place; and upper-level convergence provided by the 
downstream flow of the same large upper trough that helped to 
facilitate the cooldown we are currently experiencing here in South 
Florida will all allow surface pressures to increase and for the 
high to strengthen (MSLPs of roughly 1031-1033 mb falling within the 
96th and 90-97th percentiles for the date according to REFS and 
NAEFS ensemble guidance, respectively). At the same time, an 
extremely elongated piece of vorticity associated with a sharp, 
negatively-tilted trough stretching across the Northern and Central 
Rockies will look to splinter in two, with the southernmost fragment 
of vorticity expected to congeal and subsequently take the form of a 
shortwave trough over the Southern Plains (and as a result, aid in 
the development of a surface low just offshore of Galveston Bay in 
Texas, which is supported by deterministic model guidance).

In response to all this, we will see a fortification in our surface 
flow beginning tonight with the NBM showing sustained NE winds of 10-
15 kts over the local waters and along the immediate Atlantic/Gulf 
coasts of South Florida and gusts of 15-20 kts. With our winds 
veering out the northeast in tandem with peak daytime heating, the 
warm Gulf Stream waters should act to moderate our temperatures as 
highs are forecast to generally climb into the mid-upper 70s area-
wide to near 80 degrees in some southernmost locations furthest 
downstream from this flow. Won't be as cool as yesterday, but still 
pleasantly within just a few degrees of average for this time of 
year.

With this northeasterly flow starting to advect some more low-
level moisture into our region that should stay relatively confined 
to the first 3,000-6,000 feet of the atmosphere (and with the 
gradual depletion of the mid to upper-level saturation), we should 
revert back to seeing more of those shallow-layer, fair-weather 
cumulus clouds in the sky as they stream in from the Atlantic 
periodically throughout the day. Most of the period will again 
feature dry conditions with near-zero PoPs, however, guidance is 
starting to hint at the possibility of perhaps a few developing 
showers over our northernmost Atlantic waters by tonight, some of 
which could graze the coast of Palm Beach County downwind of our 
prevailing NE flow past sundown. This is coincident with a modest, 
yet gradual, increase that we will see in our PWATs from 0.7-0.9" 
this morning and afternoon to 0.9-1.1" by this evening. Albeit with 
the latter still being below average at around the 30-40th 
percentile for climatology, it will be just enough to at least start 
pumping out a few, low-topped marine showers. PoPs of 10-15% have 
been introduced, particularly along coastal Palm Beach County, 
tonight into early Monday to address this.

The surface low pressure system that will develop in the 
northwestern Gulf offshore of Texas tonight (as well as its 
attendant parent shortwave trough from which it originated) will 
continue to advance eastward across the northern/central Gulf on 
Monday with an eventual route towards South Florida. Deterministic 
model guidance does not show the associated moisture plume with this 
atmospheric disturbance reaching South Florida until late Monday 
afternoon or early evening, with forecast soundings still showing 
plenty of residual dry air in our atmospheric column minus the first 
kilometer or two due to the lingering onshore flow continuing to 
advect Atlantic moisture into our region. With it now being the 
second day that we will at least have some saturation in the lower 
levels of the atmosphere, it will continue to be possible, similar 
to tonight, for a few showers to make their way onshore primarily 
into Palm Beach or perhaps even northern Broward along our ongoing 
NE flow through Monday afternoon. PoPs of 20-40% will be in place 
across these specified regions, before increasing more notably as we 
cross over into the long term section of this AFD with the moisture 
plume from the aforementioned shortwave and surface low arriving in 
South Florida.

Winds will also start to increase more dramatically on Monday across 
South Florida as the anomalously strong continental high pressure 
previously mentioned in this discussion responsible for our veering 
surface flow shifts eastward once again, now towards the Mid to 
Upper Ohio River Valley, and continues to strengthen with forecast 
MSLPs of 1034-1036 mb falling to below the 2nd percentile for 
November 16th climatology as per ensemble guidance. This, coupled 
with the eastward advancement of our shortwave + surface low duo 
threat across the Northern/Central Gulf, will create a locally tight 
pressure gradient between the two weather features across our 
region. NBM has our NE surface flow increasing to 10-20 mph with 
gusts of up to 25 mph. With continued air mass moderation courtesy 
of our onshore flow, low temperatures on Monday morning across South 
Florida will not be as chilly as previous instances, dropping into 
the mid to upper 50s around the Lake, into the low to mid 60s over 
the interior and across Southwest Florida, and into the upper 60s 
across the east coast metros. Highs will generally climb into the 
upper 70s to low 80s area-wide with a few locations in SW Florida 
closely approaching the mid 80s.

OVERVIEW & IMPACTS...

Forecast confidence has continued to increase in a prolonged 
duration of dangerous marine and beach conditions across South 
Florida and vicinity waters this week. The latest 00z model suite 
now depicts a ramp up of gusty surface winds across the region 
beginning on Monday night with a persistence of these conditions for 
the remainder of the work week. Before we discuss the "why" and how 
model guidance members differ on the atmospheric setup, let's cut 
straight to discussing impacts. Gusty northeasterly surface winds 
could support the threat of life threatening rip currents and surf 
at Miami-Dade, Broward, and Palm Beach beaches, as well as hazardous 
marine conditions for small craft across all local waters of South 
Florida. In addition, forecast model guidance hints at the potential 
of coastal & frictional convergence along the east coast of South 
Florida during the middle to late portion of the work week. Forecast 
model soundings during this time frame show a classic skinny CAPE 
profile, good low to middle level saturation (>80%), and opposing 
1km to 6km wind directions. Given that we remain outside of the 
reliable convection allowing models (CAMs) forecast window and the 
evolution of surface features could still vary, the predictability 
of the likelihood and severity of higher end rainfall maxima's 
remains low. 

While the Weather Prediction Center (WPC)'s latest Quantitative 
Precipitation Forecast (QPF) for the next seven days depicts 
widespread totals of 1.0 to 1.5 inches along the east coast of South 
Florida, a reasonable worst case scenario (one that favors a setup 
of coastal & frictional convergence) could produce several inches of 
rainfall across isolated locations along the east coast of South 
Florida. The Weather Prediction Center has placed portions of the 
east coast of South Florida in a Marginal risk of excessive rainfall 
(level 1 of 4) beginning on Tuesday and continuing through Thursday. 
This means that there is a 5-14% chance of excessive rainfall 
leading to flash flooding within 25 miles of a given point in the 
highlighted areas. Forecasted rainfall totals may shift in value 
over the next couple of days which is standard in a complex pattern, 
so be sure to not anchor to specific range of projected rainfall 
values. The main takeaway is that aside from marine and beach 
impacts, there is at least some level of threat of heavy rainfall 
along the east coast of South Florida during the middle to late 
portion of this week. Stay tuned to the latest forecast from NWS 
Miami as the atmospheric setup discussed in detail (further below)
becomes more clear. 

DETAILED SYNOPSIS...

A robust mid-level shortwave and accompanying sub-tropical jet-
streak is forecast to slowly transit across the eastern Gulf and 
Florida Peninsula during much of the upcoming work week. Both 
deterministic (ECMWF and GFS) as well as ensemble (ECENS & GEFS) have 
trended with a slow progression of this feature across the region, 
keeping upper level forcing and dynamics directly overhead of the 
region. The trend of a slower propagation of the short-wave aside, 
some model members even depict the potential of mid-level vorticity 
becoming cut-off from the larger long-wave pattern. Why does this 
matter? A longer duration of favorable dynamics (cold 500mb 
temperatures, steep low to mid level lapse rates, upper level 
divergence) aloft of our region could set the stage when combined 
with sufficient instability and moisture for periods of both diurnal 
and nocturnal shower and thunderstorm activity along the immediate 
east coast of South Florida for much of the upcoming work week. 
During the mid-week period, a strengthening pressure gradient 
between 1) an expansive ridge of high pressure across much of the 
eastern United States and 2) a frontal boundary to our south over 
the southeastern Gulf and Greater Antilles is forecast to result in 
gusty northeasterly wind flow across all of South Florida. The 
highest wind speeds are projected over the local waters and along 
the immediate coastline of southeastern Florida before surface 
friction acts to reduce wind speeds at the surface further inland. 
This enhancement of onshore winds coupled with the pooling of low 
level boundary moisture across South Florida could set the stage for 
increasing spatial and temporal coverage of showers (and heavy 
rainfall) along the east coast of South Florida.

The implications of friction will also play a role in any threat of 
heavy rainfall during this period that will primarily focus across 
Miami-Dade, Broward, and Palm Beach metro areas. As air slows along 
the coast due to increased friction, ascent is maximized as air 
parcels converge and are forced to rise. Combining this mesoscale 
forcing with a potential gradient of instability along the immediate 
coastline, sufficient boundary layer moisture, and the synoptic 
kinematics (discussed in the totality of this synopsis) could set 
the stage for repeated rounds of showers and thunderstorms across 
our east coast metro locales. With soil moisture values across Palm 
Beach and Broward counties still in the 70-90th percentile, repeated 
rounds of heavy rainfall could result in localized flooding concerns 
and a marginal risk of flash flooding across urban areas. Outside of 
forecast model soundings and QPF cluster analysis, coastal 
convergence is a mesoscale phenomena that is best modeled within the 
near-term time horizon of the CAMs. Speaking of QPF clusters, while 
discrepancies remain between ensemble members, the 90th percentile 
(reasonable worst case scenario) for all clusters suggests a 1 in 10 
potential of several inches of rainfall occurring at isolated 
locations across the east coast of South Florida Wednesday into 
Friday.  

The placement of the right entrance region of a poleward sub-
tropical jet streak over the western Atlantic waters and 
northwestern/central Bahamas will set the stage for an area of 
favorable upper level divergence. Model guidance (ECENS & GEFS) has 
come into better agreement for the gradual development of a surface 
low along a stationary frontal boundary across the Bahamas during 
this time frame as low level convergence is maximized. Model 
solutions now depict a gradual deepening of the surface low pressure 
system with the low gradually lifting to the northeast beginning on 
Thursday and continuing into the upcoming weekend. The development 
of a surface low to our southeast could eventually usher in a 
reinforcing surge of continental dry air via cyclonic northerly 
surface flow as it lifts further away from the region late in the 
work week into the upcoming weekend. The modification of the airmass 
in place across South Florida is forecast to continue which will 
result in a continuation of a gradual warming trend across the 
region with maximum temperatures forecast to reach the low to mid 
80s each afternoon through next weekend.
