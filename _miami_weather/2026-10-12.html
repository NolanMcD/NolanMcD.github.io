.SHORT TERM...
(Today through Tuesday)
Issued at 127 AM EDT Mon Oct 12 2026

A reprieve from humidity, albeit brief, is indeed finally on the way 
for South Florida today as a weak frontal boundary continues to 
slide southward across Central Florida early this morning. For 
the time being however, 05z mesoanalysis reveals that South 
Florida remains firmly in a plume of deep atmospheric moisture 
that has pooled along and south of the boundary. Light winds, 
clearing skies, and the development of a nocturnal near surface 
inversion could set the stage for patches of dense fog across 
inland areas of South Florida early this morning (20-40% HREF 
probabilities of 0.5 mile or less visibility primarily confined to
the south and west of Lake Okeechobee) before diurnal mixing 
shortly after sunrise disperses any near surface stratus cloud 
deck and fog. 

As a progressive mid-level trough pushes offshore of the eastern 
United States this morning, 500mb flow will veer from a southerly 
component to a westerly component by this afternoon. At the same 
time, the enhancement of a surface low offshore (right entrance 
region of a southern stream jet-streak) of the northeastern United 
States will provide enough forcing for the weak frontal boundary 
currently located across Central Florida to slide southward across 
our region during the day today. With all of the best dynamics now 
well removed from the region, convective activity today is 
forecast to be much more sparse and shallow in nature. That being 
said, maximized ascent and residual moisture along the boundary 
could still set the stage for some isolated to scattered shower 
activity. Dry air advection aloft will keep any convection
vertically capped and shallow in nature, which will limit any 
threat of convective hazards. HREF and REFS LPMM guidance depict 
widespread totals remaining less than a quarter of an inch, which 
is also in line with guidance from the Weather Prediction Center. 
High temperatures today will peak in the upper 80s to low 90s 
during the afternoon hours as lower dewpoint values gradually push
southward into the region.

As the boundary slides south of the region tonight into early 
Tuesday, a dry continental airmass will make a long-awaited return
to South Florida. While this will not be a "cold" or even "cool" 
front by any metric, the change in the air will certainly be 
noticeable as we drop from forecasted dewpoints in mid to upper 
70s today to dewpoints in the upper 60s to low 70s on Tuesday. 
Even more remarkable will be the drop in precipitable water 
values, deterministic and ensemble guidance continue to suggest a 
potential of seeing precipitable water values across South Florida
below the 10% percentile for this time of year. While rain 
chances will remain minimal (<10%), overnight mesoscale model 
guidance (HRRR,RRFS,etc) hint at the potential of isolated 
shallow convection (sprinkles/light rain) pushing onshore along 
the east coast on Tuesday afternoon. Even in a rather dry weather
regime, breezy northeasterly flow above the warm Gulfstream 
waters can still produce an isolated coastal sprinkle or two. No 
significant temperature change is expected behind this front 
during the daytime hours (forecasted highs still reach the upper 
80s to low 90s on Tuesday) but a slight drop in overnight 
temperatures is forecast with the potential of low temperatures in
the mid to upper 60s across inland southwestern Florida on 
Tuesday morning. After the past week of rinse and repeat humidity
and deja vu above average overnight temperatures in South 
Florida, the brief overnight temperature dip on Tuesday morning 
may feel "crisp" to some. For this forecaster however, I patiently
await a cold front with a real kick.

.LONG TERM...
(Tuesday night through Sunday)
Issued at 127 AM EDT Mon Oct 12 2026

The advertised reprieve from moisture will be a short lived one as 
the frontolytic boundary and envelope of associated moisture lift 
back across the region during the day on Wednesday into Thursday as 
an expansive area of surface high pressure becomes established 
across the southeastern United States. Northeasterly surface flow 
will gradually veer to an easterly direction by Thursday as the axis 
of surface ridging across the southeastern United States slides 
eastward into the western Atlantic waters. This will focus the best 
diurnal rain chances across the western half of the peninsula during 
the second half of the work week, although overnight and morning 
activity will still be a possibility along the east coast of South 
Florida given the breezy easterly flow and continued moisture 
advection. Mid-level ridging briefly established across the 
southeastern United States during the early to mid portion of the 
work week will quickly flatten by Thursday as the next lobe of 
troughing slides eastward across the northern United States. As the 
positively tilted trough amplifies and digs across the eastern 
United States, a strengthening surface low will strengthen and push 
east-northeastward in tandem. Model guidance remains in agreement 
that a surface frontal boundary attached to the aforementioned 
surface low will push southwards across the eastern United States in 
response and arrive in the vicinity of the Florida Peninsula during 
the upcoming weekend. There has been a great amount of variability/ 
uncertainty in model solutions of exact frontal boundary placement 
(stall, full passage, etc) from run to run and model member to model 
member (ensemble vs deterministic). While forecast details for the 
upcoming weekend will surely become clearer as we progress further 
into the work week, let's briefly delve into what a stall of the 
boundary to the north could transpire for our area as opposed to a 
full frontal passage. A stall of the boundary to the north of South 
Florida would place the region in the envelope of deep tropical 
moisture which could facilitate higher than normal rain chances and 
a continuation of a humid and tropical airmass. The full passage of 
the frontal boundary to the south of the region could usher in 
another push of continental dry air into South Florida and daytime 
forecast temperature near or slightly below average. Cluster 
analysis still depicts variations in model guidance on the 
timing/amplitude of the lobe of troughing across the eastern United 
States during this period, which could have implications on whether 
the boundary will stall or make a full sweep across the region.

Forecast high temperatures will remain status quo through the end of 
the work week with values in the upper 80s to low 90s possible each 
afternoon. The return of moisture advection across the region on 
Wednesday will result in a gradual increase of nocturnal 
temperatures across the region, with a possible return to minimum 
temperatures in the low 80s along the immediate east coast late in
the week.
