.SHORT TERM...
(Rest of today through Tuesday)
Issued at 309 PM EDT Mon Oct 5 2026

An area of low pressure is proceeding eastward across eastern Canada 
early this week. An associated frontal boundary extends from the 
Maritimes south off the east coast of the United States across 
northern Florida into the northern Gulf of Mexico. With the frontal 
boundary moving south, it will serve as a focusing agent for 
abundant tropical moisture which will enable to potential for more 
widespread convective coverage. Another factor will be the 
lightening easterly flow with the surface wind flow becoming more 
southerly to southwesterly. This could enable repeated south to 
north storm motions across portions of South Florida and potentially 
pin any Atlantic sea breeze closer to the coast if the western 
component to the wind flow develops.

The Weather Prediction Center has extended the Day 2 Excessive 
Rainfall Outlook for Tuesday further south to include the Lake 
Okeechobee region and portions of urban Palm Beach and Broward 
Counties. Many portions of the area, particularly portions of metro 
Palm Beach and Broward Counties already have antecedent saturation 
from previous rainfall last week.

&&

.LONG TERM...
(Tuesday night through next Sunday)
Issued at 309 PM EDT Mon Oct 5 2026

As we head into the middle of the week, the area grows in the Day 3 
ERO for Wednesday to include most of South Florida from the Tamiami 
Trail/US 41 corridor northward. The trough that had helped propel 
the frontal boundary south early in the week will eject into the 
Atlantic which will cause the boundary to settle somewhere over the 
central to south central peninsula of Florida. Saturation from 
antecedent rainfall events, slower drainage along the coast due to 
higher than normal high tides with the new moon, and the potential 
for several rounds of excessive rainfall require monitoring of the 
forecast through the week.

This all stands independent of an area in the southwestern Gulf that 
the National Hurricane Center is monitoring. This afternoon's 
Tropical Weather Outlook currently has a 60% chance of development 
through 48 hours and an 80% chance of development through the next 7 
days. It is too early to know the precise impacts from this 
potential disturbance, but several guidance suites allow the frontal 
boundary to remain somewhere around the central peninsula of Florida 
with abundant tropical moisture capable of producing excessive 
rainfall through at least late in the week. 

Atlantic high pressure building in, the potential development, and 
then movement of the disturbance introduce some potential for the 
frontal boundary to retreat northward this weekend but a plume of 
moisture from the Caribbean could follow which could keep the rain 
around a bit longer. There is some consensus on the potential for a 
pocket of drier air to move into South Florida from the east along 
with the high shifting closer to the Bahamas for Sunday. It's still 
too early to take complete stock in the extended forecast, but the 
current trends do appear to remain wet with the potential for a few 
windows of excessive rainfall through the week.
