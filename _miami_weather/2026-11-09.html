.SHORT TERM...
(Today through Tuesday)
Issued at 200 AM EST Mon Nov 9 2026

Weak mid level ridging in the Eastern Gulf will gradually move 
over the region today and into Tuesday. At the surface, high 
pressure centered off to the north will continue to slowly push 
eastward during this time frame. These two features will be the 
ones driving the weather pattern over South Florida through the 
early portion of the week. In general, rather light easterly winds
will prevail across most areas today and Tuesday. This easterly 
wind flow will aid in lower level moisture advection during this
time frame which will cause PWAT values to slowly and gradually 
rise across South Florida. While plenty of dry air will remain in
place across the mid levels, there will be just enough lower 
level moisture in place to support a low end chance of shower 
development each afternoon. Shower chances will be mainly driven 
by the development of the sea breezes each afternoon. Any showers 
that do develop will remain low topped, however, some of the 
stronger shower activity will have the potential to produce brief 
heavy downpours, especially over areas where sea breeze and other 
mesoscale boundary collisions take place. High temperatures today 
and Tuesday will rise into the lower to mid 80s across most areas,
however, upper 80s will be possible across portions of Southwest 
Florida each afternoon mainly along and south of Alligator Alley.

.LONG TERM...
(Tuesday night through next Sunday)
Issued at 316 PM EST Mon Nov 9 2026

While the El Nino Southern Oscillation (ENSO) will be the primary 
mechanism behind the prevailing weather pattern across South Florida 
this winter, the upcoming pattern later this week is a good reminder 
that it is not the only contributor to local climate variability. 
The combination of the negative phase of the Arctic Oscillation (AO) 
and North Atlantic Oscillation (NAO) will result in the advection 
and progression of an amplified trough across the eastern United 
States with below average geopotential height and 500mb temperature 
anomalies gradually shifting from the Ohio River Valley into the Mid-
Atlantic during the second portion of the week. As the mid to upper 
level trough axis slides eastward, efficient cold air advection 
(CAA) will advect a polar airmass (albeit one that was heavily 
modified in transit) into the southeastern United States. This is a 
important distinction from a conventional El Nino setup, which would 
favor the arrival of a Pacific airmass as opposed to an origination 
point in the Arctic circle. The gradual propagation of surface high 
pressure and lessening surface wind speeds is forecast to result in 
a northwesterly flow regime at the surface during the upcoming 
weekend, as a 1020-1025mb surface high gradually slides across the 
southeastern United States. While exact forecasted ambient 
temperature values will certainly fluctuate over the next several 
days, 2m temperature anomalies across South Florida are currently 
forecast to be roughly 10 to 15 degrees below average for this time 
of year during the upcoming weekend. Before we delve further into 
exact details for the upcoming cold snap and get ahead of ourselves, 
let's rewind a bit to the mid-week period. 

By Wednesday, a zonal orientation of the sub-tropical jet stream is 
forecast to orient west to east across the northern Gulf as the axis 
of a negatively tilt trough gradually drops southward across the 
central United States. A shortwave out ahead of the main trough axis 
will remain to the north of South Florida as it quickly propagates 
across the southeastern United States. This will result in a veering 
of 500mb winds to a southwesterly component on Wednesday in tandem 
with cooling 500mb temperatures and steeper mid-level lapse rates. 
At the same time, South Florida is forecast to reside between two 
lobes of surface ridging (one located over the northern Gulf waters 
and one located to our east over the western Atlantic waters), which 
will result in light southerly to southwesterly background flow 
prevailing on Wednesday afternoon. This forecasted flow regime would 
favor inland locales of southeastern Florida and the Lake Okeechobee 
region with a caveat that brisk westerly winds aloft could result in 
convection drifting convection back towards the east coast during 
the late afternoon and early evening hours. Similar to the previous 
discussion, While chances for strong thunderstorm development will 
remain rather low, it cannot be entirely ruled out especially where 
mesoscale boundary collisions take place and maximized ascent 
occurs. The strongest thunderstorm activity could contain heavy 
downpours, frequent cloud to ground lightning, and gusty winds. High 
temperatures on Wednesday will generally rise into the lower to mid 
80s across most areas, however, some upper 80s will be possible 
across portions of Southwest Florida. 

By Thursday, the subtropical and polar jet-streams are forecast to 
phase into a combined jet-streak across the southeastern United 
States. Upper level divergence associated with this dual phasing 
(right entrance region of the jet-streak) will result in the 
development of a surface low pressure system offshore of the Mid-
Atlantic which is forecast to deepen in tandem with the synoptic 
injection of dynamics directly overhead. This strengthening low 
level feature is forecast to accelerate to the north but not before 
dragging an antecedent cold front southwards across the Florida 
Peninsula, Thursday night into Friday. A pressure gradient between 
the frontal boundary as well as a surface area of high pressure will 
result in breezy flow behind the frontal boundary as northwesterly 
surface winds enhance. While slight discrepancies still remain in 
ensemble and deterministic guidance (ECENS, GEFS, GFS, ECMWF), the 
main takeaway is that frontal boundary timing will dictate what 
transpires forecast wise on Friday. A quicker progression of the 
frontal boundary (which the ECMWF and ECENS has hinted at) will 
suppress instability across the region, which could hinder shower 
and thunderstorm development along the boundary as it moves across 
South Florida during the overnight hours. A slower progression may 
result in more diurnal heating out ahead of the boundary which could 
support a greater chance of showers and thunderstorms during the day 
on Friday. 

Cold air advection behind the frontal boundary will result in a 
comfortable and cool weekend across South Florida. Perfect for 
enjoying pumpkin spice beverages and anything cozy. While it remains 
to be seen if the current forecasted temperatures (low 50s across 
coastal southwestern Florida and upper 50s along the east coast) for 
Saturday and Sunday morning verify, the last time minimum 
temperatures below 60F were observed across South Florida was mid to 
late March of this year.
