.SHORT TERM...
(Today through Thursday)
Issued at 201 AM EST Wed Nov 11 2026

A rather fast moving mid level shortwave will push along the 
Northern Gulf Coast today before pushing across Northern Florida and 
out into the western Atlantic tonight. At the surface, high pressure 
will push further to the east in the western Atlantic as a 
developing area of low pressure moves across the Midwest today and 
tonight. This area of low pressure will then rapidly push across 
the Mid-Atlantic states as Thursday progresses before pushing 
offshore into the western Atlantic. 

As the aforementioned mid level shortwave pushes across the Gulf 
coast and towards Northern Florida, it will cause the mid level wind 
flow to veer and become southwesterly today. This should help to 
bring in some deeper layer moisture over the region as the mid 
level drier air that has been in place over the past several days 
begins to erode. At the same time, mid level heights will lower 
throughout the day and 500mb temperatures will become colder. 
These 500mb temperatures will generally range from -8C across the 
southern portion of the peninsula to -10C across the Lake 
Okeechobee region this afternoon causing lapse rates to steepen as
the day progresses. With the surface high shifting further into 
the Atlantic the background surface wind flow will remain light, 
however it will become more south southwesterly as the day 
progresses. As the sea breezes develop, there will be enough 
moisture in place to support scattered shower and thunderstorm 
development as the sea breeze boundaries push inland and interact 
with each other. While the sea breezes will remain the main 
lifting mechanism for convective development this afternoon, the 
colder air aloft provided by the shortwave passing off to the 
north will aid in the potential for an isolated strong 
thunderstorm or two this afternoon. The best chances for stronger 
thunderstorms will be located across the Lake Okeechobee region as
well as Palm Beach County where the colder air aloft and higher 
instability will be located. The stronger storms will be capable 
of producing gusty winds, frequent cloud to ground lightning, and 
even some small hail. High temperatures today will generally rise 
into the lower to mid 80s across most areas, however, upper 80s 
will be possible along and south of Alligator Alley. 

Moving into Thursday, mid level troughing will deepen and amplify 
over the Eastern Seaboard. At the same time, the surface cold front 
associated with the intensifying area of low pressure over the 
Mid-Atlantic states will gradually push across the Southeast and 
down towards the Northern Gulf Coast and Northern Florida. Out 
ahead of this frontal boundary, guidance is hinting at a 
prefrontal surface trough setting up and passing through South 
Florida as Thursday progresses. This will help to create a wind 
shift as winds become more west southwesterly across the region 
during this time frame. This prefrontal trough will also help to 
spark off some scattered showers and isolated thunderstorms as it 
passes through. Since the best dynamics and instability will still
be located well off to the northeast of the region, stronger 
thunderstorm development will be very limited as the prefrontal 
trough pushes through and most of the activity will be in the form
of lower topped showers. High temperatures on Thursday will 
generally range from around 80 across the Lake Okeechobee region 
to the lower 80s across most other areas.

.LONG TERM...
(Thursday night through Tuesday)
Issued at 201 AM EST Wed Nov 11 2026

The actual cold front will approach South Florida on Thursday 
night before pushing through the region on Friday morning. Chances
for showers out ahead of the actual front will be on the lower 
end as the prefrontal trough would have helped to reduce some of 
the available moisture over the region. Once the front passes 
through, strong high pressure will begin to build into the area 
from the northwest. As the pressure gradient tightens, winds will 
increase out of the northwest allowing for strong cold air 
advection to take place beginning on Friday afternoon and lasting 
into the first part of the weekend. This strong cold air advection
will allow for the coolest airmass of the fall season so far to 
move into South Florida during this time frame. Low temperatures 
on Friday night into Saturday morning have the potential to drop 
into the lower 50s across the Lake Okeechobee region and into the 
interior portions of Southwest Florida. Most other areas will see 
low temperatures drop into the mid to upper 50s, while lower 60s 
will remain possible along the immediate east coast. High 
temperatures on Saturday will only rise into the upper 60s across 
the Lake Okeechobee region and they will rise into the lower 70s 
across most other areas. Low temperatures will continue to remain 
cool on Saturday night into Sunday morning, however, they may be 
very slightly warmer as compared to the previous night due to
winds beginning to veer and become north northeasterly.

As high pressure continues to shift eastward dry conditions will 
remain in place for Sunday, however, with winds continuing to veer
and become more northeasterly temperatures will moderate as the 
day progresses. High temperatures on Sunday will generally range 
from mid 70s west of Lake Okeechobee to the upper 70s to around 80
across the rest of the region. 

During the early portion of next week, the latest global and 
ensemble guidance is hinting at the potential for another mid level 
shortwave to dive down from the Southern Plains into the Lower 
Mississippi Valley and along the Northern Gulf Coast heading into 
Monday and Tuesday. This mid level shortwave could cause 
cyclogenesis to take place at the surface along the Northwestern
Gulf coast early Monday before moving eastward across the Gulf 
and towards the Florida Peninsula through the early portion of 
next week. Uncertainty in this part of the forecast remains on the
higher end as this is towards the end of the forecast period and 
the latest guidance suite still remains in disagreement in regards
to the intensity and timing of the surface low development along
with its eventual movement across the Gulf. However, with a 
gradual increase in moisture anticipated out ahead of this system,
chances for showers for thunderstorms could increase across South
Florida during the early week period. The latest forecast takes a
blend of the models and increases the chances of showers and 
thunderstorms during this time frame. This will continue to be 
monitored as the week progresses.
