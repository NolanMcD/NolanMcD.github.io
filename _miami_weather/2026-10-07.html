.SHORT TERM...
(Today through Thursday)
Issued at 220 AM EDT Wed Oct 7 2026

A broad mid level trough extending into the Southeast portion of the 
country and into the northern Gulf today will gradually begin to 
slide into the western Atlantic tonight into Thursday. This trough
will continue to keep mid level ridging to the east of the region
in the Atlantic today, however, the western periphery of this 
ridge will slowly start to build towards South Florida tonight 
into Thursday. At the surface, a weakening frontal boundary will 
remain stalled out just to the north of the region throughout 
today and into a good portion of Thursday. At the same time, 
Tropical Depression Nine will continue to get better organized in 
the Southwestern Gulf as it slowly move east northeastward today 
into Thursday. 

The stalled out frontal boundary to the north will allow for deep
layer moisture to pool over South Florida throughout today as a 
south to southwesterly wind flow sets up across the region during 
this time frame. This will allow for PWAT values to range from 
around 2.1 inches across the extreme southern portion of the 
region to 2.4 inches across the Lake Okeechobee region and Palm 
Beach County which will be closer to the front. With an abundance 
of moisture in place, showers and thunderstorms will gradually 
develop across the region today and they become more widespread 
heading into the afternoon and evening hours. Since the best 
dynamics and instability will be located further up to the 
northeast, chances for strong thunderstorm development will remain
on the lower end, however, they cannot be entirely ruled out 
especially across the Lake Okeechobee region. The primary hazard 
that will be more concerning with convection today will be heavy 
downpours with high rainfall rates, as well as the potential for 
multiple rounds of heavy downpours moving over already saturated 
areas. With the enhanced rainfall rates, it would not be out of 
the question that stronger thunderstorms could drop a quick and 
localized 2-3 inches of rain over a short period of time this 
afternoon and this evening. This type of rainfall could create the
potential for localized flooding, especially across the Lake 
Okeechobee region and east coast metro areas with the steering 
flow remaining light out of the south southwest. 

Similar conditions are anticipated on Thursday across the region as 
the weakening frontal boundary remains stalled out over Central 
Florida for a good portion of the day which will keep the south to 
southwesterly wind flow in place. This will also keep the east 
coast sea breeze pinned over the metro areas along with ample 
deep layer moisture in place through most of the day for higher 
end chances of showers and thunderstorms. The combination of slow
storm motion along with high rainfall rates will allow for 
localized flooding to remain possible especially across the Lake 
Okeechobee region as well as the east coast metro areas on
Thursday. High temperatures will generally remain in the upper 
80s to around 90 across most of the region for both today and 
Thursday.

.LONG TERM...
(Thursday night through Tuesday)
Issued at 220 AM EDT Wed Oct 7 2026

Heading into Friday and the upcoming weekend, mid level ridging over 
the western Atlantic will gradually strengthen as it pushes westward 
and continues to build over South Florida. At the surface, what is 
currently Tropical Depression Nine will continue to strengthen as
it turns and moves northward towards the northern Gulf Coast 
Friday into Saturday. While this system will remain far off to the
northwest and will not have any direct impacts on South Florida, 
it will help to lift the frontal boundary that has been stalled 
out over South Florida northward beginning on Friday. Winds will 
then shift and become southeasterly on Friday, however, they will 
gradually increase heading into Friday night and Saturday as the 
pressure gradient quickly tightens across the region. 

The uncertainty in the forecast rises a bit heading into Friday
and Saturday as the frontal boundary gets lifted off to the north
of the region. Even though this front gets lifted northward, the
enhanced south southeasterly wind flow both at the surface and
aloft could help to drag in some deep layer tropical moisture over
the region from the Carribean Sea. This may keep the chances of 
showers and thunderstorms elevated across the region on Friday and
Saturday. While the general motion of showers and thunderstorms 
will increase during this time frame, the potential for high 
rainfall rates will continue keeping the chances for localized 
flooding in place as well. The timing this moisture increase also 
plays a role into the uncertainty as some of the guidance is a 
bit slower in bringing this area of moisture northward on Friday. 
If this slower scenario were to play out, Friday may end up being 
more of a typical late rainy season type day with higher chances 
of showers and storms being focused over the interior and 
Southwest Florida instead of the entire area. In any event, the 
main takeaway here is that even though the frontal boundary will 
be dragged further to the north, the wet and unsettled pattern may
last into the first part of the weekend due to the increase in 
deep layer tropical moisture trying to push over the region. This 
will continue to be monitored as the week progresses.

Heading into the second half of the weekend and into early next 
week, the guidance remains in relatively good agreement with 
showing strong mid level ridging expanding over the Florida 
Peninsula and into the Gulf during this time frame. This will 
allow for drier conditions to push into the area which will result
in a return to more of a typical late wet season type pattern 
across the region as winds begin to decrease and become east 
southeasterly. Chances of showers and thunderstorms will still 
remain in place, however, they will become more scattered in 
nature and they will favor the interior and Southwest Florida 
during the afternoon and evening hours. High temperatures during 
the second half of the weekend and into the early next week could
be above climatological normals due to the strengthening mid 
level ridge building into the region.
