.SHORT TERM...
(Today through Sunday)
Issued at 323 AM EST Sat Nov 28 2026

The synoptic pattern remains largely unchanged today, with South 
Florida situated between ridging over the western Atlantic and 
longwave troughing across the eastern CONUS. At the surface, high 
pressure centered over the Gulf continues to build eastward across 
the region. Mid/upper-level cloud cover continues to stream across 
the peninsula along the subtropical jet, however, a notably dry 
airmass remains in place. The latest MFL sounding and recent ACARS 
data indicate PWAT values between 0.6-0.8 inches, around the 10th 
percentile for late November, with a relatively dry near-surface 
layer and a pronounced dry layer aloft between 500 and 850mb.
Given limited moisture and lack of appreciable large-scale or 
mesoscale forcing, dry conditions are expected to prevail across 
South Florida once again today. 

A gradual pattern shift is expected heading into Sunday as a mid-
level disturbance ejects from the Plains toward the southeast CONUS. 
The associated surface frontal boundary will push the surface high 
pressure ridge axis eastward across Florida and into the western 
Atlantic. Moisture will begin to pool across South Florida ahead of 
the front as low-level winds gradually veer easterly to 
southeasterly, with PWAT values expected to increase to around 1.8-
2.0 inches across the region late Sunday. Easterly flow across the 
Gulf Stream waters may support the development of scattered showers 
and a few thunderstorms along the East Coast on Sunday afternoon 
into the evening. Additional isolated to scattered showers 
associated with the approaching surface front may develop near the
Lake region on Sunday night.

Temperatures will remain below seasonal averages today, with 
afternoon highs generally reaching the low to mid 70s across the 
region. Overnight lows will fall into the upper 50s to lower 60s 
across the interior and Southwest Florida, with mid 60s expected 
along the East Coast. Increasing warm air advection will allow 
temperatures to rebound closer to seasonal norms by the end of the 
weekend, with afternoon highs reaching the upper 70s to lower 80s 
on Sunday.

.LONG TERM...
(Sunday night through Friday)
Issued at 323 AM EST Sat Nov 28 2026

A low is expected to swing across the Gulf for the early 
week period next week, which will usher in deeper moisture under a 
southwesterly flow. Guidance still suggests PWATs rising above 
average to the 1.8-2.0" range with vorticity advection occurring 
around the mid-level trough into the region. However, vertical 
growth could be tempered by an upper level anticyclone centered over 
the Caribbean near Cuba that will be circulating over South Florida 
at the same time as the vorticity and moisture advection occurs 
around the base of the mid-level trough.  

As a result of the counteracting features mentioned above, this 
setup may not produce steady rainfall until Tuesday when the 
anticyclone gets forced out of the area by the incoming mid-level 
trough and an upper level jet streak helps increase upper level 
divergence. Thus, at the moment the expectation is for Monday to 
experience some scattered shower activity with perhaps an outside 
chance for a thunderstorm, but this would be a highly conditional 
risk given the expected vertical growth capping caused by the upper 
level high. Currently, Tuesday has the best chance for a more 
widespread rainfall day along with periods of moderate to heavy 
rain. The next frontal boundary is then currently expected to clear 
out the deeper moisture sometime towards the Wed-Thu time frame, 
although the strength of this front may not result in moisture being 
fully cleared out as the front washes out in its approach. 
Nevertheless, rain chances are expected to drop considerably by the 
middle to end of next week as this front pushes through and upper 
level ridging begins to rebuild in strength across the eastern US.

Temperatures will continue to increase slightly each day through the 
long term period, averaging in the low to mid 80s. Parts of SW 
Florida may reach the upper 80s as the developing ridge and easterly 
results in warmer temps in that region. Overnight lows also 
increase, reaching the upper 60s to low 70s.
