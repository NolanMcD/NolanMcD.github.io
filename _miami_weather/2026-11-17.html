.SHORT TERM...
(Today through Wednesday)
Issued at 249 AM EST Tue Nov 17 2026

At the surface, high pressure continues to dominate across the 
eastern seaboard. A weak area of low pressure in the Gulf remains 
the dominant synoptic feature in Florida's unsettled weather pattern 
over the coming days with a mid/upper level shortwave pushing across 
the Gulf coast states through the week. A stationary boundary 
associated with the surface low will remain draped across central 
Florida today, which will begin to pool moisture across the 
peninsula. PWAT values rise into the 1.6-1.7" range which is between 
the 80th-90th percentile for this point in November. As the area of 
low pressure lingers across Gulf waters through the next few days, a 
tight surface pressure gradient will lead to breezy east-northeast 
winds across the region today and tomorrow - especially near the 
immediate east coast where gusts up to 30 mph will be possible. With 
this setup, we're looking at a multi-hazard event through the week 
with 1) an increased threat of urban flooding across the east coast 
2) dangerous marine conditions for small craft and 3) life-
threatening rip currents and high surf at east coast beaches. 

Breezy onshore winds at the surface with strong westerly winds aloft 
create enhanced coastal convergence, where areas along the immediate 
east coast may receive repeated rounds of relentless heavy rainfall 
as ascent is maximized due to low level convergence. At this time, 
the region remains under a Slight (level 2 of 4) risk of Excessive 
Rainfall from the Weather prediction center today and tomorrow. If 
repeated rounds of heavy rainfall occur, impactful urban flash 
flooding will be possible. At this time, most likely widespread 
rainfall totals of 2-4" through Friday are possible along the east 
coast, with a reasonable worst case forecast of 6-10" (10% chance of 
higher totals than these). The Flood Watch has been expanded across 
interior Palm Beach County and Broward and Miami-Dade this morning. 
Although impactful flash flooding will be possible anywhere along 
the east coast, the highest totals are currently forecast across 
coastal Palm Beach and Broward counties as they are closer to the 
stationary boundary. There is always inherent uncertainty in 
these events on where the highest rainfall totals will fall as 
coastal convergence is a complicated process occurring on smaller 
scales that models don't always effectively resolve. 

Not much change in the synoptic setup for Wednesday as the surface 
low will continue to linger in the Gulf, leaving its stationary 
boundary draped across central Florida. PWATs will continue to 
increase through Wednesday with some models showing maximum values 
between 1.8 and even 1.9" by Wednesday night. This is well above the 
90th percentile for this time of year.

low entering the western Atlantic as high pressure breaks down and a

from the north. PWATs drop between 1-1.3" by the end of the weekend
