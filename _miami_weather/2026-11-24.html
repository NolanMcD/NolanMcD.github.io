.SHORT TERM...
(Today through Wednesday)
Issued at 346 AM EST Tue Nov 24 2026

The weak surface ridge that we were under yesterday will break down 
over the day today as a weak forerunning shortwave sneaks through 
our area. There is uncertainty on the effects this afternoon as 
ensemble guidance does not agree of shear profile that will take 
shape. The European(ENS) and American(GEFS) global ensembles are 
favoring a profile conducive for afternoon coastal convergence 
while the high-res ensemble (REFS) is not so certain that low 
level winds will turn east enough to match. The key here looks to 
be the strength of the sea breeze this afternoon as it works its 
way inland. A stronger sea breeze would favor coastal convergence
storm formation but surface heating is going to regulate this 
potential. If these factors align there will plenty of 
precipitable water (above the 90th percentile for this time of 
year) for coastal storms to drop 1-2 inches. Early indication this
morning points to mid- level cloud cover which could work to 
suppress the required surface heating to really get the coastal 
convergence setup to pop, but we'll continue to monitor how this 
evolves over the day. 

The star of the show this week is due to appear sometime early 
Wednesday as a shortwave laden trough swings over our area. Little 
has changed from yesterday and uncertainty in the track and speed of 
the low pressure system remains high. An interesting discrepancy to 
highlight is between the high-res ensembles and the global 
ensembles, with the high-res ensemble (REFS) favoring a more 
southerly track of the low pressure center while the global ensemble 
(LREF) favors a more northerly one. This northerly solution 
increases the severe threat for South Florida due to increased 
moisture advection and a more optimal shear profile for severe storm 
formation. The other split among the models is with the overall 
speed of the northeast moving system. The European(ENS) and 
Canadian(GEPS) ensembles favor a slower track over South Florida 
which would increase not only the time in the warm sector, but also 
the risk for higher precipitation amounts and severe storm 
formation. The American ensemble (GEFS) favors a faster track with 
overall less precipitation than the other two. Mean rainfall values 
for Wednesday still sit around the 2 inch mark but localized amounts 
above 4+ inches are possible. We will continue to refine these 
values as guidance comes in. 

The impacts of the increased rainfall Wednesday would coincide with 
a return of moderate flooding thresholds due to the return of the 
king tide so partners at risk of coastal flooding should 
remain vigilant in monitoring the development of this system.

.LONG TERM...
(Wednesday night through Monday)
Issued at 346 AM EST Tue Nov 24 2026

The frontal boundary and shortwave axis will move east of the area 
on Thursday. Residual moisture will keep the chance of showers in 
the forecast through at least the first half of the day. High 
pressure and much drier air will filter into the region for Friday 
and Saturday. Attention then turns to a cut off low over the eastern 
Pacific on Thursday which will cut across Mexico on Friday and 
Saturday and enter the Gulf late Saturday into early Sunday. This 
feature will eventually get absorbed into the larger upper level 
trough moving eastward across the country. There are some timing 
differences between the global models, but rain chances should 
return to South FL on Sunday or Monday next week. 

Below normal temps are expected Friday and Saturday before 
rebounding back to near normal Sunday and early next week as the 
next system arrives.
