.SHORT TERM...
(Today through Friday)
Issued at 220 AM EDT Thu Oct 8 2026

Mid level troughing will continue to pull northeastward away from 
the region into the Atlantic today as a strengthening mid level 
ridge centered to the northeast of the Bahamas begins to shift 
westward towards South Florida. As this ridge continues to try to 
build westward over the Florida Peninsula, it will act as a key 
feature in steering Hurricane Isaias more northward tonight into 
Friday towards the northern Gulf Coast. At the surface, a weak 
frontal boundary remains stalled out over Central Florida today, 
however, as Hurricane Isaias begins to turn northward tonight, it 
will also play a role in lifting the frontal boundary back up to 
the north further away from the region. 

A light south to southwesterly wind flow will remain in place across 
South Florida today, however, as the front begins to move northward, 
winds will gradually become southeast tonight and then increase in 
speed on Friday as the pressure gradient tightens over the area. 
Deep tropical moisture will remain in place across most of the 
region for a good portion of the day which will allow for scattered 
to numerous showers and thunderstorms to develop once again across 
most of the region today. With the front once again acting as an 
extra source of lift in combination with sea breeze development, the 
highest chances of showers and thunderstorms will remain over the 
east coast metro areas as well as locations along and north of 
Alligator Alley this afternoon into this evening. Storm motion 
will continue to remain slow today and rainfall rates will remain 
high due to the abundance of moisture in place. With the stronger 
storms having the potential for higher rainfall rates, it would 
remain possible that a quick and localized 2 to 4 inches of rain 
could fall over a short period of time this afternoon into this 
evening. This will allow for the potential of localized flooding 
to continue with the highest chances occurring over the urban and
poor drainage areas as well as locations already saturated from 
recent heavy rainfall.

As ridging continues to build into the region on Friday, while the 
surface level wind flow will increase out of the southeast, mid 
level winds will remain southerly which will continue to draw in 
moisture as PWAT values remain between 2.0 and 2.2 inches area wide. 
With the frontal boundary having less of an influence on lift and 
instability since it will be pushing north of the region, convective 
development will be more sea breeze driven. As southeasterly 
winds increase, this will keep the higher chances of showers and 
thunderstorms focused over the interior and Southwest Florida 
during Friday afternoon into Friday evening. 

High temperatures today will generally rise into the upper 80s to
around 90 across most of the region. High temperatures on Friday 
will rise to around 90 along the east coast, and into the lower 
90s across Southwest Florida.

.LONG TERM...
(Friday night through Wednesday)
Issued at 220 AM EDT Thu Oct 8 2026

Moving into the upcoming weekend, Isaias will make landfall along 
the northern Gulf Coast on Friday night and then move into the Deep 
South heading into Saturday. At the same time, mid level ridging in 
the western Atlantic will strengthen as it gradually pushes over the 
Florida Peninsula and ultimately into the eastern Gulf as the 
weekend progresses. The pressure gradient over the region will 
still remain tight at the surface during the first part of the 
weekend due to Isaias to the northwest, and a strengthening large 
area of high pressure in the Atlantic. This will keep a breezy and
gusty southeasterly wind flow in place at the surface and a more 
southerly wind flow aloft. This type of set up will continue to 
advect deep tropical moisture into the region from the Carribean 
as the latest global and ensemble guidance shows PWAT values 
rising and ranging between 2.2 and 2.4 inches across the area on 
Saturday. This will keep the wet and unsettled pattern in place 
during this time frame with higher chances of showers and 
thunderstorms across most of the region. While storm motion will 
be much faster than it has been over the past several days, the 
deep tropical moisture will continue to allow for the possibility 
of very efficient rainfall rates with the stronger storms. This 
will keep the possibility of localized flooding in place across 
the urban and poor drainage areas as well as locations that are 
already saturated from recent heavy rainfall. 

During the second half of the weekend the latest global and ensemble 
guidance suite continues to suggest that mid level ridging will 
center over the Florida Peninsula on Sunday before shifting westward 
into the Gulf on Monday while quickly flattening over South 
Florida. This flattening and westward shift will be caused by the 
remnants of Isaias pushing across the Mid-Atlantic States and 
into the western Atlantic well off to the north on Monday. At the 
surface, high pressure will build over the region on Sunday before
starting to shift into the Gulf on Monday. This will allow for 
the pressure gradient to decrease across the area and 
southeasterly winds will gradually decrease during this time 
frame. At the same time, deeper layer subsidence will bring some 
drier air back into the region which will bring a typical late wet
season type pattern back into the region for Sunday and maybe 
even lower chances of showers and storms heading into Monday. High
temperatures on Sunday and Monday will generally range from 
around 90 along the east coast to the lower 90s across interior 
portions of Southwest Florida. 

Heading into Tuesday and Wednesday, the forecast uncertainty does 
increase a little bit as it is towards the end of the forecast 
period, but the latest global and ensemble guidance is hinting at 
the potential for a weakening front to approach and push through 
the area sometime towards the middle of the week. This would 
significantly decrease rain chances behind the front as the 
beginning of the dry season approaches. This will continue to be 
monitored as the week progresses.
