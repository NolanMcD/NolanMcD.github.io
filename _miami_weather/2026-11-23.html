.SHORT TERM...
(Today through Tuesday)
Issued at 215 AM EST Mon Nov 23 2026

Weak high pressure over the northeastern Gulf will be in control 
today, with fairly zonal flow aloft. OOZ sounding showed PWAT values 
of 1.1 inches and plenty of dry air in the mid and upper levels. Dry 
air will remain in place today with a light north to northwesterly 
flow across the peninsula. Some patchy fog is possible early this 
morning over the interior with light winds and mostly clear skies. 
As the day progresses, partly to mostly cloudy skies are expected. 
High temps will be a bit cooler today ranging from the upper 70s to 
lower 80s. Comfortable overnight temps with lows tonight in the 60s. 

On Tuesday our attention will turn to the next system that will 
eventually impact the area for the mid week period. A shortwave will 
move out of the SW US and advance towards the western Gulf by late 
in the day. Meanwhile a slow moving frontal boundary will approach 
northern FL by late in the day. Low level flow will turn to more of 
a northeasterly direction which will pull some low level moisture in 
from the Atlantic. While most of the day will remain dry, some 
scattered coastal showers are possible during the second half of the 
day. High temps on Tuesday will again be in the upper 70s to lower 
80s.

.LONG TERM...
(Tuesday night through Sunday)
Issued at 215 AM EST Mon Nov 23 2026

Looking forward to the mid-week there is a low-pressure system that 
is forecast to impact our area Wednesday. Global model ensemble 
guidance is in good agreement that an extended area of mid-level 
troughing will move off the Rockies Tuesday night into Wednesday 
morning. Within the trough an interaction of two short waves leads 
to a negatively oriented trough axis running through the Southeast 
and down the Florida Peninsula. There is also good agreement between 
the members that a surface low will develop in the Gulf waters 
somewhere west of Florida, but this is where we begin to see 
divergence among ensemble guidance. Preceding this setup, the 
ensemble mean for precipitable water will approach 2 inches which 
is well above the 90th percentile for this time of year and would 
put it in the ballpark of the maximum observed values. From the 
Storm Prediction Center (SPC) ensemble cluster analysis, two 
general scenarios emerge for South Florida.

One of these scenarios has the mid-level trough swing over Florida 
quickly and the developing surface low does not reach its deepest 
point until after it has moved off to the east of South Florida. 
These scenarios have lower precipitation totals than the slower 
solutions but depending on the strength of the shortwave, this setup 
could produce a better shear profile for severe storms. A more 
northerly track of the surface low would further increases this 
potential as the optimal sector of the storm for severe storm 
development would move closer to South Florida. These faster 
solutions bring a frontal boundary over the area by early Thursday 
morning.

Another of these scenarios points to a higher rainfall solution for 
South Florida on Wednesday due to two different reasons. Either a 
slower propagating surface low, or a slightly faster propagating low 
that forms further to the south west near the Yucatan Channel. Both 
of these scenarios give more time for the surface low to mature 
before reaching our area and allow for more moisture advection ahead 
of the system. It is these scenarios that produce the highest 
precipitable water values for Wednesday and point to the elevated 
rainfall totals compared to the faster moving storm scenarios. Early 
indications have rainfall amounts of 2-4 inches for the eastern 
metros with the possibility of localized areas receiving higher 
amounts. 

The impacts of the increased rainfall Wednesday would coincide with 
a return of moderate flooding thresholds due to the return of the 
king tide so partners at risk of coastal flooding should monitor the 
development of this system over the next two days. Following the 
low, a frontal boundary will bring drier air over South Florida no 
later than Thursday night and dew points will fall to the mid 50s as 
we move into the weekend.
