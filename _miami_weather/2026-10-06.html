For the North Atlantic...Caribbean Sea and the Gulf of America:

Southwestern Gulf of America (AL92):
Showers and thunderstorms are showing signs of organization in 
association with a broad area of low pressure located over the 
southwestern Gulf of America. Continued development of this system 
is expected, and a tropical depression is anticipated to form over

monitor the progress of this system. The NOAA Gulfstream is 
scheduled to sample the environment over the Gulf later today and an

system tomorrow.

.SHORT TERM...
(Today through Wednesday)
Issued at 220 AM EDT Tue Oct 6 2026

Rather broad mid level troughing will remain in place across the 
Southeast as well as most of the Florida Peninsula today into 
Wednesday. This will cause mid level ridging to be shunted off 
into the western Atlantic during this time frame. At the surface, 
a weakening frontal boundary will continue to slowly push 
southeastward across the northern Gulf Coast and into Northern 
Florida today. This front will stall out somewhere over Central 
Florida heading into Wednesday. Surface level winds will remain 
rather light and sea breeze driven today, however, as the front 
pushes closer to the region on Wednesday, a deeper south to 
southwesterly wind flow will set up not only at the surface, but 
aloft as well. This wind profile will bring deep tropical moisture
into the region throughout the atmospheric column as PWATs rise 
and generally range between 2.2 and 2.4 inches across a good 
portion of South Florida through the middle of the week. 

While shower and thunderstorm activity will be more sea breeze 
driven today and more scattered in coverage, as the front pushes 
closer to the region on Wednesday before stalling out just to the 
north, convective coverage will become more widespread across 
South Florida. Due to the south to southwesterly wind flow 
setting up across the region, the east coast sea breeze will 
remain pinned over the metro areas today, and even moreso on 
Wednesday. This in combination with the abundance of moisture in 
place will allow for the potential for multiple rounds of heavy 
downpours to take place over the east coast metro areas as well as
the Lake Okeechobee region during this time frame. With the east 
coast metro areas already saturated from previous heavy rainfall, 
any additional heavy rain will add to the increased potential of 
localized flooding this afternoon through Wednesday.

High temperatures today will generally rise into the upper 80s and 
lower 90s across most of the region. Temperatures on Wednesday may 
may be a few degrees cooler due to an increase in cloud cover and
more widespread rainfall over South Florida.

.LONG TERM...
(Wednesday night through Monday)
Issued at 220 AM EDT Tue Oct 6 2026

Moving into the second half of the week, the broad mid level trough 
will remain nearby on Thursday, however, mid level ridging in the 
western Atlantic will start to build closer to South Florida heading 
into Friday. At the surface, the stalled out frontal boundary will 
linger close to the region over Central Florida before slowly trying 
to lift back northward heading into Friday. This would be in 
response to a broad area of low pressure trying to develop in the 
Southwestern Gulf and the Bay of Campeche. The National Hurricane 
Center gives this broad area of low pressure a high chance (80%) 
of development over the next 48 hours as it slowly drifts east 
northeastward during this time frame. With the front remaining 
close by on Thursday, deep tropical moisture will still continue 
to advect into the region, keeping enhanced rainfall chances in 
place over most of the area. The potential for localized flooding
will also remain in place especially along and north of Alligator
alley which will be closer to the front during this time frame. 

The forecast heading into Friday and the first part of the weekend 
still remains uncertain as the broad area of low pressure 
currently in the southwestern Gulf will ultimately play a role in 
lifting the stalled frontal boundary further north of the region. 
The latest global and ensemble guidance suite remain in 
disagreement in regards to timing and intensity of this area of 
low pressure as it gradually pulls northward towards the central 
Gulf Coast states for the end of the week and into the upcoming 
weekend. If the low is slower to move north northeastward, the 
front could remain closer to the region drawing in additional 
deep layer moisture for showers and thunderstorms to develop on 
Friday and Saturday. In addition to this, the developing system 
could draw up some more moisture from the Carribean into South 
Florida during this time frame. With all of these variables in 
place for Friday and Saturday, the latest forecast keeps higher 
end chances of showers and thunderstorms over the region during 
this time frame. High temperatures for Friday and Saturday will 
rise into the upper 80s to around 90 across most of the region. 

Guidance does show some signals of a pattern change heading towards 
the second half of the weekend and into early next week as mid level 
ridging strengthens over the western Atlantic and tries to build 
over the Florida Peninsula. This could introduce some drier air
into the region during this time frame bringing the chances of 
showers and thunderstorms back to a typical late wet season type 
pattern with increased southeasterly wind flow. The latest 
forecast reflects this and keeps the higher chances of showers 
and thunderstorms confined to interior locations as well as 
Southwestern Florida. High temperatures look to be near or 
slightly above climatological normals during this time frame with 
ridging building back into the region.
