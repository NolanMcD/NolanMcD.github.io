.SHORT TERM...
(Today through Saturday)
Issued at 341 AM EST Fri Nov 6 2026

The drying trend continues on guidance with little or no rain 
activity anticipated for the start of the weekend. Models show a 
weak frontal boundary over the Se CONUS slowly migrating southward 
and into the northern portions of the peninsula. Consensus keeps the 
parent trough/low complex and its associated dynamic support well 
north of SoFlo during the next couple of days. 

Overall instability parameters remain highly suppressed across the 
region, even with the aforementioned front moving into northern 
Florida. So far, the forecast scenario brings this feature into our 
area as a dry FROPA, with main potential impacts being cooler temps 
in its wake as flow becomes northerly. Ensembles and NBM Wx/POPs 
coverage remains at 10% or less each day, with mainly warm and 
mostly sunny to partly cloudy conditions prevailing. Definitely, a 
welcomed change from the flooding issues experienced on previous 
days.  

With better daytime heating and decreased cloud cover, expect 
afternoon highs to reach the mid to upper 80s each day.

.LONG TERM...
(Saturday night through Thursday)
Issued at 341 AM EST Fri Nov 6 2026

The dry weather pattern remains in guidance on Sunday as high 
pressure briefly descends upon the area in the wake of a 
relatively dry FROPA. Some lingering moisture may be enough for a 
few coastal showers to develop in the afternoon hours, but so far 
NBM keeps max POPs around 10-15% right along the east coast. 

For early next week, high pressure will have broad control of the 
region, keeping low POPs in place and winds veering from N/NW to a 
more NE flow by Tuesday. Meanwhile, global models show a short wave 
trough feature developing over the SE CONUS, and gradually pushing 
into northern Florida around the late Tuesday-early Wednesday 
timeframe. The timing of this feature will continue to be adjusted. 
In the mean time, will keep following NBM's scenario, having this 
feature bringing a modest air mass modification for Wednesday 
afternoon and Thursday. Low-lvl moisture intrusion from the Atlantic 
may be enough to push POPs into the 20-30% range. This scenario will 
also be adjusted as new guidance becomes available.   

The latest trend in guidance is also to bring a more pronounced 
temperature gradient as overall northerly flow develops in the 
wake of the FROPA. Afternoon highs should remain in the upper 70s 
to low 80s by early next week.
