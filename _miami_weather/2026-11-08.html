.SHORT TERM...
(Today through Monday)
Issued at 201 AM EST Sun Nov 8 2026

While mid level zonal flow will continue across the region today, 
weak mid level ridging will gradually start to move over the area
from the Gulf as Monday progresses. At the surface, a weakening 
frontal boundary will eventually stall out just to the south of 
the region today before gradually washing out tonight into 
Monday. At the same time, high pressure centered to the northwest 
will gradually build into the region behind the front today 
through Monday. With plenty of subsidence occurring behind the 
frontal boundary throughout the atmospheric column, this will 
allow for PWATs to lower as they will generally range between 1.1 
and 1.3 inches across most areas today into Monday. The only 
exception to this will be over the extreme southern area of the 
peninsula which will be closer to the front where PWAT values 
will be slightly higher. The drier air mass will help to keep
mostly dry conditions in place through the rest of the weekend 
and into Monday, with the only exception being a very low chance 
of a passing shower or two over the extreme southern areas closer 
to the front. Any shower that would develop during this time frame
would be low topped and short lived. High temperatures today and 
Monday will generally rise into the lower to mid 80s, however, 
some locations south of Alligator Alley could rise into the upper 
80s. 

&&

.LONG TERM...
(Monday night through Saturday)
Issued at 201 AM EST Sun Nov 8 2026

Weak mid level ridging remains in place across the region heading 
into Tuesday while surface high pressure to the north gradually 
shifts eastward. Heading into the middle of the week, the mid level 
ridging will gradually start to give way to a mid level trough as a 
shortwave impulse passes off to the north of the region. At the 
surface, high pressure will gradually weaken over the area on 
Wednesday as it pulls away to the northeast. While most of Tuesday 
will remain on the drier side, some moisture advection will take
place on Wednesday as the mid level wind flow becomes more
southwesterly. This additional moisture moving into the region 
may be enough to introduce a low end chance of showers during the 
afternoon when instability will be maximized due to peak diurnal 
heating. High temperatures both on Tuesday and Wednesday will 
generally rise into the lower to mid 80s across most areas. Some 
locations south of Alligator Alley in Southwest Florida may see 
highs in the upper 80s each day. 

Moving into the second portion of the week, the weather pattern 
across the region will change as mid level troughing will rapidly 
amplify over the Eastern Seaboard during this time frame. At the 
surface, a developing area of low pressure will push eastward from 
the Midwest into the Mid-Atlantic states and then eventually off the 
Northeastern coastline during this time frame. The frontal boundary 
associated with this system will gradually approach and move through 
South Florida during the end of the week timeframe. Uncertainty 
still remains rather high in this part of the forecast as the 
global and ensemble guidance suite is still in disagreement in 
regards to the timing of this frontal passage across the region. 
The ECMWF suite continues to show a more progressive and faster 
frontal passage while the GFS solutions do show a slower passage 
through the area. While the exact details still need to be worked 
out over the next several days, it does look like chances of 
showers will increase heading into the Thursday and Friday 
timeframe across South Florida as the front pushes through. While 
thunderstorm chances look to be on the lower end at this point due
to the best dynamics and instability remaining off to the north 
of the region, this will need to continue to be monitored to make
sure these trends continue. 

Behind the front, strong high pressure will build back into the 
region from the northwest causing winds to shift and increase 
rapidly out of the west northwest as cold air advection takes
over. While uncertainty may remain high in regards to the timing
of the frontal passage during the end of the week, trends are 
pointing towards below normal temperatures and much drier 
conditions heading into next weekend. This will continue to be 
monitored as the week progresses.
