.SHORT TERM...
(Today through Sunday)
Issued at 307 AM EDT Sat Oct 24 2026

South Florida finds itself at the base of a positively tilted mid-level
longwave trough. Water vapor imagery and upper air sounding 
observations from around the state indicate that deep layer dry 
air is currently in place across most of Florida, keeping 
conditions more dry and stable through the region. As the main 
trough axis swings across the state through tomorrow, unsettled 
weather will remain across the more unstable Gulf Stream waters. 
A few isolated showers may hug the Atlantic coastline during the
day, but most activity will remain offshore. Deeper moisture 
remains further southeast over the Bahamas and closer to a 
stalled boundary near eastern Cuba, where a complex of 
thunderstorms is currently ongoing. However, here in South 
Florida, model soundings seem to indicate that moisture content 
will actually trend drier on Saturday. Precipitable water values 
will drop from a near average 1.6 inches, to a bottom tenth 
percentile of 0.8 to 0.9 inches. Models hint at the drier air 
reaching the surface this time, with dewpoints dropping into the 
lower 60s through the middle of the day. As a result, PoPs will be
very low across most of South Florida, less than 10% for most 
areas. In addition to the dry air, subsidence aloft should keep 
any showers from being able to mature or develop. Coastal 
convergence may be possible, but there is very low confidence for
rain amounts to be anything greater than half an inch through 
tomorrow afternoon.

Forecast confidence for the Saturday night through Sunday period has 
quite honestly decreased. Ensemble and deterministic guidance 
continues to struggle with a solution for the shortwave feature 
coming off of the Carolina coast. There is less agreement among 
guidance as to weather this feature develops into a closed mid-level 
low as it tracks southward along the Florida coastline, or 
whether it stays an open wave and join the overall longwave 
troughing pattern. Solutions also differ as to how fast and how 
far east and south this feature will move over the next two days. 
This has major implications on the amount of rainfall South 
Florida may get. A deeper, slower, and more westerly tracking 
system would increase chances of precipitation areawide. However, 
most model guidance has continued to trend lower with 
precipitation amounts. WPC QPF ensemble clusters show that 1 to 
1.5 inches through Monday would be the highest confidence scenario
to play out. Yet, there is one cluster of solutions that shows a 
scenario of 3 to 4 inches possible (but this is a lower 
confidence scenario). Overall, better dynamics aloft and increased
streams of vorticity across the region will favor greater shower 
and storm activity. PoPs will climb into the 70% to 80% range over
the eastern metro. Coastal convergence may allow for several 
storms to focus along the coastline for several hours, so a Slight
Risk of excessive rainfall exits for minor urban flooding along 
low lying and poor drainage locations. 

Breezy northeasterly winds will continue to strengthen through the 
weekend for all areas. Breezy winds may combine with high surf and 
unusually high tides over the weekend. This may result in 
moderate to potentially major tidal flooding for coastal areas 
along Dade, Broward, and Palm Beach counties. Remember to never 
drive, walk, or wade across flooded roads.

.LONG TERM...
(Sunday night through Friday)
Issued at 307 AM EDT Sat Oct 24 2026

By the time Sunday night rolls around, strong surface high pressure 
over the mid-Atlantic states and deepening surface troughing over 
the northern Caribbean will create a tightening pressure gradient 
across the Florida coastline. As a result, winds will continue to 
strengthen into Monday and Tuesday. Sustained winds over the waters 
will already be howling at 30 mph starting on Sunday, but the 
strongest overall winds inland look to be moreso on Monday. Some 
areas will be flirting with Wind Advisory criteria, with gusts of 
35 to 40 mph possible. There is pretty high confidence that winds 
will at least reach 25 mph for many locations, but probabilities 
for winds greater than 30 mph area only around 30%. Nevertheless, 
these conditions have the potential to break weak tree branches 
and blow around loose objects and debris.

Strong winds and high surf will combine with the highest King Tides 
of the year early next week. This will result in the potential for 
moderate and major tidal flooding. This tidal flooding may also be 
exacerbated by coastal showers and rainfall, which could lead to 
significant water rises along the far eastern metro areas. While the 
peak tides will be during the beginning of the week, moderate coastal 
flooding may persist through the end of the week as we come out of 
this full moon phase.

Deeper moisture, with increased PWATs of around 1.6 to 1.8 inches, 
will persist over South Florida through at least Tuesday. This will 
keep the potential of scattered showers and storms moving along the 
eastern half of South Florida. A few localized stronger storms and 
coastal convergence may create some minor urban flooding concerns. 
However, the threat of rainfall will generally be on the downtrend 
after Monday as the mid-level troughing pattern finally ejects 
out into the Atlantic. Ridging from the Gulf and drier air from 
the north will begin to exert more influence over Florida for the 
middle to end of the week. This will likely result in decreasing 
coverage of showers and storms areawide. But with persistent 
northeast flow continuing, pesky onshore showers from the Gulf 
Stream will continue to be a nuisance each day through the end of
the week.
