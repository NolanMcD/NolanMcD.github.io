.MESOSCALE UPDATE FOR HEAVY RAINFALL... 
Issued at 1202 PM EST Mon Nov 2 2026

The aforementioned shortwave across the eastern Gulf continues to
enhance upper level divergence across South Florida early this 
afternoon, enhancing low level convergence and facilitating a 
gradual spatial expansion of shower and thunderstorm activity 
across the region. 17z Mesoanalysis in tandem with GOES east 
imagery indicates a corridor of enhanced surface heating across 
the southeastern half of the Florida Peninsula, resulting in the 
development of a differential heating boundary across the 
peninsula with the influence of sprawling cold pools, 1) across 
southwestern Florida and the 2) second more pronounced cold pool 
just north of Lake Okeechobee and across the majority of the 
Treasure Coast. Across southeastern Florida, diurnal heating has 
resulted in temperatures in the low to mid 80s with a lull in 
activity across the majority of metro Miami-Dade and Broward 
counties. SBCAPE and MUCAPE analysis indicates enough instability 
remains in the low level boundary layer for additional convection
along and to the south of mesoscale boundaries with a foci in 
near-term activity across Palm Beach County and the Lake 
Okeechobee region. While 500mb flow is out of a more southerly 
component today, as opposed to the inverse 1km (easterly)/6km 
(westerly) orientation that led to efficient coastal convergence 
(and a high- end maxima of 15+" of rainfall across Palm Beach 
County) yesterday, slow movement of showers and thunderstorms and
upstream growth (backbuilding) could re- aggravate urban flooding
across areas that remain completely saturated/inundated from a 
soil moisture perspective. A Flood Watch remains in effect for all
of South Florida through 7PM this evening with a close eye being 
kept on the latest trends with radar coverage and rainfall totals 
as the afternoon progresses for any need of watch extension.

Recent ACARS soundings from FLL/MIA depict some residual CIN and 
drier air in the vertical column that may act to suppress more
robust convection across metro Miami-Dade and Broward in the near
term. This residual inhibition and drier air in the column may be
largely responsible for the break in activity that has been 
observed for much of today across most east coast locales. 
Although that trend has recently come to a close across northern 
and central Palm Beach County where convection continues to back- 
build along locales that certainly don't need any additional 
precipitation. Given the forcing aloft and the pocket of residual 
instability across these areas that have received ample surface 
heating, additional showers and thunderstorms could still 
materialize across the eastern half of South Florida for the 
remainder of today. The 12z HREF LPMM has a widespread 1-2" across

.SYNOPSIS...
Issued at 327 AM EST Mon Nov 2 2026

Water vapor imagery and model analysis fields show an elongated
shortwave extending from the southeast Gulf, through the Southeast
and into the Mid-Atlantic where it connects in with the northern
stream flow. There's very little surface reflection to this upper
low, with the latest WPC surface analysis depicting high pressure
centered over the Mid-Atlantic/NW Atlantic, with its western
reaches covering much of the eastern CONUS. Locally, we remain
under easterly flow at the base of the ridge with a coastal trough
still established down the east coast of Florida. Additionally, an
MCV is likely still present across Central Florida as a result of
yesterday's storms.

.SHORT TERM...
(Today through Tuesday)
Issued at 327 AM EST Mon Nov 2 2026

We enter another very low predictability period today with the
environment primed to produce more heavy rainfall should a surface
focus emerge. CAMs have struggled to identify the ongoing precip
pattern and thus low confidence exists regarding their future
predictions. Some of the CAMs that at least more closely resemble
the ongoing pattern would suggest banding features along the east
coast where upslope flow coincides with mesoscale boundaries
through mid-afternoon. They also depict convection in the SE Gulf,
associated with the upper low, moving across south Florida through
the afternoon and evening. As the storms from the Gulf reach the
east coast, a period of slow moving storms producing heavy
rainfall will be possible. Forecast rain amounts look very 
similar to yesterday, with common amounts of 1 to 3 inches where 
showers and storms occur. Scattered locations around south Florida
may see closer to 3 to 5 inches of rain. The more isolated high 
end amounts have the potential to peak around 6 to 9 inches. These
amounts could accumulate rather quickly, on the order of 2 to 4 
hours. The Flood Watch will continue through today and has been
expanded over all of South Florida due to low confidence in where
the heaviest rain will setup.

As the base of the elongated trough cuts off over the SE Gulf
today, it will be slow to move out of the region and likely have
to wait for an approaching northern stream frontal system to nudge
it through south Florida. This will likely mean a continued wet
pattern through Tuesday. However, the good news is that dry mid-
level air, associated with the part of the shortwave that will
advect into the Atlantic, may result in lower PWATs and less
forcing. This means an overall lower top-end to expected rain
amounts. Expect common rainfall amounts on Tuesday closer to 1-2"
with localized higher amounts in the 2-4" range. Not nothing, but
better than Sunday and Today. It also look as though most of the
rainfall on Tuesday will be confined to the east coast.

.LONG TERM...
(Tuesday night through Sunday)
Issued at 327 AM EST Mon Nov 2 2026

With the aforementioned mid-week frontal system, models are
struggling with whether the shortwave cuts off and loses latitude
from the northern stream. In solutions where it cuts off the
shortwave it has a much slower progression across the CONUS. This
will ultimately influence when/if we get frontal passage across
south Florida late this week. Regardless, models tend to keep the
frontal passage pretty dry, this far south, and also have very
little signal for any anomalous sensible weather fields in the
extended range. 

The slow nudge of the frontal system will displace the ridge axis
further north. This will remove the direct easterly component and
calm winds a bit. Both of these results mean that we'll probably
have less precipitation across south Florida from mid-week onward.
