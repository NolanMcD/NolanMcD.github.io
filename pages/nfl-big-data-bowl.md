  <section class="blanket-section blanket-intro" aria-labelledby="blanket-meaning">
    <div><p class="blanket-eyebrow">A stat built around staying close</p><h2 id="blanket-meaning">What counts as a Blanket?</h2></div>
    <div><p>For this first version, I average the distance between a cornerback and the same receiver <strong>from the snap through the throw</strong>. A time-weighted average below one yard earns one Blanket. Exactly one yard does not.</p><p>A receiver can briefly get more than a yard away and still be blanketed on average. Being close only when the ball is thrown does not guarantee a Blanket.</p><p><strong>Blanket rate</strong> is Blankets divided by eligible, measured man-coverage assignments. Missing tracking is excluded from the denominator; it never counts as a failed rep.</p></div>
  </section>

  <section class="blanket-section" aria-labelledby="blanket-combine-title">
    <p class="blanket-eyebrow">Combine → coverage</p><h2 id="blanket-combine-title">Do drill features help on unseen cornerbacks?</h2>
    <p><strong>There is no convincing predictive improvement yet.</strong> {{ b.players_min_ten_reps }} cornerbacks have at least ten screened pairings. I hold out one CB at a time and compare a context baseline with the same model plus one drill feature. The outcome is player-average separation; {{ b.provisional_blankets }} positive Blanket reps are too few for a reliable Blanket-rate model.</p>
    <div class="blanket-two-up"><article><h3>Brake. Turn. Accelerate.</h3><p>The prototype finds the largest moving heading change within each attempt, then measures speed through that window, braking into it, and reacceleration afterward. Medians across attempts give one set of features per player and drill. These are operational windows, not coach-annotated phases.</p></article><article><h3>Hold out the whole player.</h3><p>The baseline uses starting cushion, forty time, height, and weight. A fixed ridge penalty limits model complexity. Missing-value imputation and scaling use training players only. Each held-out CB has equal weight, rather than letting high-volume players dominate.</p></article></div>

  <section class="blanket-section" id="methodology" aria-labelledby="blanket-method-title">
    <p class="blanket-eyebrow">Methodology · version {{ b.analysis_version }}</p><h2 id="blanket-method-title">How the first pass works</h2>
    <ol class="blanket-method"><li><strong>Choose the cohort.</strong> Join combine and NFL players by ID and draft year. Keep CBs without requiring a three-cone time, then regular-season passing plays with CB/SLOT_CB alignment, team MAN_COVERAGE, player assignment MAN, and no nullified penalty.</li><li><strong>Infer a receiver at the snap.</strong> Choose the nearest tracked offensive route runner within 5 yards, with a 1-yard margin to the next observed candidate when there is one. Freeze that identity for the rep. Reject receivers paired to multiple measured CBs.</li><li><strong>Check the window.</strong> Require unique snap/throw events, at least one second of coverage, both endpoints, at least 90% of expected 10 Hz samples, finite positions, and no synchronized gap above 0.2 seconds.</li><li><strong>Measure separation.</strong> Integrate Euclidean distance between simultaneous x/y positions using the trapezoidal rule, then divide by elapsed time. Coordinates are treated as yards under the NFL tracking convention; the final study must confirm units against the 2027 field dictionary.</li><li><strong>Count and compare.</strong> A time average strictly below 1 yard is a provisional Blanket. Model continuous player-average separation with leave-one-CB-out validation, one drill feature at a time, and a fixed ridge penalty of 1. Resample held-out-player error differences 2,000 times.</li></ol>
    <details><summary>Expanded sensor-feature definitions</summary><p>Smooth speed with a centered three-frame median and direction with a three-frame circular mean. Find the largest 0.4-second heading change while moving at speed ≥1, leaving 0.6 seconds of observed data on each side. Turn speed is the minimum smoothed speed within ±0.2 seconds. Braking is speed lost from the earlier speed maximum to that minimum, divided by elapsed time. Reacceleration is the corresponding rise to the later maximum. Both flanks end 0.6 seconds from the selected center. Aggregate attempts by median. Combine speed and acceleration retain source units until the field dictionary is confirmed; standardized model comparisons do not require a speed-unit conversion.</p></details>
    <p class="blanket-caption">Assignments remain unverified with film. The 5-yard filter favors tighter starting alignments. Results describe this screened sample, not all NFL coverage or all draft prospects. No player rankings are published.</p>
