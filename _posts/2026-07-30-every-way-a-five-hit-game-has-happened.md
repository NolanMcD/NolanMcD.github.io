Five hits in one game is already rare. But five hits can create wildly different lines: five singles, four home runs and a double, or dozens of combinations in between.

Using Stathead, I broke down **exactly five-hit games** by the type of each hit and the resulting total bases. The research returned **2,598 games**, covering 37 of the 56 possible combinations.

<p class="data-note"><strong>Scope:</strong> Exactly five-hit games only. Research and counts via Stathead.</p>

## The five-doubles mystery

This is the result I cannot get over. Every step toward a five-double game has happened—and the frequency falls in an almost perfect staircase—until the final step disappears.

<figure class="double-staircase" aria-label="Five-hit games by number of doubles, from 594 games with one double to zero games with five doubles">

Five doubles would be a ten-total-base game. It sounds much more attainable than the most explosive lines in the dataset: three games have paired a single with four home runs, and two have paired a double with four home runs. Yet the all-doubles version has never appeared.

Most of the missing combinations are built around triples, which makes sense. But five doubles sits apart. It requires no triples and no home runs—just the same extra-base hit five times. Baseball has produced the louder five-hit games. Somehow, it is still waiting on this one.
